# M3c.3 — Famine relief and better storage, and review fixes for M3c.2

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3c, slice 3. Status: verified (6 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run; the review findings below are fixed in M3c.4.

**Observable outcome:**
- **A realm feeds its hungry regions from the rest.** When a region goes hungry, its realm sends stored food from its regions with food to spare, nearest first, overland through its own land. Roads shorten the way.
  - The realm pays for the carriage, and food carried far spoils on the way.
  - The chronicle tells when relief begins, and the region inspector shows relief food arriving.
  - A rich realm carries its people through a drought a poor one cannot.
- **Better storage:**
  - **Silos** (Architecture, Classical) keep a region's grain longer and better.
  - **Refrigeration** (Industrial) keeps food far longer, in store and on the way.
- **Taxes are bounded by how calm a realm's people are** (from the M3c.2 review). A realm raises taxes above the custom only as far as its people bear it; a restless realm accepts arrears instead.

**Relevant spec sections:**
- VISION.md "Population": famine is mitigable by wealth and knowledge (user, M3b review);
- VISION.md "Wealth": taxes, and the choice between heavy taxes and arrears;
- milestone M3c and its health check "famine kills a smaller share of people in rich realms than in poor ones".

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Exact accounting:**
  - food carried is a flow: carried out, spoiled on the way, carried in;
  - carriage is a wealth flow (`relief`);
  - the invariants close both every month.
- **Relief rules** (tests):
  - the nearest donor first;
  - a donor keeps its needs until its next harvest and its reserve;
  - a poor treasury buys less relief;
  - roads cheapen carriage and refrigeration keeps more of the food;
  - a people beyond what its land feeds is not kept there by relief;
  - a new relief episode is an event.
- **Health check** (five-seed study): famine deaths per person over their lives are lower in the richest third of civilizations by output a year than in the poorest third.
- **Gates:** M1–M3, M3b and M3c gates hold, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Relief** (`relief.ts`; `RELIEF_TUNING`), each month in the production system, after a civilization's people have eaten:
  - **Hungry regions:** food security below 1 with less than a month of food in store, and no more people than the land lastingly feeds. Beyond that, hunger follows the land and food from elsewhere cannot keep them there.
  - **What they want:** enough in store for 2 months of need, the hungriest first.
  - **Donors:** the realm's other regions that are not hungry themselves. Each keeps what it needs until its next harvest plus its reserve (`POPULATION_TUNING.reserveMonths`). The nearest give first, along the realm's own land (overland only; sea carriage waits for trade, M5).
  - **Carriage:**
    - costs 0.2 wealth per person-month of food per 1,000 travel-km; roads shorten the travel-km;
    - travels 500 km a month and spoils on the way at the realm's monthly store spoilage;
    - the treasury pays at most a fifth of itself a month;
    - less than a person-month still wanted is not carried.
  - **Events:** relief reaching regions that had none the month before begins an episode (event `famineRelief`, with the regions, people and donors). Relief continuing month after month is the same episode.
- **Storage:**
  - **Silo:** a food building of stone, 12,000 to build over 24 months, upkeep 240, in a town or larger, one per region. It doubles the store's limit and spoils 0.6 as much. With a granary in the same settlement the two multiply.
  - **Refrigeration** (Industrial; Chemistry and Steam power): spoilage × 0.3 and store limit × 1.5, in store and on the way.
- **Health check** (`famineByWealth`, the study's relief table):
  - each civilization counts its famine deaths, the months its people lived and their output before tax while a civilization;
  - civilizations of at least a century are ranked by output a year: a realm's riches, what it can spend on relief. Output per person is nearly the same everywhere (0.36–0.47 a year in Chronicle at 2,000), so it does not tell rich realms from poor;
  - the richest and poorest thirds' famine deaths a year per 1,000 people are compared.
- **Review fixes for M3c.2:**
  - **Taxes only yearly:** a union re-judges the larger realm's regions (measured from its capital again) but sets no taxes. Taxes are set only at the yearly assessment.
  - **Rates** are kept to a millionth, so a step of 2 points reaches 30% exactly; the heavy-tax and easing events fire at their stated rates.
  - **Calm bounds taxes** (VISION.md: a realm chooses between the unrest of heavy taxes and the decay of arrears): the most a realm will levy is customary rate + (50% − customary) × clamp((calm − 0.4) ÷ (0.85 − 0.4), 0, 1). Calm is its regions' people-weighted mean stability apart from its taxes and arrears.
  - **Administration per person** is reported beside total costs per person.
  - **Negative invariant tests** cover the tax bounds, the remoteness owner and the new cost flows.
  - **Dead code:** the branch for regions without a living settlement is gone; an invariant guarantees every held region has one.
  - **A note at the remoteness invariant:** war and fracture (M6–M7) must re-measure the regions they move.
- **Contract:** protocol 19 (event `famineRelief`; the region's weather says whether relief is arriving). **Rules:** 17.

- **Famine is mitigable, by wealth:**
  - famine kills a smaller share of people in the richest third of civilizations than in the poorest third in every seed and every century, by up to 16 times (Chronicle at 3,000);
  - famine deaths by 3,000 fall to a quarter to a half of M3c.2's;
  - famines fall to about a third.
  - **The measure:** a first Chronicle run ranked realms by output per person and found the rich dying more at 2,000. A probe showed output per person is nearly the same everywhere, while the large realms with big treasuries died least and small realms with tiny treasuries most. Ranking by output a year (what a realm can spend) measures riches; see Decisions.
  - **Caveat (review):** output a year follows population, so the richest third are also the largest realms. They have more donor regions outside a drought as well as deeper treasuries. Nothing in this study yet shows a treasury limiting relief; M3c.4 counts relief wanted but not bought, to show it.
- **Gates hold in every seed:**
  - **Borders:** the ratio at 1,000 is 1.073–1.251.
  - **M3:** 20–30 civilizations at 1,000.
  - **M3b wonders:** 3–7 by 2,500.
  - **M3b roads:** coverage 100% at 1,500. Bridges came within 1–3 years of Engineering in the three seeds that reached it (Chronicle and Elsewhere did not by 3,000, as Elsewhere did not in M3c.1).
  - **M1:** silent band-years 0, and the longest stay above 1.1× capacity is 3–10 months.
  - **M2:** growth after Agriculture ×4.1–5.5, and Agriculture invented independently 32–36 times by 1,000.
  - **M3b settlements and fields:** settlements by water or a site 88–93%, and fields regrew after famines in every seed (306–565 times).
  - **M3c.1 tiers:** at most 5 tier changes of one settlement within any 100 years, and 0–3 settlements change more than 3 times.
  - **M3c.2 budget:**
    - treasuries 1.6–2.0 years of costs;
    - a third of settlements and half of regions at a loss;
    - the largest, oldest realms cost about twice as much per person as small young ones, and their administration per person 2.3–3.7 times as much;
    - 0–4 realms in arrears at a time;
    - the largest share at most 33% (Atlas).
- **Speed:** 91–143 ms per simulated year.

## Review (independent agent, 6 October 2026)

- **No accounting, determinism, hash or architecture bug.** Food and wealth flows close, stores and treasuries cannot go negative, and the focused tests pass.
- **Findings, fixed in M3c.4:**
  1. **Medium: episodes split.** A region topped up to two months is not hungry the next month and hungry again the month after, so one drought splits into many relief "episodes" (about 24,000–30,000 against 3,000–4,000 famines) and the inspector's "relief food arriving" flickers. Relief within a year of the last should be the same episode.
  2. **Medium: calm.** It is not stability apart from taxes and arrears: it adds today's burdens back onto last year's clamped stability. It reads too calm where stability clamped at 0, and is biased while arrears change. It should be computed from stability's parts without taxes and arrears.
  3. **Medium: the health check confounds riches with size** (see the caveat above). Count relief wanted but not bought for lack of treasury.
  4. **Low:** relief landing after the month's meal erases that month's hunger (one month per episode), and arrival is instant whatever the distance.
  5. **Low:** `relieved` is not cleared when a region's people leave or die out.
  6. **Low:** "the hungriest first" sorts by food wanted, not by how short people are.
  7. **Low:** relief reads the cached capacity, which the population system may refresh later in the month.
  8. **Low:** ARCHITECTURE's contract bullet lacks relief.
  9. **Low:** there is no negative invariant test for the relief flow, and Refrigeration's store limit is untested.

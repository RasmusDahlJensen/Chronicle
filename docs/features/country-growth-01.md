## Contracts and rules

New monthly games use protocol 5 / rules 4. Earlier protocol 1–4 saves retain their rules on observation/stepping. Explicit reset upgrades monthly saves at the original site and history seed to the new day-zero country. No terrain generator/protocol change. Existing `settlements` keeps the capital's population, food, working cells and food ledger; its local territory remains its inhabited capital cell. A separate `country` record owns `territory` (countryId, capitalCellId, sorted cells), claim counters, demographic fractions/totals, person-days, upkeep/spoilage totals, unsupported duration and current economic metrics. Population mirrors are validated; nothing is fabricated for display. Country AI profile/RNG/intent selection is reused, with claim/consolidate choices in this rules version.

Starting claims: capital plus compact adjacent land, targeting about 4,000 km², rounded to available whole cells. Use physical distance and land connectivity; bound initial allocation. Ocean/lakes cannot be claimed or used as bridges. Mountains/snow may be owned but supply little/no food and cost more to support. Sparse known grain/game and adjacent fish affect subsistence; unknown minerals do not. Initial claims and 7,500 food person-days are explicit starting endowments.

Food production uses only owned reachable cells, available gatherers, terrain/known foods and travel costs. Productive sites have area-scaled worker capacity; a claim alone produces no food. Sixty percent of population is an abstract labor budget, reduced by territorial support and active expansion crews. This first aggregate demographic model has no age cohorts; labor share is an explicit approximation. Food is consumed per person per day, territory has terrain/distance/area-priced daily upkeep, and stocks above a reserve buffer spoil gradually. All spending/spoilage/shortfalls have cumulative ledgers.

Demography: baseline annual birth/death rates 4%/2%, using daily integer fractions over 360 days. Births require adequate reserves and current food/support coverage. Hunger adds one death per 30 cumulative unfed person-days, never more deaths than living people. Births, natural deaths, starvation deaths and fractional carry are saved. Zero population is explicit collapse: no active territory or further births/production. Rates are provisional game balance, not calibrated historical demographics. Person-days replace the old fixed-250 consumption assertion for this rules version; old ledgers remain strict.

Claims expand one adjacent unowned cell at a time via canonical ownership operations. Score frontier opportunities by food opportunity, compactness, terrain/travel costs and existing seeded expansion preferences. An undertaking reserves a small crew (12 people) and its priced food cost, performs dated work, then pays/claims exactly once. These people are assigned, not consumed. Continuing territorial support must fit a bounded share of available labor and predicted supplies; crisis expansion is allowed only for affordable, materially better food access. Normal expansion respects food reserve preferences and requires forecast ongoing food coverage. Continuing projects budget remaining work against a seven-day safety reserve; expected spending and minor population changes do not restart already funded work. No remote/foreign stores or unknown ore can fund a claim. Loss of feasibility cancels work without consuming reserved people/food. Persistent unsupported claims, including net food deficits before reserves are exhausted, trigger gradual release of removable outer cells after 30 days, preserving capital connectivity; the capital is lost only on population collapse. Preventive retreat forecasts food improvement, avoids removing a productive cell that deepens the shortage, and waits for funded claim work and its completion day. Actual food/support/labor emergencies retain a retreat fallback. A country can hold unworked land if it pays support.

No provinces, extraction chains, research, migration, disease, war, diplomacy or village founding in this slice. No claim of realistic population history or large multi-country throughput. Preserve earlier saved histories rather than reinterpret their accounting.

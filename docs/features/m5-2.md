# M5.2 — Effective values, needs and causes

**Feature / status:** the second slice of M5 (the plan is in the [M5.1 brief](m5-1.md)). Status: in progress.

**Why:** VISION.md's decision model weighs three layers of values (culture, ruler, government) and a set of needs, and records why each step chose what it did. M3 built the step with a few needs. Until now it followed the values of the realm's ruling culture alone. VISION.md says later milestones extend that step, never replace it.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Effective values.** A civilization decides by the values of all its people (their groups' values, people-weighted), not its ruling culture's alone. A realm of many peoples is pulled by them all.
  - Ruler and government layers exist and are neutral until M7 (VISION.md: "until M7 every civ has a neutral ruler and the default government for its stage").
- **All needs, each 0 to 1,** computed each step from what the civilization knows (VISION.md "Needs"):
  - **hunger** and **land pressure** (as before);
  - **resource shortage** (0 until M5.3 brings supply and demand);
  - **threat:** a neighbour it believes stronger and does not like;
  - **unrest:** the share of its regions in unrest and its low stability;
  - **ideological friction:** neighbours of rival faith, by its Zeal and less in the secular age (government differences come with M7);
  - **opportunity:** clearly better land, or a neighbour it believes weaker or richer;
  - **grievance** (0 until M6 brings claims);
  - **infrastructure:** towns its roads do not reach, hungry places without a granary, a coast without a harbor once it could sail.
- **Threat moves it.** A small realm threatened by a stronger, unfriendly neighbour is drawn toward joining a larger realm it likes. Pacts and alliances answer threat from M5.5.
- **Causes.** Every decision log entry stores the effective values and the needs it weighed. Every chosen step names at least two causes whenever two or more of its factors are nonzero: the strongest that drove it and, when only one did, the strongest that held it back.
- **Lab:** the inspector's decision shows the needs and the effective values it was taken with, and its causes.

- **Browser:** the decision in the inspector shows its needs and effective values.

## Decisions and defaults

- **Effective values** (`values.ts`) are the people-weighted mean of a realm's groups' values. Ruler and government layers are neutral functions that M7 fills.
  - **Who uses them:** the decision step (every action's scoring), the realm's taxes (its Tradition), a tribe's choice to join a civilization (its own people, not its culture's mean across all peoples), and the Zeal and Expansionism in its opinions.
  - **Who does not:** how alike two cultures are stays a matter of culture. Research, faith and culture dynamics follow the peoples themselves as before: they are not the decision step, and their calibration rests on them.
- **Needs**, each 0–1 (`NEED_TUNING`), use what the civilization believes of the civilizations it has met whose land touches its own:
  - **Threat:** clamp01((believed strength ÷ its own − 1) ÷ (2 − 1)) × clamp01(−opinion ÷ 50). It needs dislike: an indifferent or friendly neighbour is no threat, however strong. The review found that the first form (hostility pivot 0.5) made threat almost universal (above 0 in 65% of steps).
  - **Unrest:** the larger of the share of its regions in unrest and how far its stability is below 0.8.
  - **Friction:** the share of its neighbours of a rival organized faith (half for a tolerant faith on either side) × Zeal × (1 − secularity).
  - **Opportunity:** the larger of better land (best candidate ÷ its own − 1) and a neighbour believed weaker (× 0.5) or richer (× 0.3).
  - **Infrastructure:** the largest of the share of its towns and cities in regions its roads do not reach; the share of its settlements going hungry (hardship ≥ 0.2) without a granary or silo; and 1 for a coast with somewhere to sail but no harbor once it could build one.
  - **Not yet:** shortage (M5.3) and grievance (M6) are 0.
- **What the needs move now:**
  - hunger, land pressure and land's opportunity drive expansion; unrest and hunger draw unions (as before);
  - threat draws a threatened realm toward joining a larger realm it likes: 0.15 × the most it fears any *other* neighbour × clamp01(opinion of the one it would join ÷ 100);
  - the rest are shown and logged, and later slices' actions answer them.
  - The review found that the first form applied the realm's whole threat to every larger neighbour, the threatening one included. About 30 of 46 threat-driven unions in one seed were into the very realm it feared.
- **Causes:**
  - **What is logged:** every step logs the chosen option's causes: its positive factors, strongest first (at least two when there are two). When only one factor drove it, the strongest that held it back is added (a negative weight; the lab says "despite"). When nothing positive drove it, the two that weighed most are logged.
  - **The count:** the study counts the steps that fall short (`causesShort`; the rule asks for none).
  - **Events:** chronicle events keep citing the positive drivers as before.
- **The log** keeps each step's effective values and needs; the lab shows them with the causes.
- **Contract:** protocol 27 (`NEED_NAMES`, and the last decision's `values`, `needs` and `causes`). Rules version 25.

## Review

The independent review found no breach of the query layer, no NaN or out-of-bounds needs in 29,713 logged steps, and no aliasing or save-readiness problem. Its findings, all resolved:
1. **Threat pull.** It drew realms into the very neighbour that threatened them: about 30 of 46 threat-driven unions in one seed. Fixed: threat is per neighbour, and a union weighs the threat from the others, scaled by liking.
2. **Threat without dislike.** Threat came from indifferent and even warm neighbours (hostility pivot 0.5). Fixed: dislike is required.
3. **Ruling-culture values.** Taxes, a tribe's joining and opinion still used the ruling culture's values. Fixed: they use effective values. Research and sharing acceptance stay with culture (recorded above).
4. **Tolerance.** Tolerance did not ease friction. Fixed.
5. **Infrastructure need details.** Fixed: towns counted per unreached region, and a coast needs somewhere to sail.
6. **Wording.** "The needs it weighed" overclaimed, since only some needs move actions yet; the brief, ARCHITECTURE and lab wording are fixed ("despite" for what held a choice back).
7. **Robustness.** `reasonsFor` now gives two causes whatever `factorCount` is, and falls back to the two strongest negatives.
8. **Tests:**
   - effective values and threats are checked on a real history;
   - the browser checks the causes;
   - threat is never drawn from the realm it comes from.

## Results

Developed with Dev 03's routine:
- a two-seed, 1,500-year check (1 min 42 s) showed no drift from M5.1 (civilizations 26 and 31 against 29 and 27; unions, dominance and sharing nearly the same);
- then one full study (`.chronicle/studies/m5-2`, 7.1 minutes).

Findings:
- **The causes rule:** 0 of 122,584–136,691 decision steps per seed logged fewer than two causes when two or more factors were nonzero.
- **Needs** (means over civilizations' last steps, by 3,000):
  - land pressure 0.49–0.52, opportunity 0.25–0.33, threat 0.14–0.30, friction up to 0.14 (Atlas, where faiths are rivals), unrest 0.03–0.07, hunger and infrastructure about 0;
  - shortage and grievance are 0 until M5.3 and M6.
- **Gates hold** (M5.1 in brackets):
  - unions by 3,000: 37/57/47/46/59 (40/49/43/47/57);
  - civilizations at 2,000: 28/28/32/30/29 (27/27/32/32/30);
  - the longest run above 35%: 79/0/0/0/102 years (78/0/0/0/102);
  - hybrids by 2,000: 2/5/5/4/1;
  - splits from a ruling culture by 2,000: 11/18/7/12/20;
  - the first religion founded in 1,599–2,200, followed in 3–16 civilizations within 500 years;
  - decisions citing a belief more than half off: 6–32 per seed.

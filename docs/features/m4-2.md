# M4.2 — Mixed peoples: assimilation, hybrids and traits

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M4 (culture and religion), slice 2. Status: verified, awaiting user review.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Peoples take up their realm's culture** (VISION.md "Assimilation").
  - Over generations, a smaller people of another culture living under a realm takes up its ruling culture once its ways have grown close to the heartland's. Peoples taken in long ago and far provinces drawn back are typical.
  - It goes slower for a traditional people, in a tolerant (open) realm and far from the court.
  - The culture map shows realms' cultures spreading over the peoples they hold, and each change is an event ("In 3 regions of the Kesh, 40,000 people of the Vael have taken up the ways of the Kesh").
  - A culture whose last people take up another dies and stays in history.
- **Large peoples fuse instead of dissolving** (VISION.md "Hybrids"). When two unrelated cultures have each been a large part of one realm for a long time and have grown alike, they become one people. The new hybrid culture has both as parents, weighted by their people; its name joins the larger parent's first syllable to the rest of the other's name; its language mixes theirs and its colour lies between theirs. It is an event, and the inspector shows both parents and the ancestry percentages.
- **Cultures earn traits** (VISION.md "Traits"): Seafarers, River builders, Mountain folk, Desert nomads and Builders, from the life most of their people have led for a century.
  - Traits pull their values a little (Seafarers toward Openness, Mountain folk toward Tradition).
  - Daughters keep them with high probability, the visible thread of cultural memory.
  - Earning one is an event, and the inspector lists them.

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md "Culture and lineage" (traits, assimilation, hybrids, ancestry query), milestone M4.

**Not in this slice:** religion (M4.3–M4.4); Horse lords, Merchants and Warrior tradition (they need war and trade, M5 and M6); government policy on assimilation (M7).

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Tests:**
  - a hybrid's language comes from its parents' sounds, and its name joins the heavier parent's first syllable to the rest of the other's;
  - a small people close to the ruling culture may assimilate (slower with Tradition and distance), while one still apart, or a large one, never does; in time it takes up the ruling culture, with an event;
  - two large unrelated peoples long together fuse into a hybrid (not before the years required), with both parents, ancestry 50/50 and the realm ruled by the hybrid; a culture and its own daughter never fuse;
  - a trait is earned after its years, pulls values, and passes to daughters and hybrids;
  - invariants: traits within bounds, and a realm's second people tracked consistently.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):**
  - M4: at least one hybrid culture by year 2,000 in every seed;
  - assimilation brings down the share of civilizations' people not of their realm's culture;
  - traits are earned in every seed;
  - M1–M4.1 gates hold.
- **Browser and check:** the inspector shows traits and a hybrid's parents, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Whole peoples, within a realm.** A region holds one people (M4.1), so assimilation and fusion act on whole groups, within the realm that rules them. VISION.md's minorities within a region are left out.
  - VISION.md has a minority converting "toward the region's dominant culture" under "the dominant civ's policy". Here the realm's ruling culture is the dominant one; Romanization is the model.
  - Government policy arrives with M7.
- **Assimilation** (`realmPeoples`, once a year per civilization): each group of another culture takes up the ruling culture with a chance of `assimilationRate` 0.01 ×
  - how close its values are to the realm's heartland people's (none beyond `assimilationRange` 0.1);
  - × (1 − ½ its Tradition) × (1 − ½ the heartland's Openness: tolerant realms let peoples be);
  - × (½ + ½ e^(−remoteness)) × how small a share of the realm its culture holds (none at `hybridShare` 0.25 or more, except for kin, below).
  - **Taking up its ways:** the people's values move `assimilationBlend` ½ of the way to the heartland's, so they end within 0.05, inside the split threshold (0.07). A people that has just taken up the culture does not split off again at once, which would cycle (validated).
  - One event per culture and realm a year, citing the mean of what drew its people in (close ways, how readily they change, how hard the realm presses, nearness to the court, how small a people).
  - The no-re-split guarantee holds for the realm whose heartland is the culture's heart. Elsewhere a culture's heart is the heartland of the realm it rules with the most people. In the review's check of the earlier study, no split's largest region had been assimilated into its parent within 20 years.
  - **Calibration** (study and an in-process harness over Chronicle and Atlas to 3,000):
    1. **Values had to come close first** (rate 0.02, range 0.05, no blend): each culture's own random walk kept most peoples just beyond the range, and the share of civilizations' people foreign to their realm only fell from about 60% to 46–55% by 3,000.
    2. **Their ways came along** (rate 0.03, range 0.12, blend ½): realms became nearly single-culture, with 1–4% foreign by 3,000 and living cultures down from about 500 to about 100. That is unlike real multi-ethnic empires, and M7's secession needs distinct peoples.
    3. **Kept** (rate 0.01, range 0.1, blend ½): about half foreign at 1,000, about a third at 2,000 and a fifth to a quarter at 3,000. This is gradual Romanization with minorities that last.
- **Kin:** two cultures are kin when their ancestry from the starting peoples overlaps by at least `kinShare` ½, as sister peoples and a daughter and her forebear are.
  - Kin do not fuse: a hybrid is two peoples.
  - Kin fold into the ruling culture by assimilation whatever their size.
  - The review found that "unrelated" first meant only direct descent, so about half of the hybrids joined sister or cousin cultures of one founding people.
- **Hybrids:** a realm watches one pair: its ruling culture and its largest other people, not kin, while each holds at least `hybridShare` 0.25 of its people (`together`, `togetherRuling`, `togetherYears`).
  - Their years together grow while both are large and fade while either is not, so a dip does not wipe them out (no hard cutoff, VISION.md rule 7).
  - The count starts again only for a new pair, including a new ruling culture.
  - After `hybridYears` 150, if their people's values differ by at most `hybridRange` 0.1, they fuse with a chance of `hybridRate` 0.02 a year.
  - All of both peoples' groups in the realm take the hybrid culture, and the hybrid rules.
  - **Becoming one people brings their ways together:** every group's values move `fusionBlend` 0.8 of the way to the hybrid's. Before this, fusion only relabelled the groups, and 7 of 30 hybrids in the earlier study split again along the old seam within 2–25 years (validated: `hybridRange` × (1 − `fusionBlend`) lies within the split threshold).
  - The parents' counts wait for their next yearly refresh.
- **Hybrid names and languages** (`names.ts`):
  - **Language:** of each kind of sound, the sounds both share, then a mix drawn from each in proportion, as many as the parents have on average.
  - **Name:** the heavier parent's first syllable plus the rest of the other's name (one vowel dropped where two would meet), never a name in use.
  - **Colour:** the hue lies between the parents', weighted.
- **Traits** (`traits.ts` data, `TRAIT_TUNING`):
  - **Earned** when at least 60% of the culture's people have lived in the trait's conditions for 100 years in a row. A culture holds at most 4, and never loses one.
  - **Conditions:**
    - Seafarers: a coast the people sail from;
    - River builders: a great river and at least half their food from fields and herds;
    - Mountain folk: rough land (the regions' `rough` environment flag, which research already uses);
    - Desert nomads: desert (a new `desert` environment flag beside `arid`, which also counts steppe; no tech reads it) and 30% or more of their food from herds and fields, away from any river or lake (desert farmers live by water, and `farmShare` counts fields as well as herds);
    - Builders: a city, a metropolis or a wonder in the region.
  - **First tried:**
    1. Harsh land (≥ 0.7) for Desert nomads, and defensibility for Mountain folk. Harsh land includes cold forests and mountains, so Desert nomads became the most common trait in every seed (172–252 earned).
    2. The `arid` flag, which still counts steppe, gave 130–265; steppe herders are VISION.md's Horse lords, which need a military history (M6).
    3. Traits were also so common that 60–70% of cultures held one, so a trait now needs 60% of a culture's people, not half.
  - **Pulls:** small value pulls of 0.05–0.1 on the targets.
  - **Inherited** by a daughter with chance 0.85 each, and from a hybrid's lighter parent with half that.
- **Trait data is validated at startup:** unique keys, known conditions, at least a year, pulls within ±0.3, at most 8 traits (the contract's limit).
- **Contract:**
  - protocol 23;
  - events `assimilation` and `traitEarned`;
  - the inspector's `people` gains `parents` (with weights) and `traits`.
- **Rules:** 21.

## Review (independent agent, 7 October 2026) and fixes

- **Fixed:**
  1. **Blocking: hybrids split apart again along the old seam** (7 of 30 within 2–25 years). Fusion now brings both peoples' values together (`fusionBlend`). A test fuses two peoples, then runs a century of culture years without a split.
  2. **Medium: kin fused** (about 14 of 30 hybrids joined cultures of one founding people). Kinship is now shared ancestry. Kin do not fuse but assimilate whatever their size. Tested with sister peoples.
  3. **Medium: the pair's clock followed only the partner**, and any dip reset it. The pair now includes the ruling culture, and the years together grow and fade. Tested: a new ruling culture starts again, and a dip takes off one year.
  4. **Medium: trait data was not validated, and the contract's limit could be exceeded.** Both are now validated, and `hybridShare` must lie in (0, ½].
  5. **Medium: Desert nomads counted desert-river farmers.** Desert herding is now away from rivers and lakes.
  6. **Low:**
     - assimilation events cite causes;
     - the stale subtraction from parents' counts is gone;
     - the hash counts each culture's traits;
     - the comment on the environment flags is updated;
     - new tests cover a tolerant realm slowing assimilation, kin assimilating at any size, culture death after full assimilation, the hybrid's colour and a smaller ruling people's hybrid, each trait's condition, and the text of all three new or changed events.
- **Not changed:** the browser check covers the inspector's people generally. Traits and a hybrid's parents are schema-checked in every frame and were looked at in the lab by hand, because a short browser run cannot be sure to reach either.

- **M4 acceptance (hybrids):** every seed has a hybrid culture by 2,000 (1–5; the first between 981 and 1,383).
  - No hybrid split within 50 years of forming.
  - None joins two cultures of one family: kin no longer fuse, so there are fewer hybrids than before the review (Aster has one).
  - Hybrid names join their parents' (Fiagail + Sidiazis → Fiagiazis).
- **Assimilation is gradual:**
  - about half of civilizations' people are of another culture than their realm's at 1,000, a third at 2,000, and a sixth to a fifth at 3,000;
  - 781–1,213 regions take up their realm's culture over 3,000 years;
  - cultures die out: living cultures fall from 446–625 at 1,000 to 324–409 at 3,000.
- **Traits** in every seed (321–507 earned by 3,000): Mountain folk 111–162, Desert nomads 103–156, Seafarers 60–129, Builders 0–65, River builders 2–7. About two thirds of living cultures hold one at 3,000.
- **In the lab** (my own instance, Elsewhere to year 1,100), the inspector showed:
  - "Heivai · formed from the Heivulu (62%) and Skerai (38%), year 981", with descent "62% Heivulu, 38% Skerai" and 79 regions;
  - a mountain tribe "Known as: Mountain folk".
- **M4.1 still holds:** 10–22 splits from a culture that rules a civilization by 2,000, and the spread of values between cultures stays 0.12–0.18.
- **Gates hold in every seed:**
  - **M3 borders:** the ratio at 1,000 is 1.026–1.181; civilizations at 1,000 number 19–31.
  - **Dominance:** the largest share is at most 30.4% from 1,000 on, with runs above 35% of 0–102 years.
  - **M1:** silent band-years 0, and the longest stay above 1.1× capacity is 2–10 months.
  - **M2:** growth ×4.2–5.6, and Agriculture invented independently 27–42 times by 1,000.
  - **M3b:**
    - settlements by water or a site 88–93%;
    - 3–6 wonders by 2,500;
    - road coverage 100% at 1,500;
    - bridges within 2–6 years of Engineering where it is reached (Elsewhere does not reach it by 3,000);
    - fields regrew in every seed.
  - **M3c:**
    - treasuries 1.2–1.9 years of costs;
    - at most 4 tier changes of one settlement in any 100 years, with 0–4 settlements changing more than 3 times;
    - famine kills less in rich realms at 2,000 and 3,000.
- **Eras:** the leader reaches the Classical era at 1,638–2,400.
- **Speed:** 100–150 ms per simulated year.

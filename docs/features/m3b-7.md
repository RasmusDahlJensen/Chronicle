# M3b.7 — Cultivated land

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3b, slice 7. Status: verified (6 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run; the M3b close-out follows.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Each region keeps a set of cultivated cells:** its best farmland nearest its main settlement site, sized by the people who farm it.
- **Fields are cleared as farmers need them,** within about a year. Land farmed before it is cleared yields less.
- **Fields others have left fall fallow over the years.** After a famine, a people's fields shrink; they regrow as the people recover.
- **The map shows the cultivated land as an overlay** (the Fields layer). The region inspector gives its share of the region's farmland.

**Relevant spec sections:**
- VISION.md "Settlements and infrastructure", Cultivated land: "Each region keeps a set of cultivated cells, chosen from its best farmland nearest its settlements and sized by its farming population. It grows with farming and shrinks after famine, devastation or collapse."
- VISION.md "Region" data model: cultivated cells.
- VISION.md "Display data": cultivation display data in the separate simulation contract.
- M3b lab: "the cultivated-land overlay".
- M3b acceptance: "In each seed, at least one civ's cultivated land shrinks within 10 years after a recorded famine and later regrows."
- Devastation comes with war (M6).

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **The VISION.md line above**, measured in the five-seed study: the regions of a civilization's famine have less cultivated land within 10 years, and later as much as before or more.
- **Fields stay within the region's farmland** (an invariant). They grow toward what its farmers work and fall back when they are fewer.
- **The overlay draws exactly the cultivated cells** the simulation reports (browser check).
- **Gates:** M1–M3 and M3b.1–6 gates hold, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Fields** (`fields.ts`; `FIELD_TUNING`): a region's fields are farm labour (people-equivalents) under cultivation, at most its farmland's.
  - **Target:** each month the target is the share of the region's farmland its farmers work, the share of the land's farm yield they reap: farmland × (1 − e^(−farm workers ÷ the food model's farm labour)). More farmers clear more, ever more slowly as the land fills.
  - **Clearing:** fields below the target are cleared at the target's whole a year, so about a year from nothing.
  - **Fallow:** fields above it fall fallow by a quarter of the gap a year. Regions nobody lives in only fall fallow, and a remnant under one person's work is gone.
  - **Output:** while fields are below the target, farm output is × (0.8 + 0.2 × fields ÷ target): land farmed before it is cleared yields 80%.
  - **Earlier versions:**
    - fields = farm workers, capped at the farmland: regions at capacity stayed fully cultivated, so famines barely shrank them;
    - uncleared land at 60%: it caused 24% more famines, uncited.
- **Farmland:** each region's cells with at least 1 person of farm labour per km², or a grain site (its labour on its cell, as the food model counts it).
  - Desert, tundra and most mountains are never fields; people may still farm them, and the food model counts them as before.
  - **Ranking:** cells are ranked once by labour × 1 ÷ (1 + cells away from the region's best settlement site ÷ 4); the fields cover the cells whose cumulative labour they reach. Settlements founded later do not change it.
- **The acceptance measure** (`watchFamine`, `checkFamineWatches`): each famine onset of a civilization records the fields of the regions it struck.
  - **Shrank:** within 10 years they fall below 98% of that.
  - **Regrew:** later they are back to it.
  - Watches end at regrowth, at 10 years without shrinking, or at 100 years.
  - Measured on the struck regions rather than the whole civilization, which grows and unites meanwhile. Each onset batch is a watch (a famine spreading later adds watches).
- **Contract** (protocol 17, rules 14):
  - the region map's `fieldRank` (u16 per cell; 65535 for no farmland), validated: none on water, and within each region 0, 1, … with no gaps or repeats;
  - frames' `fields` (region, cultivated cell count), validated;
  - the inspector's `fields` (share of farmland, cells).
  - The region map is 3.3 MB for a large world; the lab's limit rises from 4 to 6 MB.
- **Lab:**
  - the Fields layer (on by default) paints cultivated cells wheat-coloured, every other diagonal darker, over the territories and under the borders and marks;
  - the tint is faint at world scale, so the peoples' colours show through, and stronger zoomed in;
  - it is recomputed only when the counts change;
  - the canvas reports `data-field-cells`; the inspector shows "Fields: 45% of its farmland cultivated (32 cells)".
- **M3b.6 review fixes:** recorded in that brief.

## M3b close-out

- **Performance pass** (VISION.md: "well under 50 ms per simulated year … measure through the real worker path").
  - **Profile.** Chronicle, years 2,500–2,600, without invariants: 62 ms per year. Of the step:
    - production 32%: the labour allocation, housing, and a second pass for income;
    - decisions 25%: the Build view 10%, travel searches about 9%;
    - research 10%;
    - population 10%.
  - **Invariants** checked every tick cost about 26 ms per year more, spread over thousands of settlements, groups and regions.
  - **Changes:**
    - income is summed region by region as production houses townspeople, not in a second pass (identical results apart from floating-point rounding);
    - the Build view looks up works and wonders under way once and skips site income until mines or quarries are known;
    - the region building-effect cache from M3b.6.
  - **Result:** no measurable gain in the late centuries. The cost is spread thinly over thousands of groups and settlements, with no single hot spot left.
  - **Over 0–2,500 the simulation alone averages about 40 ms per year** (Chronicle), under the target but not "well under". The late centuries run at 60–65 ms, and the study's worker path with per-tick invariants at 64–97 ms (3,000 years in 3–5 minutes per seed).
  - Meeting the target in late centuries needs algorithmic work (cheaper labour allocation, staggered decision views, sampled invariants in the live lab); this is put to the user at the M3b stop.

- **Map labels.** Settlement names no longer pile on each other. They are placed largest place first, capitals first within a tier, and a name that would overlap one already placed is left out; zooming in brings the rest. In the year-3,000 detail view 121 names are drawn instead of 450, legibly.

## Independent milestone review

One general-purpose agent reviewed M3b as a whole (`civ-m3..a298930` plus the label change).
- **Verdict:** M3b meets every VISION.md acceptance line, the M1, M2, M3 and M3.5 gates hold in the final study, and nothing in the code blocks the tag.
- **What it checked:**
  - **Causes:** it scanned 98,256 building, 6,308 road and 72 wonder events; every one has causes.
  - **Determinism:** two independent runs, with and without invariants, gave the same hash at year 800.
  - **Fields measure:** it is faithful. In matched controls (regions without famine), fields shrank 1.3% of the time, against 33% after a famine.
- **Fixed:**
  - Shrines and temples cited "unrest" for any instability (2,102 in Chronicle, of which 29 followed real unrest). They now cite instability and piety, in proportion to the need.
  - Capitals' names win label space first. Label placement is a pure, tested function (`src/renderer/labels.ts`).
  - Settled civilizations' region squares give way to their settlements once zoomed in.
  - The road-route reuse share and the tier-fall thresholds moved into tuning data.
  - ARCHITECTURE's stale wealth line; the README wording.
  - This brief's famine rate and tier-churn figures.
  - The reproduction commands and steps above.
- **Put to the user at the stop** (below), not changed.

**Questions for the user at the M3b stop:**
1. **Persistence:** VISION.md's order puts P (saving and resuming) before M4.
2. **Performance:** see the close-out. The late centuries run at 60–65 ms per year, and the lab, with invariants checked every month, at 64–97 ms, against a target of well under 50.
3. **Wealth never limits anything.**
   - Treasuries reach 74–141 billion by 3,000; the largest civilization holds about 1,000 years of its income.
   - The cost term of the Build choice is negligible, no building, road or wonder has been lost to unpaid upkeep, and wonders and roads arrive purely on need.
   - VISION.md's wealth sinks (trade in M5, armies in M6) come later; scaling wealth or costs down now is an option.
4. **Famine frequency** rises to 5–9 a year worldwide by 2,900, mostly from droughts.
5. **Tier churn:** a quarter to a third of all events are small settlements crossing the village–town line, often within years. Remedies: demote only after some years below the threshold, or spread a region's swing over its settlements.
6. **The food-security agent default marked in VISION.md:** farmers' reserve, and stores carrying people through famine only within capacity.
7. **Wonders of unknown peoples:** a civilization knows which wonders stand or are being built anywhere, including by peoples it never met (an M3b.4 decision against pillar 4).
8. **Atlas's largest polity** holds 40–50% of the world's people from 700 on, above story health's 35% (but below the degenerate 60%). This has been so since M3.5 and was not raised before.
9. **Do nothing is 94–98% of decision steps** after 1,500. M3.2 expected Build to bring it under 70%, but one decision builds a whole batch and wealth never binds.

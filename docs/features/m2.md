# M2 — Knowledge and resources

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M2, delivered as one slice. Status: verified, awaiting user review (2 October 2026); committed on `main` and tagged `civ-m2`; not pushed, so CI has not run.

**Observable outcome:** bands research a tech graph from Stone to Atomic; on fertile river and lake land, once their land has filled, they take up Agriculture (herding on grassland), and neighbours learn it from them within years. Farmers settle into civilizations with a named village capital and grow many times over; their surplus frees specialists, who drive later research. In the lab, markers are colored by era, villages appear, and the inspector shows known techs, current research with its weights, and specialists.

**Relevant spec sections and agreed decisions:** VISION.md "Knowledge and technology" (research, weights, eras, resource gating, mobility, starter list), "Food, population and borders" (production, population, specialists, settling), "Core loop and time" (harvest calendar, store with Pottery), M2 acceptance, story health (plateau stop from M2 on), rules 4–6 and 8. User feedback in memory: growth limited by what the land feeds, graded trade-offs, every behaviour with an inspectable reason.

**Scenario, inputs, and expected result:** the five study seeds at Large; Agriculture is first discovered by year 600 in every seed, in most seeds in a top-quartile farming region with river tier ≥ river or open-lake access; the world grows at least 3× faster in the 300 years after a quarter of living polities know Agriculture than in the 300 years before; no polity holds or enters another landmass before Sailing (checked every month); every `RESOURCE_RULES` extraction tech exists by name.

**Boundary/failure cases and invariants:** the invariants of M1 (exact population and food accounting, one polity per region) plus: a polity off its home landmass knows Sailing; knowledge arrays and progress are consistent; specialists are whole people within the population; a civilization owns its region and has a living capital village on a land cell of that region; owners are living civilizations.

**In scope / out of scope:** in: everything in VISION.md M2 with the neutral forms it names (contact = within two regions; settlements are named villages; no harbors or mines yet; specialists produce research only). Out: expansion, joining, region knowledge, migration between populated regions (M3); wealth, buildings, cultivated land (M3b); persistence (P).

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Tech graph** (`src/simulation/techs.ts`): 63 techs from Stone to Atomic with prerequisites, costs, tags, environment affinities and effects limited to kinds the systems understand (coefficient multipliers, food methods, resource reveal / extraction / use, mobility, unlocks for later milestones). Starting techs Foraging, Fire, Hunting, Fishing; Agriculture requires Pottery. Costs: Neolithic 600–7,000 (what a band's base research reaches in centuries), Bronze 2.7–4.8 M, Iron 8–11 M, Classical 13–16 M, later eras rising to 0.42 G for Spaceflight (Classical and later were lowered by 40% after the first study put Classical at 2,380–2,660, target 2,000 ± 300). Bronze and later costs are set for one-region civilizations whose specialists research; M3 recalibrates them once civilizations span many regions. `validateTechs` checks the graph at worker startup, including every `RESOURCE_RULES` extraction tech name.
- **Research** (`research.ts`, `knowledge.ts`, `RESEARCH_TUNING`): points a year = (0.006 × min(people, 1,500) × (1 + 0.08 × contacts, at most 12) + 0.3 × specialists) × the techs' research multiplier. Interpretation of "small base research per person": experience and tinkering grow with people up to a community of about 1,500; beyond that, research needs specialists. Without the cap, the single largest band on any landmass decided when Agriculture appeared (a 4,500-person band on a small Chronicle landmass invented it ~100 years before the main continent, and its farmers then dominated world growth).
- **Target choice:** weighted random among available techs. Weight = base weight × need (food techs × (1 + 6 × food need), food need = max(hunger, land pressure)) × environment affinities × exposure (× (1 + 12 × exposure × (0.5 + Openness))) × effort (1 ÷ (1 + years to finish ÷ 100)) × Tradition brake on economy techs (× (1 − 0.6 × Tradition)). Effort is an addition: without it a small band could spend millennia on a Bronze tech. A deposit on the polity's land that it knows but cannot work multiplies its extraction tech's weight by 2 (`knownUnusable`, every extraction tech). Once a year a polity reconsiders and redraws if any option now weighs 1.5× its current target; research put into any tech is kept per tech; after a discovery the next target is chosen at once. Agriculture has base weight 0.05 and fertile-river affinity ×40 (×1.5 river or lake, ×4 wild grain), so well-placed bands choose it and others mostly learn it from neighbours; Animal husbandry 0.05 with grassland ×20, fertile river ×2, river or lake ×1.2. The fertile-river class is VISION.md's acceptance class (top-quartile farming potential with river tier ≥ river or open-lake access), so the acceptance region and the environment weight coincide by design.
- **Contacts and diffusion:** contacts are polities within two regions over land, and over sea links up to 300 km with Sailing (any with Navigation), refreshed yearly; all have intensity 1 in M2 (`farContact`). Exposure to a tech = summed intensity of contacts that know it, at most 1, recomputed every month; it discounts the cost by 99.8% and raises the weight. Discoveries take effect after every polity has researched that month, so knowledge moves at most one contact a month (review: it chained through hundreds of polities in one tick, in polity-id order). It crosses a connected region within years to decades, but not uninhabited gaps or seas.
- **Farming and herding** (`food.ts`, `FARM_TUNING`): farming labour capacity = Σ cell area × biome density (grassland 10/km² … desert 0.4) × fertility^1.2, plus 3,000 per grain site; herding = Σ area × biome density (grassland 1, steppe 1.2 …). Yields: farming 2.6 × river/lake multiplier (1.05 stream, 1.15 river or open lake, 1.35 great river), herding 2.2, then the techs' multipliers. Neolithic farming capacity is about 24 people/km² of fertility-weighted land (VISION.md: 10–40), herding about 1–2/km².
- **Harvest calendar and crops in the field:** harvests come in September north of 20°, March south of −20°, April and October in the tropics. What farm workers sow each month is kept as crops in the field (`planted`, integer food units with its own ledger: sown, harvested, lost) and the harvest moves all of it into the store; people who move or die out lose their crops (recorded). Sowing rule: when the store (less what will perish before the harvest) will not cover need until the harvest, people value crops they must wait for below their yield — down to 30% of it with an empty store — and workers flow to whichever method then gives the most. A band that learns Agriculture with an empty store therefore sows little at first, while a large farming people whose other food is saturated keeps its fields and goes hungry for a while instead. (Review: the first version paid harvests from the harvest month's labour only, and its all-or-nothing bridging rule made fed people die of "famine"; probe: 26 M famine deaths among fed civilizations in years 900–920, now 1,304.)
- **Food security** is expected food ÷ need, or the share of need eaten in a month when people went short. Foragers and herders: the year's expected food from the land. Farmers: the coming harvest (crops in the field plus what is still to be sown before it) and other food, over one harvest cycle's need, so cutting a month's sowing lowers it by that month's share only. Stored food counts by preventing short months, not as security of its own: with Pottery's twelve-month store, counting stored food let bands live 50+ months above capacity (M1 allows 24), and counting the bridge to the harvest held young farming peoples at zero growth (the store is capped at twelve months and perishes) until hundreds died out.
- **Specialists:** share = cap × clamp(0.3 + 3.5 × (food security − 1), 0, 1), cap = 2% × the techs' specialist multipliers (Pottery 1.5, Agriculture 2, Animal husbandry 1.3, Writing 1.2 …). Specialists live in settlements (VISION.md), so bands have none. A people living at what its land feeds keeps 30% of its cap; surplus frees the rest.
- **Settling:** a band that knows Agriculture or Animal husbandry settles with yearly chance clamp((years in region − 10) ÷ 20, 0, 1) × (0.3 + 0.7 × share of food from farming and herding); it becomes a civilization, owns its region and founds a named village (best settlement site) as capital. Events: settled, settlement founded.
- **Mobility:** Boatbuilding gives river boats (no reach change in M2); Sailing allows moves, splits and contact over sea links up to 300 km; Navigation over any. Sea reach comes only from knowing Sailing or Navigation, never from a later mobility level (rail, flight). A lineage's home landmass is where its first band began, and daughters keep it, so the invariant — checked every month — says: off its home landmass, a polity knows Sailing (review: a daughter took its destination as home, and Railways implied ocean reach).
- **Resource gating** (`deposits.ts`): per polity each deposit is unknown, known or usable from the reveal / extraction / use techs; known-but-unusable deposits on its land raise their extraction tech's weight. Startup checks that every tech's extraction effects are exactly what `RESOURCE_RULES` gives it.
- **Capacity solves:** a polity's capacity is solved yearly (with its band decisions), on arrival in a region, and monthly while its people exceed 1.04 × the last solve or the region's game stock has moved by more than 0.01 since then; knowledge only raises capacity, so the cached value stays within a few percent and the 1.1× over-capacity check reads the same (the review measured one missed over-capacity month, of a six-person band, before the game-drift trigger; longest streak unchanged).
- **Civilizations that die out** leave a ruined capital and a "civilization comes to an end" event with its cause (hunger or dwindling).
- **Eras are labels** computed from the latest-era tech a polity knows, so the first Bronze-era polity is the first to learn any Bronze tech — usually Irrigation — not Bronze working (VISION.md: eras are labels, never gates).
- **Forager retuning (M1 numbers):** forager, hunting and fishing capacities × 0.45 (foragers about 0.06 people/km² of productive land, within VISION.md's 0.05–0.2) and the birth slope 0.015 → 0.03 (unconstrained net growth up to 3%/year, VISION.md's starting value is about 1%). The growth criterion compares the 300 years before and after a quarter of polities know Agriculture; with foragers still spreading at 0.5–1%/year then, no farming boom is 3× faster. Faster foragers fill their land by about year 500, so the transition is sharp.
- `SIMULATION_RULES_VERSION` 3; `SIMULATION_PROTOCOL_VERSION` 3 (frame: polity markers with kind and era, settlements, summary counts, knowledge inspection; worker report: yearly series and knowledge report).

Change (rules version 4, protocol 5, so histories differ from `civ-m2`):
- **Tribes:** a polity holds one population group in each of its regions — a band, and after settling a village — and a heartland group (`state.ts`: `Polity.groups`, `core`; `groupAt` per region). Each band moves and splits on its own, as before. A split stays in its tribe as a new band (event: band spread) unless it breaks away as a new tribe with a daughter culture (event: band split), with chance 1 − e^(−s·c): s = (great-circle km from the heartland band ÷ 1,200)³ + (the tribe's bands ÷ 30)², c = 1 + 0.8 × (Expansionism − Tradition) (`BAND_TUNING.reachKm`, `distancePower`, `tribeBands`, `sizePower`, `cultureWeight`, validated). Its causes record distance from the heartland and tribe size. Knowledge, culture, contacts and research belong to the tribe.
- **Settling:** the tribe settles once its heartland band has stayed long enough and its people (weighted) farm or herd; the whole tribe becomes one civilization owning all its regions, with a named village in each, the capital in the heartland. A group that dies out frees its region and ruins its village; the heartland and capital pass to the largest remaining group (event: capital moved); the last group's death ends the polity (civilization destroyed).
- **Research per polity:** base research is 0.006 × min(the polity's people, 1,500) × contact bonus, plus 0.3 per specialist. Interpretation of "small base research per person": a tribe shares its ideas, so all its bands are one community, and spreading over more regions does not by itself make a people inventive. Summing the per-band cap instead (the first implementation) gave tribes of 10–15 bands 10× the research: Agriculture came at year ~200, before the land filled, tribes settled and the map froze half-empty (civilizations cannot expand until M3). Environment weights are graded by the share of the polity's people living where each condition holds (1 + share × (affinity − 1)), so one fertile valley among many regions draws a tribe to farming less than a people living on them. A tribe's discoveries are placed in its heartland, in the record of firsts (which the acceptance check reads) and in the event alike.
- **Costs and storage recalibrated:** civilizations now pool specialists across all their villages, which brought Bronze to ~690 and Classical to ~850. Bronze-and-later costs ×8 restore VISION.md's era targets (anticipated above: "M3 recalibrates them once civilizations span many regions"). Pottery's store limit rose from 12 to 14 months: with 2% spoilage a month, 12 months could not bridge a single yearly harvest, so every farming group in the north and south went short in the month before its harvest; large groups absorbed it, but small villages that farm only shrank ~2% a year and died out (221 in Aster by year 1,500, leaving holes in civilizations' land). VISION.md "Time": farmers can always store a harvest. A test now follows villages through two harvests and checks that a harvest of at least the store limit feeds them every month until the next.
- **Lab:** Peoples **By polity** (default; each tribe or civilization its own colour, `polityColor`), By descent, By era, Hidden; the legend lists the largest polities (kind, regions, people) from the frame's `largest` list, which the contract cross-checks against the markers.

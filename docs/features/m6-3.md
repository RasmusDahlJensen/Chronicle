# M6.3 — The ravages of war

**Feature / status:** the third slice of M6, "War and peace" (VISION.md; the plan is in the [M6.1 brief](m6-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** VISION.md's "Diplomacy and war": "Regions can be occupied when war score on that front passes a threshold. Occupation causes devastation (lower food output for years, cultivated land lost) and may loot, burn or raze settlements, destroy bridges and roads, and create refugees." Its "Destruction": "when a region is occupied or raided, its settlements can be looted (wealth and stored food taken), burned (buildings destroyed, people killed or driven out as refugees) or razed (wiped out, leaving named ruins on the map). The chance depends on the attacker's Militarism, ruler traits (Cruel), religious hatred, the war goal and the settlement's walls. Bridges and roads can be destroyed to stop an advance." M6.2 made wars that occupy land; this slice makes occupation cost the people who live there, and leaves its marks: devastated land, ruins, broken roads. And "conquest that pays": loot fills the treasury, and army size follows recent war income.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Devastation:** an occupied region is devastated month by month (0–1); devastation cuts its harvests, burns its fields (they regrow as farmers clear them again), unsettles it and drives some of its people out, and fades over years after the occupier leaves.
- **Looting, burning and razing:** while it holds a region, the occupier may each year
  - **loot** its settlements: wealth from the owner's treasury (the region's share) and stored food taken (exact flows);
  - **burn** them: buildings destroyed, townspeople killed (war deaths) or driven out as refugees;
  - **raze** one: wiped out, leaving named ruins (crossed out on the map); its townspeople killed or driven out. A region left without a settlement founds one again when the occupation ends, on the ruins ("razed and later resettled").
  The chance follows the occupier's Militarism, religious hatred (a holy war, a rival faith), whether it means to keep the land (the war's goal) and the settlement's walls; never a capital. Each is an event with its causes.
- **Roads and bridges destroyed:** occupied land loses roads and bridges (bridges first, "to stop an advance").
- **Refugees:** people driven from burned or razed settlements and from devastated land flee to the nearest region of their own realm that is not occupied (migration in the population ledger); a large flight is an event.
- **War income:** loot and tribute received raise what a realm can afford for its army for some years (VISION.md M6 "army size follows threat and recent war income").
- **The game:** the wars layer shades devastated land; the Region tab shows devastation, what was looted, burned or razed, and refugees; the ledger's world now counts all ruins (the frame carries the total, not only the last ones); the chronicle and stories tell the ravages.

## Acceptance

- **Tests:** devastation rises under occupation, cuts the harvest and fields, and fades; looting moves wealth and food exactly; burning destroys buildings and kills or drives out townspeople exactly; razing ruins a settlement, and the region refounds on its ruins when the occupation ends; roads and bridges destroyed; refugees counted as migrants; war income raises the army budget; the invariants hold through all of it.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):** in most seeds at least one settlement is razed and later resettled, and at least one bridge or road is destroyed in war (VISION.md M6); ruins exist after M6; M6.2's war health and the M5 gates still hold.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **State:** `state.devastation` per region (0–1); each civilization's war income (`warIncome`: a value and when, fading); each occupied region's flight this year (`fled`, on its occupation entry). Settlements already had `status` alive, ruined or razed. Metrics: settlements looted, burned and razed, razed ruins resettled, buildings burned, roads and bridges destroyed in war, refugees.
- **Devastation** (`ravages.ts` `ravage`, monthly in the war system after the fronts, before the levy): an occupied region gains 0.5 a year (at most 1), and its fields lose 0.3 × devastation of their cultivated land a year while occupied (farmers clear them again); elsewhere it halves every 4 years (gone below 0.01). Effects: the harvest × (1 − 0.6 × devastation) (`harvestYield`, with the weather and drought; a famine there names devastation, not drought, for its part); a stability burden of 0.25 × devastation ("devastation"); while occupied, 5% × devastation of its people a year flee (told once a year).
- **Ravages** (monthly per occupied region): each living settlement but a capital is razed with chance 0.06 ÷ 12, else burned with 0.06 ÷ 12, else looted with 0.2 ÷ 12, each × the occupier's intent: (½ + Militarism) × (1 + hatred: a holy war or a rival state religion) × (1 − its walls' defence), razing × ¼ where the occupier fights to keep the land (its goal).
  - **Looting:** the owner's treasury × 0.5 × the settlement's share of its townspeople (or of its people, shared among the region's settlements, if larger) goes to the occupier (wealth flows given and received, and the occupier's war income); 40% of the region's stored food, shared among its settlements, is carried off to the occupier's nearest land (food carried out and in).
  - **Burning:** each building is destroyed with chance 0.4 (a harbor lost closes the sea there and redraws the realm's sight); 5% of its townspeople die (war deaths, the war's dead on its owner's side) and 15% flee; the rest live where housing is left (`house`), or stay as rural people.
  - **Razing:** the settlement becomes razed ruins: its buildings and any wonder lost, works there abandoned; 30% of its townspeople die and the rest flee (those with nowhere to flee die), all counted as the war's dead; its owner's side tires (exhaustion + 0.05: VISION.md "destroyed cities").
  - **Roads and bridges:** a bridged road on its edges loses its bridge with chance 0.3 ÷ 12 a month (its upkeep then a road's); a road without one is broken with 0.25 ÷ 12.
- **Refugees** go to the owner's nearest region (by land through its own) not occupied, as migrants out and in (exact), their values blending into their hosts'; none move when all its land is occupied. A flight of 2,000 or more is an event (from a burned or razed town at once, from devastated land once a year).
- **Rebuilding:** a region whose last settlement was razed has none while occupied, and after it lies in ruins until its people rebuild: each month with chance 0.3 ÷ 12 × (1 − devastation), and at the latest when its devastation is gone; half the time on the ruins ("razed and later resettled"), half on a free site beside them, the ruins left standing (they stay on the map until the region grows into them, as VISION.md's ruins do). The invariant that every civilization region has a living settlement allows one occupied or devastated; nothing grows or is resettled by growth in a region an enemy holds or that has no settlement.
- **War income** ("conquest that pays"): loot and tribute received, halving every 10 years; the army budget (M6.1's) adds 0.1 × it.
- **Frames** (protocol 43): the inspected region's devastation and its stability burdens (an enemy holding it, devastation); the frame's devastated regions (0.05 or more, for the wars layer) and the count of all ruins and of those razed; events: settlement looted, burned and razed, roads and bridges broken in war, refugees.
- **The game:** the wars layer shades devastated land in ash; the Region tab says how devastated a region is, whether its people have yet to rebuild, and lists the occupation and devastation among what lowers its stability; the ledger's world now counts all ruins (those razed in war among them) and the regions devastated; the lab's inspector shows devastation; the war story tells its war's ravages, a decline the towns it lost.
- **Development control:** "Raze a settlement here" (test only, like Dev 02's others) razes a settlement of the selected region's civilization through the same rule, the region devastated as war would leave it.
- **Rules version 33.**

## Calibration

With the fork tool (five large seeds from year 500, `.chronicle/forks/m6-3-cal1`): razing at a chance of 0.04 razed and resettled settlements in 3 of 5 seeds (0–3 each), and broke roads in every seed (2–18); at 0.08, with roads broken at 0.3, every seed razed 2–15 settlements and resettled most of them, and broke 11–47 roads. The first runs found three invariant breaks, each fixed with its test: townspeople left above the housing a burned building took with it (now rehoused, the rest stay as rural people), a burned harbor still counted (and the realm's sight not redrawn), and a road whose bridge was torn down still paying a bridge's upkeep.

## Review

The independent review (an agent) ran three 3,000-year histories with every invariant checked each month (all held), razed a region's last settlement and ended its occupation every way one can end (the score turning, white peace, cession, annexation, the owner or the occupier annexed by a third realm, the owner uniting into another): the invariants held and the region was rebuilt each time. Copies taken mid-war continued exactly. Its findings and what was done:
1. **Ruins almost never existed (material):** a razed region was rebuilt the moment its occupation ended, and growth resettled ruins even while an enemy held the region. Fixed: no growth or resettling in a region an enemy holds or that has no settlement; its people rebuild after a while (by chance, sooner as it heals), and half the time on a free site beside the ruins, which stay on the map.
2. **Decisive conquests were never ravaged (material):** ravages came on the anniversary of an occupation, and a war won by holding its goal a year ended a month before. Fixed: the ravages' chances run month by month (a twelfth of the yearly chance), so any occupation can bring them.
3. **The tests checked nothing on their world (material):** the burned town had no buildings and the razed region no roads. Fixed: the tests furnish a town (a market, an aqueduct, a harbor; full of townspeople) and lay a bridged road on a river edge, and also test walls holding, a capital spared, refugees with nowhere to go, war income, the frame's ravages and its parse check; the browser scenario razes a settlement (a development control, through the same rule) and checks the ruins drawn.
4. **The stability burdens of occupation and devastation were missing from the panels (material):** listed now, in the game and the lab.
5. **Stories did not tell the ravages (material):** a war's story now gathers its war's lootings, burnings, razings and broken roads; a decline names its razed and burned towns and its refugees.
6. **Minor, fixed:** a famine on devastated land named drought for the harvest devastation cut (devastation is its own cause now); the people killed in ravages are the war's dead (its count, its story), and a razed city tires its owner (VISION.md "destroyed cities"); a bridged road loses only its bridge; looted food is shared among a region's settlements (no compounding); refugees are named for the town they fled; looting names a village a village; a burning that destroyed nothing says only what it did; flight from devastated land is told once a year; one devastation threshold (0.05) for the map, the ledger, the panel and the study; "it heals" only once the enemy has gone.
7. **Minor, kept:** loot pays little against a realm's treasury (most war income is tribute); the kinds of the chronicle (refugees under Peoples, roads broken under Settlements) follow the event's type, not its cause.

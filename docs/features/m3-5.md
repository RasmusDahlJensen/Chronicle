# M3.5 — Research paths and shared knowledge

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3, slice 5 (added after the M3 review, 2 October 2026, at the user's request). Status: verified (2 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run. M3 stays tagged `civ-m3` at its review commit; this slice has no tag of its own. **Awaiting the user's review of this slice, and their approval before M3b.**

**Observable outcome:**
- Farming is no longer a timeline that reaches every people within decades of its invention. Each people researches its own path, at its own speed, from its land, needs and culture. River peoples farm first; others herd, build boats or work stone, and come to farming centuries later or not at all.
- Knowing a people who farms teaches nothing by itself.
- A civilization can choose to share knowledge with a people it knows. The chronicle records the exchange and why it happened; for 40 years both sides research what the other knows several times faster.
- A people far behind what it knows of the world catches up a little faster on older techs.
- The research panel says what is speeding up the current research: sharing (with whom) or catching up.

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **No automatic learning.** No tech is researched faster because a people without an active exchange knows it. Every month on Chronicle, each polity's progress on its target is its own research times exactly the catch-up for that tech's era and, only when a partner with an exchange in force knows the tech, the sharing speed-up. Contacts still raise the general research rate (VISION.md "scaled by contact").
- **Exchanges.** Every exchange is an event with its causes, records both sides symmetrically (an invariant), and comes from a civilization's decision step.
- **Divergence**, in every study seed:
  - Agriculture is invented independently (with no sharing) in at least three places, over at least 200 years;
  - at year 1,000 the settled civilizations span at least two eras.
- **Catch-up** applies only to techs of an earlier era than the polity's frontier, and is bounded (unit test).
- **Unions and joins** keep what either people knew (unit test).
- **Existing gates still hold:**
  - M2 acceptance (Agriculture by 600 on fertile river land; growth ≥ 3× after a quarter farm);
  - M3 acceptance (10–60 civs at 1,000; borders above the median; first contact; unions; the labelled scenario's expansion gap);
  - M1's checks;
  - the complete `npm run check`.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **No automatic learning** (`research.ts`, `knowledge.ts`):
  - Exposure is gone: its cost discount (0.998, which made a contact's techs almost free), its weight bonus, contact intensities and the exposure cache.
  - Contacts (polities met within two regions) still raise the base research rate (`contactBonus`, as VISION.md's "scaled by contact"); they teach no particular tech.
- **Research speed** (`researchRate`):
  - Base research = 0.004 per person up to 1,500 people, then 1,500 × (people ÷ 1,500)^0.15, × (1 + 0.08 × contacts, at most 12), plus 0.3 per specialist (less in unrest), × the techs' research multiplier.
  - A tribe of 100,000 researches about 1.9× one of 1,500, instead of the same.
  - It was 0.006 per person, capped at 1,500.
- **Catch-up** (`catchUp`):
  - A tech of an era behind the polity's frontier is researched 1 + 0.25 × eras behind (at most 4) times as fast, so at most twice as fast.
  - The frontier is the most advanced era it knows of: its own, or a living people's it has met. It is refreshed yearly with contacts.
  - It covers both readings of the user's words: a tech behind one's own age, and being far behind the peoples one knows.
- **Sharing** (`decisions/choose.ts`, `sharing.ts`, `SHARE_TUNING`):
  - **Candidates.** The view lists each people in contact (tribe or civilization) with:
    - what each side would teach and learn (peoples in contact see what each other can do);
    - kinship and culture similarity;
    - willingness = (1 − their Tradition × (1 − similarity)) × (0.5 + 0.5 × min(1, techs they would learn ÷ 4)): a people that would learn nothing is half as ready to give.
  - **Exclusions.** A people it already shares with, or one that refused in the last 20 years, is not listed.
  - **Score** = (0.2 × min(1, techs to learn ÷ 4) + (0.03 if kin + 0.02 × similarity + 0.06 × Openness) × min(1, techs to teach ÷ 4) − 0.12 × Tradition − 0.05 × exchanges it already has) × willingness. The score is −∞ with nothing to teach or learn.
    - What it would learn is the main draw. Only open, kin peoples of little Tradition teach for nothing in return.
    - **Review finding, fixed.** The first tuning (learn 0.3; kin 0.1, similarity 0.1, Openness 0.15; Tradition 0.15; no cost per exchange; acceptance not weighted by gain) made sharing close to automatic. Chronicle had 2,532 exchanges by 1,000, 2,500 of them gifts to tribes, about 97% accepted, and 52 of 68 Agriculture discoveries helped. With every civ teaching every tribe nearby, that is "friendly neighbours teach you" in all but name.
  - **The exchange.**
    - The other side accepts with chance = willingness.
    - It runs both ways for 40 years. It is an event (knowledge shared) with the decision's drivers as causes, and is held on both sides with the same end.
    - While it runs, a tech the partner knows is researched 1 + 2 × (0.5 + Openness) times as fast (×3 at Openness 0.5), and weighs 1 + 4 × (0.5 + Openness) times as much.
  - Tribes have no decision step, so they share only when a civilization offers. The salt for the acceptance draw is SHARING = 4.
- **Merging** (`mergeKnowledge`, `learnFrom`, in `transferGroup` and `unite`):
  - A tribe that joins, a band a civilization takes in, or a civilization that unites into another, brings its techs. The union knows what either knew and keeps the receiver's research in progress.
  - The join, absorption and union events record how many techs were new to the receiver.
  - **Absorption: an M1 failure, found and fixed.**
    - The first five-seed study broke M1's "no region above 1.1× capacity for more than 24 months" in Elsewhere: 29 months, against at most 10 before.
    - The scenario was preserved first (seed Elsewhere, region 17, year 1,271). The Hatein, farmers who had never learned herding, took in a band of 11,034 Sataen herders. Under the Hatein's knowledge the land fed 1,483 instead of 30,411, so the herders starved for two years.
    - When every people knew the same techs, absorption could not shrink capacity like this.
    - Absorbed bands now bring their knowledge, like joining tribes. The M2 test checks after every join, absorption and union that the receiver knows all the other knew.
- **How a discovery was reached:**
  - Progress records the points from sharing (`taught`) and from catch-up (`caught`).
  - The event cites ownResearch, sharedKnowledge and catchUp as shares of the progress (they sum to 1), and names the teacher when an exchange still runs.
  - "Independent inventions" counts Agriculture learned with no sharing help (`metrics.agricultureInventions`).
- **M3 border check** (the user's decision, 2 October 2026):
  - It now compares border edges with the edges within civilization-held land, instead of all land edges.
  - Farming spreads people by people, so countries form and merge later (5–8 unions by 1,000, against 125–192 before). At 1,000, civilizations hold only 60–68% of the land, all of it easy farmland, while "all land" includes the empty mountains and deserts.
  - Against all land, the ratio at 1,000 was 0.89–1.01 (failing in 4 of 5 seeds) and passed everywhere only from about 1,100–1,300. Against civilization land it is above 1 from year 900 (1.06–1.23 at 1,000).
  - A stronger crossing cost in expansion (0.1 instead of 0.03 per 1,000 travel-km) made it worse (0.89–0.97). Civilizations then leave the mountain tribes alone and meet each other on easy land. Reverted.
  - The study reports both ratios; M6's 1.5× target stays against all land.
- **M2 test: who settled** (an expectation change in `tests/simulation-m2.test.ts`, made here and explained):
  - The test asserted that more than half of living polities at year 900 are civilizations. It is now 27 of 231, because most remaining polities are small forager tribes that never farmed.
  - Its intent, that farmers settled into civilizations, still holds strongly: 26 of 35 farming or herding polities are civilizations, and 99.9% of the world's people live in civilizations.
  - The assertion now checks exactly that: more than half of farming or herding polities have settled, and civilizations hold most people.
- **M2 test: sharing exercised.** This slice first required more than 20 Agriculture-era discoveries helped by sharing by year 900. That was calibrated to the near-automatic sharing the review rejected; with chosen sharing, Chronicle has 9. The test now requires that some discoveries are helped and named with their teacher. It also requires that every exchange is an event with causes, offered by a civilization, and counted with its offer.
- **M2 growth check** (the user's decision, 2 October 2026): measured from when a quarter of the world's people live in polities that know Agriculture, instead of a quarter of living polities. Counting polities, the mark fell at years 928–1,021, long after the boom (ratio 0.08–0.15), because most polities by then are forager tribes absorbed before they farm.
- **Contract** (protocol 10, rules 7):
  - actions include share;
  - event types append knowledgeShared;
  - the research view replaces exposure with sharedBy, shareSpeed and catchUp;
  - polities list their exchanges;
  - the conditional text in event templates may hold placeholders.

## Results, known gaps, and user review

**Five seeds, 3,000 years** (`.chronicle/studies/m3-5/story-health.md`, final tuning).

**This slice's acceptance**, all passing:
- **No automatic learning.**
  - The Chronicle M2 test checks every month for 900 years that each polity's progress equals its own research × exactly its catch-up and, only with a sharing partner that knows the tech, the sharing speed-up.
  - It also checks that the frontier never exceeds what the polity knows of, and that after every join, absorption or union the receiver knows all the other knew.
  - The reviewer's probe also found 0 violations over 1,000 years on Chronicle and Elsewhere.
- **Divergence:**
  - Agriculture was invented independently 29–34 times by 1,000, spread over 378–468 years in every seed, and 47–72 times by 3,000. Sharing helped a farming discovery only rarely.
  - Polities knowing Agriculture: 2–4% at 700 and 9–12% at 1,000, against about 90% within a century of its invention before.
  - Civilization eras: 2 at 1,000, 3–4 at 2,000 and 4–5 at 3,000. The fewest–most techs a civilization knows: 8–13 at 1,000, 8–29 at 2,000 and 8–39 at 3,000.
- **Exchanges:**
  - 20–90 were agreed by 1,000 (mostly open civilizations teaching kin tribes), and 567–773 by 3,000, about half of the offers.
  - Most exchanges are between civilizations: 461–529 of them by 3,000, once their paths diverge in the Bronze age.
  - Discoveries helped by a named teacher: 69–133 over 3,000 years.
  - Share knowledge takes about 0.4–1.7% of decision steps.

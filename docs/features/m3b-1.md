# M3b.1 — Settlements: tiers, townspeople and housing

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3b, slice 1. Status: verified (6 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run; M3b continues with M3b.2. The user approved M3.5 on 6 October 2026 and asked to finish M3 entirely (M3b), then stop for their approval before moving on.

**Observable outcome:**
- Civilizations' settlements grow from villages into towns and cities as their regions' surplus frees townspeople, and the chronicle records it ("Kesh becomes a city").
- A region whose townspeople outgrow the housing of its settlements founds another settlement on its next best site. Most settlements end up by rivers, lakes, coasts and resource sites.
- A people that returns to land with ruins resettles them, keeping the old name.
- On the map, settlements are drawn by tier, and towns and larger are named as you zoom in. The region inspector lists the region's settlements with their tier, townspeople, housing and history, and splits the region's people into rural and urban.

## Acceptance (this slice)

- Every settled civ has a capital settlement in its heartland (an invariant every month, as before).
- Every civ region has at least one living settlement owned by the civ; the region's townspeople (its specialists) are exactly the sum of its settlements' urban populations, none above its housing (invariants).
- In every study seed, at least 80% of living settlements at years 1,000, 2,000 and 3,000 sit within 1 cell of a river, lake, coast or resource site.
- Towns appear in every seed, and cities by year 1,500 in most seeds (story health).
- Every founding, resettling and tier change is an event with its causes and the settlement's id.
- Lab: settlements by tier with names, and the settlement list in the region inspector (browser scenario).
- The complete `npm run check` passes; M1, M2 and M3 gates hold in the five-seed study.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Townspeople = specialists** (VISION.md: "A region's people are rural … or urban (specialists, soldiers and the elite)"; soldiers and the elite come with M6 and M7).
  - They live in the region's settlements, shared by housing, at most all of it. The rest stay rural.
  - Housing per settlement is 8,000 (`baseHousing`), twice that for the capital, the seat of government. Buildings raise it from M3b.2.
  - Sharing by housing replaced an earlier fill order (capital first, then founding order). Under the fill order the last town absorbed every swing in a region's specialists and changed tier 2,004 times by year 1,000 in Chronicle.
- **Tiers:**
  - village; town at 4,000 townspeople; city at 12,000; metropolis at 50,000;
  - taken from a 12-month moving average of townspeople;
  - a settlement falls a tier below 0.75 × its tier's threshold;
  - checked yearly per civilization in the population system.
  - With the average and the margin, no settlement changed tier more than 6 times in 1,000 years.
  - Each change is an event with the settlement's id. Rising cites townspeople; falling cites hunger, unrest and fewer townspeople.
- **Growth.** When a region's townspeople fill 90% of its housing, it founds another settlement on the next free candidate site, or resettles ruins (salt GROWING = 5 on the civilization's population-system stream).
- **Placement:**
  - Candidate sites (`regions.ts`) gain a water bonus of 1.5 for a cell on or next to a river, lake, coast or resource site. This is derived simulation data; geography is untouched. The region partition is unchanged, so `REGION_PARTITION_VERSION` stays 1; rules version 8.
  - A region's first settlement takes its best site wherever it lies. Further ones need a free site by water or a resource site: dry country holds one village, and its extra townspeople stay rural.
  - About 30% of civilization regions (603 of 1,997 in Chronicle at 1,000) have no candidate site by water. They are real farmland with a median of 50,000 people; their villages are the dry 9–14%.
  - Without the rule, the share by water was 77%. Without the water bonus, only 63–68% of each region's best site lay by water.
- **Ruins** are resettled before a new site is used. They keep their old name with chance 0.5; otherwise they are renamed, and the event says so.
  - Settlements in ruins keep their place in `state.regionSettlements`, which lists every settlement ever founded per region.
  - No ruins were resettled in the study: civilization regions are rarely abandoned before war (M6).
- **Contract** (protocol 11, rules 8):
  - settlement arrays add tiers, and names for towns and larger and for capitals;
  - the region inspector lists the region's settlements with tier, townspeople, housing, founding, former name, owner and their latest six events;
  - event templates for tier changes and resettled ruins.
- **Lab:**
  - Villages are small diamonds (shown from zoom 1.5), towns larger diamonds (from zoom 1), cities rings, metropolises double rings, and capitals stars sized by tier.
  - Names appear for metropolises from zoom 1.2, cities from 2, towns from 4 and capitals from 2.
  - The history panel counts settlements by tier and townspeople. The inspector shows rural and urban people and "Settlements here".

# U.2 — The realm panel

**Feature / status:** the second slice of milestone U (the plan is in the [U.1 brief](u-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** VISION.md's "Civ panel" has four tabs (Overview, Diplomacy, History, Knowledge); the game adds Economy. U.1 shows only a realm's summary, so most of what M1–M5 simulate is still reachable only in the lab's inspector. This slice brings it into the game, in words a player can follow: every figure is the simulation's state, and every decision comes with its reasons.

**Observable outcome:**
- **The realm panel** opens when a civilization's land is selected or a great power is chosen. A tribe's land shows the tribe's smaller panel. Its tabs:
  - **Overview:**
    - the realm's name, era, capital, faith, culture, people, regions and treasury;
    - why it acts: its last decision in words, with its reasons and what held it back, and its needs and values as bars;
    - its realm at a glance: settlements by size, its largest cities, its wonders, its stability (mean, and regions in unrest), land let go unkept, and the cultures and faiths of its people.
  - **Diplomacy:** each people it knows, with:
    - its standing (allied, pact, trade partner, rival, neutral) and since when;
    - the opinion and its reasons;
    - the treaties and joint roads between them.
  - **Economy:**
    - taxes, revenue (trades, farms, mines and quarries, trade agreements) and costs (administration, services, upkeep, roads), with the balance and any arrears;
    - each resource against what its techs demand, with shortfalls marked;
    - its trade agreements;
    - its buildings and roads under construction.
  - **Knowledge:**
    - research now: the tech, its progress, why it was chosen and what speeds it;
    - the techs it knows, by era;
    - what it believes of each people it knows (their people and strength) beside the truth;
    - how much of the world it knows.
  - **History:**
    - its origin (the people it descends from, the year it settled);
    - its own chronicle, newest first, in the peoples' names with each event's causes.
- **The region panel:** selecting land shows the region beside the realm. It shows the land and its people (culture, faith, values), its settlements with their sizes, buildings and recent events, the harvest, cultivated land, and its stability with what lowers it.

- **Browser:**

## Decisions and defaults

- **The frame** (protocol 34). The inspected polity gains:
  - **`realm`** (civilizations only; null for a tribe):
    - its living settlements by tier, and its six largest (by townspeople);
    - the wonders standing in its cities;
    - its regions' mean stability, and how many are in unrest or let go unkept;
    - the road edges it keeps;
    - its four largest cultures and faiths, as shares of its people.
  - **`history`:** up to 40 entries of the notable events it acts in, newest first.
    - **Notable** means importance 0.05 or more. Each village founded, town grown, small building or road is left to the settlements in the region panel; with them, a large realm's list was nothing but towns growing.
    - **Runs:** events of one type, each within 25 years of the next, are one entry: the newest event, how many more there were and the year the run began ("544–574: The Taewai take in the Taewaitaer band… And 11 more like it since year 544."). Without this, bands taken in filled 30 of 40 entries (the review), and the list covered only a few decades.
    - **The scan** runs backward from the newest event, stopping at the polity's founding, after 60,000 events, or where a 41st entry would begin.
    - **Names:** the worker names its actors with the frame's other names.
  - **`unmet`** (the god view; VISION.md "Civ panel"): the nearest peoples within three regions of a civilization's land, by land, that it has not met yet, nearest first (at most 6). The Diplomacy tab lists them greyed under the peoples it knows: who is about to meet whom.
  - **`knownEras`:** each known tech's era, so techs can be listed by era.
  - **The truth beside each belief:** `truePeople` and `trueStrength` on each diplomacy row. The observer sees both.
  - **Ids where names may repeat:** each city carries its settlement id and each trade agreement its partner's id (names are not unique). The realm also counts its road edges let go unkept.
  - **The parser checks** that each known tech has an era, and that the history is newest first and no later than the frame.
- **Shared labels** (`src/observer/labels.ts`): the words for decisions, actions, needs, beliefs, opinion's reasons, standings and treaties, with `decisionTarget`, `causeText` and `units`. They moved out of the lab, which now uses them too, so the game and the lab name things alike. `factorName` turns a cause's code into words ("landPressure" → "land pressure").
- **The panel** (`src/game/RealmPanel.tsx`) replaces U.1's realm summary in the place panel.
  - **Above the tabs:** a line naming the land (region, biome, the people of the realm living there), then the realm's name, kind, era, capital, faith, culture, people, regions and treasury.
  - **The tabs:**
    - a civilization has Overview, Diplomacy, Economy, Knowledge, History and Region;
    - a tribe has no treaties or treasury, so it has Overview, Knowledge, History and Region.
  - **The tab** is kept while other land is chosen, so realms can be compared. While newly chosen land is read, the panel keeps the last realm, dimmed, so it neither flickers nor loses keyboard focus.
  - **The close button** stays in view; the panel's content scrolls under it.
  - **Keyboard and screen readers:** the tabs follow the ARIA tabs pattern (arrow keys, Home and End), and stay in view while the panel scrolls.
  - **Land with no one** shows the region alone.
- **Diplomacy:**
  - the standing is a badge with its year; the treaties are listed only where they add to the standing (a pact is not repeated);
  - the trade between them is shown in the partner's row.
- **Layout:** the place panel is 420 px wide, so the six tabs fit on one row; the ticker starts beside it. In narrow windows the panel reaches down to the ticker.

## Review

The independent review (an agent) read the slice and measured a frame at year 650 (223 KB of the 1 MB limit, history 13 KB). It confirmed the realm counts against the invariants (settlements stand only in their owner's regions; one group per polity and region) and that the moved labels are byte-identical in the lab. Its findings, all resolved:
1. **The History tab was mostly one event (material):** 30 of the leading realm's 40 entries were bands taken in. Fixed with runs (above); the unit test checks them against the chronicle.
2. **The new parser rules had no tests (material).** A frame built from a real inspected realm now passes the contract, and frames with a missing era, a history out of order, an event beyond the history or the frame, a run beginning after its newest event, or too few tiers are refused.
3. **Smaller fixes:**
   - **Keys and matching by id:** cities are keyed by settlement id, and trade is matched to a people by id (names repeat).
   - **Roads:** the count now says how many of its roads go unkept.
   - **Tabs:**
     - the tab panel takes focus;
     - arrow keys with a modifier are left to the browser;
     - the panel no longer unmounts between selections.
   - **Layout:** the close button no longer scrolls away.
   - **Wording:**
     - "since year";
     - "has met one other living people";
     - a missing lineage reads "Of unknown descent";
     - the ticker uses the shared factor names.
   - **Tests:**
     - **Browser:** the history is non-empty, with the standing's badge, the treasury and the End key;
     - **Unit:** wonders by city, and unkept roads;
     - **Worker:** a reset starts a realm's history again.
4. **Left as is:** between 1,101 and about 1,200 px wide the lens row wraps to two lines in the narrower ticker; it stays usable.

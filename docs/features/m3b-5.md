# M3b.5 — Roads and bridges

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3b, slice 5. Status: verified (6 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run; M3b continues with M3b.6.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Roads link the realm.** Once a civilization knows the Wheel, it builds roads from its capital to its towns and cities, along the cheapest route through its own land.
- **Roads improve with knowledge.** With Engineering it paves them and bridges the rivers they cross. Railways and highways follow with later techs.
- **Roads extend governance reach.** Each road edge lowers travel cost, and a bridge removes the cost of crossing the river, so far towns become easier to govern (stability).
- **Roads wear without upkeep.** Unpaid upkeep wears them down, and roads in land nobody holds crumble.
- **The chronicle records each road** with its reasons ("The Ora build a road from Kesh to Tal, with 2 bridges").
- **The map draws roads as lines by tier,** with bridges marked.

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md "Infrastructure" (roads, bridges), "Destruction" (neglect), the M3b scope ("roads and bridges inside civs") and acceptance:
- "By year 1,500, in every seed, at least 80% of the civs that have 5 or more regions, know Wheel and have at least one town or city have a road from the capital to at least half of their towns and cities."
- "At least one bridge exists in every seed within 200 years of the first civ learning Engineering."
- "Every building, road and wonder event records its causes."

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Road tiers as data** (`roads.ts`), each unlocked by the tech that names it:

  | Tier | Unlocked by | Cost per travel-km | Travel-km built a month | Travel cost left | Bridges rivers |
  | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
  | road | Wheel | 4 | 100 | 75% | no |
  | paved road | Engineering | 12 | 40 | 50% | yes |
  | railway | Railways | 60 | 30 | 25% | yes |
  | highway | Combustion engine | 100 | 30 | 20% | yes |

  - Railways and highways are data only: no study seed reaches them by year 3,000.
  - **Bridges:** 2,000 over a stream, 6,000 over a river, 15,000 over a great river, plus 12 months.
  - Startup validation checks that each tier has an unlocking tech, that costs rise and travel cost falls, and that the names match the contract's `ROAD_TIER_NAMES`.
- **A road lies on a land edge between two regions** (`state.roads`, keyed by edge). It records its tier, bridge, condition, builder, the year built and its upkeep a year (2% of what its tier and bridge cost to build on that edge).
  - A road on an edge costs `travelKm × (its tier's share + the river's surcharge unless bridged)` to travel instead of `travelKm × (1 + surcharge)`.
  - Travel from the capital uses this, so roads extend governance reach (stability) and expansion's reach.
  - Any polity travels any road; borders matter from M5's road agreements.
- **Routes** (`roadRoutes`):
  - the cheapest travel under today's roads, from the capital through the civilization's own land, to the largest town or city of each of its regions;
  - the work is the edges below the best tier it knows, or lacking a bridge where that tier bridges rivers, that none of its works at that tier or better already builds;
  - new roads follow existing ones where those are cheaper (after the review, roads that already serve count a tenth of their cost, so new roads branch off the network);
  - towns and cities in the capital's own region, or out of reach by land, get none.
  - VISION.md's "cities to each other" waits for trade (M5): before trade, a road between two cities has nothing to do, since governance reach is measured from the capital.
- **The choice** (`decisions/build.ts` `bestRoad`, a Build option):
  - each candidate town's need is connection (0.2 + 0.3 × Openness) + remoteness (0.8 × min(1, its travel-km from the capital ÷ governance reach)), at most 1, × size (townspeople ÷ 8,000, at most 1);
  - score = 0.5 × the mean of the best batch (as for buildings: one per 8 regions, at most 12) − the cost and upkeep burdens as for buildings;
  - the causes cite connection and remoteness. Paving with Engineering is weighed the same way as building anew.
- **Construction:**
  - a road is begun to each town of the batch; each is a work paid in equal monthly instalments;
  - on completion its edges take the tier (and bridges where it crosses a river), and the event "The Ora build a paved road from Kesh to Tal, with a bridge" carries the decision's causes;
  - a work ends if its town falls to ruin or leaves the civilization, or any region on the way leaves its land;
  - a union passes works on; a civilization that dies out abandons them.
- **Upkeep and wear:**
  - the civilization holding a road's first region keeps it (else the holder of its second);
  - its upkeep joins the keeper's other upkeep, and the road wears by the share left unpaid (1 ÷ 240 of its condition a month at nothing paid) and mends when paid;
  - a road no civilization keeps wears at the full rate, so an abandoned network crumbles over about 20 years;
  - a road worn away is lost, with an event ("infrastructure destroyed") citing unpaid upkeep or that nobody keeps it.
- **Contract** (protocol 15, rules 12):
  - frames carry the standing roads as parallel arrays (the two regions, tier, bridge), validated to be on this world's regions, lower id first, each edge once;
  - the inspector's wealth line counts roads under way;
  - decision logs carry `pick`.
- **Lab:**
  - roads are drawn under the markers, between the main places of the regions they join (the capital, else the largest settlement, else the region's centre), by tier: dashed brown tracks, solid dark paved roads;
  - bridges are small light squares once a cell is 3 pixels wide;
  - the canvas reports `data-road-marks` and `data-bridge-marks`.
- **Shared heap:** the travel search's binary heap moved to `heap.ts`, shared with the route search. It is the same algorithm, so earlier results are unchanged.
- **M3b.4 review fixes:** recorded in that brief.

- **The first bridge comes 5–7 years after the first civilization learns Engineering** (acceptance: within 200).
  - Chronicle and Elsewhere reach no Engineering by 3,000, so the line holds there without a bridge.
  - Chronicle reached Engineering in 2633 in a run before the M3b.4 review fixes; its history diverges after them.
- **Every road event cites its causes:** no road event is without them. A typical one: "The Namae build a paved road from Nemerk to Heifeisoux, with 2 bridges", citing remoteness 0.36 and connection 0.14.
- **No road was lost.** No civilization collapsed and none left its upkeep unpaid; the unit tests show roads wearing away.
- **Gates hold:**
  - M1: at most 11 months above 1.1× capacity, no silent bands;
  - M2: growth 4.0–5.5×;
  - M3: 20–28 civilizations at 1,000; border ratio against civilization land 1.05–1.22;
  - M3b.1: 87–93% of settlements by water;
  - M3b.4: 3–7 wonders by 2,500.
- **Speed:** 57–78 ms per simulated year with invariants in a run of the same road code before the review fixes (M3b.4: 52–75). The final run shared the machine with the complete check and measured 75–105.
- **Decision mix:** unchanged from M3b.4. Late in a full world, Do nothing is 96–98% of decision steps, as before; Build is 0.5–2.5%.
- **Known gaps:**
  - roads come within years of the Wheel, because treasuries are vast (sinks arrive with M5 and M6);
  - "cities to each other" waits for trade;
  - road lines can cross other marks at world zoom.

## Independent review (after the commit)

One general-purpose agent reviewed `093ba81`.
- **No blockers.** Its probes passed with invariants on every tick: Chronicle to 1,500 and Verdant to 2,250, Engineering forced on a civilization with roads under way, and a forced union with roads and works.
- **Determinism:** two Atlas runs gave the same hash.
- **Wealth accounting:** road instalments and upkeep are exact.
- **The M3b.4 fixes** are all present.

**Its findings, fixed with M3b.6:**
- **Should-fix: the network was not a tree.** About 10% of road edges closed loops (40 of 377 in Atlas at 1,420). Towns already joined to the capital by road were offered again as shortcuts, scored as if unconnected, because building a road changes which paths are cheapest.
  - Now towns already joined at the tier (bridged where it bridges rivers) through the civilization's own land are not offered.
  - Routes are laid with roads that already serve, or are being built, counting a tenth of their travel cost, so new roads branch off the network.
- **Should-fix: a mass loss of roads would flood the chronicle,** one event per edge in the same month (the frame carries only 200). Roads lost in a month are now one event per keeper, or for the unkept, with their number ("The Ora can no longer keep up their roads: 12 stretches, from region 1234 on, are lost").
- **Should-fix: the coverage measure was looser than VISION.md's wording.** It counted the capital as a town reached and any road tier as knowing the Wheel. It now leaves the capital out of both counts and tests for the Wheel. The reviewer's recount found coverage still 100% at every checkpoint.
- **Minor:**
  - lower-tier roads still under way are abandoned where a higher-tier road begins on the same edges, instead of being paid for and finished beside it;
  - work that waits below its tier is given up after 20 years (a wonder holding its type for the world, a building its region's slot), with an event for a wonder;
  - travel from the capital counts roads only where the civilization sees both ends (roads in land it only remembers are not in its snapshot);
  - roads crossing the world's seam are drawn from one copy further on each side;
  - ARCHITECTURE's contract notes (protocol and frame fields) and README's bridge wording are corrected.
- **New tests:**
  - branching off the network;
  - towns already joined are not offered;
  - the keeper falls back to the second region's holder;
  - the union passes road works;
  - a dying civilization abandons them;
  - tamper cases for road works, unpaid road upkeep and a wonder's end cause;
  - the browser decision heading accepts Build.
- **Noted, not changed:**
  - **A rare free road.** A road that wears away while a work to improve it is under way is laid again at completion for the improvement's price.
  - **Two reach effects.** The Wheel's reach multiplier (carts on any track) and roads' cheaper edges are both VISION.md's "reach grows with roads, transport…", and both are kept.

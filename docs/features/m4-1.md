# M4.1 — Living cultures

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M4 (culture and religion), slice 1. Status: verified, awaiting user review.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Cultures live on their people.** Each region's people hold their own values (the five sliders).
  - Year by year the values drift with the land and life there (VISION.md "Drift"): harsh land toward Tradition, a frontier with other peoples toward Militarism, towns and a sea they sail toward Openness, the memory of hunger toward Zeal, crowding toward Expansionism.
  - They blend toward the peoples in contact with them (VISION.md "Influence"): their neighbours, a more prestigious people more strongly (size, era, wonders), another polity's people less, and in a civilization its heartland, the more the nearer.
  - Each culture also goes its own way: its people take a small common step each year.
  - A culture's values are its people's mean; a realm's decisions follow its ruling culture, the culture of its heartland's people.
- **Cultures split where their people grow apart** (VISION.md "Splitting"). The far parts of a large realm, under other skies and far from the court, drift from the heartland. Where at least three neighbouring regions' people differ enough from their culture's heart, they become a daughter culture (an event, with how they differ: "with more tradition and less openness than their forebears").
- **Names and colours descend.** A daughter culture's language changes a couple of sounds and its name keeps its parent's first syllable (the Vaelor become the Vaelin); its colour on the map lies near its parent's. Bands that break away from their tribe are daughters too, named the same way.
- **The map and inspector show it:**
  - a "By culture" peoples lens colours each region by its people's culture, with a legend of the largest cultures and their parents;
  - the region inspector names the people's culture, how and when it began, its descent from the starting peoples in percentages (summing to 100%), its line of descent, and the values of the people there and of the whole culture.
- **Foreign rule follows culture.** A region whose people split from their realm's culture is now under foreign rule, which unsettles it by how far they differ (M3's existing rule).

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md "Culture and lineage" (drift, influence, splitting, ancestry query), "Personality and the decision model" (layer 1), "Naming", milestone M4.

**Not in this slice:** mixed peoples in one region, assimilation and hybrids (M4.2); traits (M4.2); religion (M4.3–M4.4); effective values from rulers and government, and polity values as the mean of all its people (M5).

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Tests:**
  - generated daughter names share their parent's first syllable, and a daughter language changes only a few sounds;
  - ancestry returns each starting people's share in a culture, summing to 100%, through splits and hybrids, and the line follows the heaviest parent;
  - conditions pull values as described; a group blends toward its neighbours and heartland, and a prestigious neighbour pulls harder;
  - part of a culture grown apart over three or more regions splits off as a daughter (parent link, descended name, near colour, event with its differences); two regions do not; the realm's ruling culture stays its heartland's;
  - a culture with nobody left dies and stays in history;
  - invariants: every living group's culture is alive and its values within 0–1; a polity's ruling culture is its heartland's; parents' weights sum to 1 and come earlier.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):**
  - M4: at least one culture split from a civilization's people by year 2,000 in every seed;
  - cultures stay distinct (no collapse of the spread of values between cultures), and splits stay bounded;
  - M1–M3c gates hold.
- **Browser:** the culture lens colours regions and lists cultures; the inspector shows the people's culture and descent; the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **One culture per region's people.** Since M1 a region holds one population group (a band, later a village), so a group has one culture; VISION.md's groups per culture within a region (minorities) are left out. M4.2 makes assimilation and hybrids work on whole groups within a realm.
- **Values live on the people** (`PopulationGroup.values`). A culture's values are its people's mean, refreshed yearly, and frozen when nobody of it is left (`deathTick`). Decisions keep reading the ruling culture's values until M5 builds effective values from all of a polity's people, its ruler and its government.
- **Ruling culture:** a polity's `culture` is its heartland group's (an invariant). When the heartland moves, so does the ruling culture.
- **Drift** (`CULTURE_PULLS`, `CULTURE_TUNING.driftRate` 0.005 a year): each value moves toward base + 0.6 × a measure of the group's conditions, kept within 0–1. The bases put each value's mean near 0.5, where the starting peoples' values centre.
  - Tradition: harsh land (base 0.32), how far the region feeds fewer farmers and herders per km² than the median habitable region (`state.harshness`, static).
  - Militarism: frontier (base 0.45), the share of neighbouring regions where other peoples live.
  - Openness: towns and sea (base 0.42), half (`seaShare`) for a coast its people can sail from, half for townspeople (full at a quarter of its people).
  - Zeal: hardship (base 0.45), the memory of hunger.
  - Expansionism: open land (base 0.48), the share of neighbouring regions that could feed people where nobody lives. Crowding was tried first, but it is near 1 wherever people live at their land's capacity, so it pulled everyone the same way.
  - Long wars and prosperous peace (VISION.md's experience pulls) arrive with M6.
- **Influence** (`influenceRate` 0.03 a year), toward the weighted mean of the following (scaled down while the weights add up to less than 1, so a lone weak contact pulls less):
  - neighbouring groups: 1 for its own polity's, `foreignContact` 0.25 for another's;
  - times prestige, 2P ÷ (P + P′), with P = √people × (1 + 0.25 × era) × (1 + 0.5 × standing wonders) × (1 + 0.1 × cities and metropolises) × (1 + 0.5 × treasury per person ÷ 0.2, at most 1). These are VISION.md's size, wealth, tech, great cities and wonders; military victories come with M6;
  - times homophily, 1 − difference ÷ `confidence` 0.25 (none beyond it). This follows Axelrod's model of cultural dissemination and bounded-confidence opinion models: peoples far apart hardly sway each other. Without it every culture would blend into one;
  - and in a civilization its heartland group at `heartPull` 2 × e^(−remoteness).
- **A culture's own way:** each year its people take a common random step of up to `fashion` 0.03 in each value (VISION.md's "small random walk").
  - Against the drift's mean reversion this keeps cultures about as distinct as the starting peoples were.
  - The independent review noted that much of the spread between cultures is this walk rather than land and life. The environment sets where values are pulled; the walk is each people's own history. Rulers (M5, M7) are meant to add the next layer of variety, and this is to be revisited there.
- **Schedule:** groups take their culture year by id (so a band that moves still has one a year), cultures by id.
- **Splitting** (`splitOff`):
  - the heart is the heartland group of the polity the culture rules with the most of its people, or else its largest group;
  - groups more than `splitDivergence` 0.07 from the heart (mean absolute difference over the five values), in at least `splitRegions` 3 neighbouring regions, split off;
  - the chance is `splitRate` 0.05 a year × how far beyond (in units of the threshold, at most 1), at most one split per culture a year, larger parts first;
  - the daughter's values are the part's mean, and the parent's are its remaining people's again at once;
  - the event names the values it has more and less of than the heart (by at least `splitNote`, a quarter of the threshold);
  - a split is counted as one from a settled civilization's culture (M4's criterion) when the parent rules a living civilization.
- **Names and colours:**
  - a daughter language replaces `languageChanges` 2 sounds of its parent's with sounds the parent lacked;
  - a daughter's name is its parent's first syllable (opening consonants, first vowels and the next consonant) plus a new ending in the daughter language, never a name another culture has. The review found 79–123 duplicated names per seed, for example seven cultures called Seki, before this rule;
  - its hue turns by up to `hueShift` 20°;
  - starting peoples take the hue of their lineage colour (`hueStart` and `hueStep`, as the observer's lineage palette);
  - the map squeezes the hue circle evenly around the sea blues, so a daughter's colour stays near its parent's.
  - Bands that break away now get daughter cultures named this way (before, they kept their parent's language unchanged and drew a fresh name).
- **Ancestry** (`ancestry`): shares pass down from the newest ancestor to the starting peoples, so they sum to 1 for any culture. `lineOf` follows the heaviest parent.
- **Calibration** (Chronicle and Elsewhere, to year 2,000, an in-process harness over settings):
  1. **First settings** (drift 0.0025, influence 0.05, threshold 0.12): cultures stayed uniform inside (mean divergence from their heart about 0.01), and 0–1 cultures split in 3,000 years.
  2. **Stronger drift and a lower threshold:** 5–15 splits from civilizations' cultures by 2,000. But the spread of values between cultures fell from about 0.08 to 0.04, and the means sank (Militarism and Expansionism toward 0.2–0.3), so peoples became alike.
  3. **Weaker cross-border pull** (0.25), **homophily** at 0.25 and a common step of 0.02: 47–48 splits from civilizations' cultures by 2,000 (most while realms grow, between 750 and 1,250, then a few a century), and a spread of about 0.09–0.10. In the five-seed study, though:
     - Elsewhere's border ratio at 1,000 fell to 0.994, below M3's gate;
     - Atlas's largest people held 45% of the world for 1,878 years.

     The value means had sunk to 0.35–0.45 (the targets' bases sat low), and neighbours had grown more alike. Lower Tradition and Expansionism and higher similarity raise union scores, and unions rose about 25%, which fed Atlas's consolidation.
  4. **Kept:** the targets centred so each value's mean stays near 0.5, where the starting peoples' values centre, and a common step of 0.03, so peoples stay as distinct as before (a spread of about 0.13). A weaker cross-border pull alone (0.1) did not stop Atlas's consolidation.
  - The validation checks each pull's base within 0–1 and its weights within ±1; targets are kept within 0–1.
- **Contract:** protocol 22.
  - The frame lists living cultures (id, name, hue, parent, regions, people) and each marker's culture.
  - The inspector's `people` gives the region's culture, its origin, founding tick, descent, line and values, and where and how many live by it now (counted from the groups, as the map is).
  - The `cultureSplit` event carries the daughter, parent, regions, people, the polity holding most of them, and the values it has more and less of.
  - Frame parsing checks that the culture list matches the markers.
- **Rules:** 20.
- **Test fixture change:** the M2 test's expansion exercise relied on a tribe beside a civilization happening to have free land to move to. The land is now full before civilizations arise (the study's "bands moved on" has been 0 by year 1,000 since M3), so the exercise now makes free land: a band of another tribe beside it dies out between months. Its old-capital check also allows the ruins being resettled the same month.

## Review (independent agent, 7 October 2026) and fixes

- **No blocking defect.** Determinism, the ruling culture through every way groups change hands, splitting, ancestry and the contract checks hold. The M2 test's changes were found honest: the constructed free land is a real die-out between months, and the old-capital check still needs the ruins resettled.
- **Fixed:**
  1. **Medium: culture names collided** (79–123 duplicated names per seed; seven cultures called Seki in Chronicle). New names avoid every name in use, with a longer ending as a last resort. The rerun has none (tested with sixty daughters of one people).
  2. **Medium: the inspector showed a new breakaway culture in "0 regions (0 people)"** until its first yearly refresh. It now counts where and how many live by the culture from the groups, as the map does (checked in the browser).
  3. **Medium: the brief did not match the code** (bases, step, figures). It is corrected above and below.
  4. **Medium: untested claims.** New tests cover:
     - the heartland's pull weakening with distance;
     - hardship pulling Zeal;
     - another polity's people pulling less;
     - a split that takes another polity's heartland, making the daughter that polity's ruling culture;
     - the parent counted without the part that left;
     - a culture that had people dying;
     - the split event's text.
     `rehome` keeping the ruling culture its new heartland's is held by the invariant in every full run (the M2 test moves a capital).
  5. **Low:**
     - prestige counts great cities and wealth, as VISION.md lists;
     - a parent's values are refreshed at once after a split;
     - the remaining inline numbers are tunables;
     - the state hash covers culture names, languages, parents, origin and founding;
     - the map's culture colours squeeze the hue circle evenly around the sea blues, so daughters stay near their parents;
     - the split count follows M4's wording (the parent rules a living civilization);
     - groups take their culture year by id;
     - contract comments are fixed.
- **Recorded, not changed:**
  - **Distinctness is partly random:** much of the spread between cultures is each culture's random walk (see Decisions).
  - **Cultures stop dying:** none died in 3,000 years, because nothing yet takes a people's culture away. M4.2's assimilation will.
  - **Decisions follow the ruling culture's values,** although about 60% of civilizations' people are of other cultures (M5).
  - **Unions rose** with the changed values (Chronicle 35 → 42, Aster 46 → 64 by 3,000).

- **M4 acceptance (splits):** every seed has a culture split from a settled civilization's culture by 2,000 (8–20 such splits; the first between 627 and 944).
  - Most splits are of tribes' cultures and come while peoples spread and realms grow (by 1,500); after 2,000 there are fewer than one a century per seed.
  - Daughters keep their parents' first syllable, and no two cultures share a name.
- **Peoples stay distinct:** the spread of values between cultures holds at 0.12–0.16 from 1,000 to 3,000, and each value's mean stays near 0.5.
- **Foreign rule is common:** 51–66% of civilizations' people are not of their realm's ruling culture, at 1,000–3,000. Bands taken in keep their own culture, and splits add far provinces. M4.2's assimilation is meant to bring this down.
- **Gates hold in every seed:**
  - **M3 borders:** the ratio at 1,000 is 1.056–1.141; civilizations at 1,000 number 19–29.
  - **Dominance:** the largest share is at most 32.5% from 1,000 on, with runs above 35% of 0–102 years.
  - **M1:** silent band-years 0, and the longest stay above 1.1× capacity is 2–10 months.
  - **M2:** growth ×4.2–5.6, and Agriculture invented independently 27–41 times by 1,000.
  - **M3b:**
    - settlements by water or a site 88–93%;
    - 3–7 wonders by 2,500;
    - road coverage 86–100% at 1,500;
    - bridges within 2–4 years of Engineering where it is reached (Chronicle and Elsewhere do not reach it by 3,000);
    - fields regrew in every seed.
  - **M3c:**
    - treasuries 1.2–1.9 years of costs;
    - at most 4 tier changes of one settlement in any 100 years, with 0–2 settlements changing more than 3 times;
    - famine kills less in rich realms at 2,000 and 3,000.
- **Eras** drift with the changed history: the leader reaches the Classical era at 1,667–2,425 (Elsewhere's 2,425 is late, as it was at 2,341 in M3c.5).
- **Speed:** 98–145 ms per simulated year (M3c.5: 79–121).

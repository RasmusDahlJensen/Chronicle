Status: verified (2 October 2026), committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run. M3's civilization count now holds. **One M3 criterion still fails, the border ratio**, so `civ-m3` is not tagged; stopped for the user's decision (below), and in any case for their approval before M3b.

**Observable outcome:**
- At its decision step a civilization next to a larger civilization it knows can choose to unite with it. Kinship, a much larger, better-fed and more stable neighbour, its own hunger or unrest, and an easy crossing make that likelier. Its Tradition, Expansionism and its own contentment make it less likely, and so do mountains or great rivers between them.
- The larger civilization accepts only land it can govern from its capital.
- On union, the smaller civilization's regions and villages become the larger one's, its capital becomes an ordinary village, and its people keep their culture. The chronicle records "The X join the Y" with the reasons.
- Over the centuries after settling, neighbouring colours merge into countries, mostly within one people and over easy land, so borders come to follow barriers.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Unite** (`decisions/choose.ts`, `UNITE_TUNING`):
  - The view lists every civilization it has met whose land touches its own: kinship, culture similarity, the regions it knows that civilization holds, how well fed and stable that civilization's people in sight are, the median crossing between their lands, and its own region on the cheapest crossing.
  - Pull = 0.16 if kin + 0.08 × similarity + 0.22 × min(1, log10(their known regions ÷ its own) ÷ 1.5) + 0.03 × fed + 0.03 × stability + 0.25 × min(1, its hunger + its share of regions in unrest).
  - Resistance = 0.06 × Tradition + 0.06 × Expansionism + 0.1 × its own mean stability + 0.25 × the median crossing's travel-km ÷ 1,000.
  - A neighbour no larger (as far as it knows) cannot be joined (−∞).
  - Unite competes with Expand, Explore and Do nothing at the same step.
- **Union** (`expansion.ts`, `unite`):
  - The larger civilization admits with chance 1 ÷ (1 + (travel-km from its capital to where they meet ÷ its reach)⁴). It refuses if it is gone or no larger in fact.
  - Every region, its people and village pass to it; the smaller capital becomes an ordinary village; the maps merge; the smaller civilization ends.
  - The event records both, the regions and people, kinship and the larger civilization's size. Nothing changes the people's culture, so far-flung or foreign regions are less stable.
- **Peoples part along barriers** (to bring borders onto mountains and rivers):
  - A split's breakaway distance is the travel-km from the heartland through the tribe's land (`BAND_TUNING.travelDistance`, reachKm 4,000 travel-km), not straight-line km. The pure `breakawayOdds` holds the grading.
  - Splits and moves weigh a crossing by min(1, (500 ÷ its travel-km)²): bands prefer easy land, so each side of a range is filled from its own side.
  - Joining and uniting weigh the median crossing between two lands (`JOIN_TUNING.crossing` 0.4, `UNITE_TUNING.crossing` 0.25 per 1,000 travel-km), so a range with one pass still divides.
- **Where a discovery happens:** where the polity's people live in the conditions that drew it to the tech (the most populous of its regions with one of the tech's environment affinities), else its heartland. The same rule serves the record of firsts and the event, fixed in advance. With larger tribes the heartland often lay off the fertile valley where the farming happened, so Chronicle's first Agriculture fell outside the top quartile.
- **Tech costs:** Bronze-and-later costs × 4 again (× 32 in all against M2). The few large countries pool many specialists; this brings the eras back to VISION.md's targets.
- **Contract** (protocol 9): actions include unite; decision options carry a label (the neighbour's name); event types append unification, independence movement, referendum and dissolution, with templates (the last three for M7).

**The border ratio:**
- Only 24% of land edges cost at least 1.5× the median to cross (Chronicle and Aster). For the median border edge to reach 1.5×, more than half of all border edges would have to come from that roughest quarter: borders almost entirely on mountains and great rivers.
- Attempts and their ratios at 1,000:
  - crossing-averse joining and uniting (0.25–0.8 per 1,000 km): 1.15–1.32;
  - the median crossing instead of the cheapest: 1.17–1.31;
  - travel-km breakaways: 1.13–1.33;
  - terrain-averse spreading: 1.19–1.38;
  - all combined: 1.05–1.28.
- In peaceful M3, borders are where growing peoples happened to meet. Two large countries never join each other, and the land between them was settled long before the countries formed. Borders settle onto defensible lines over centuries of war and secession (M6, M7).
- **Options for the user:**
  1. **Recommended:** move this criterion to M6, when war shifts borders onto defensible lines. M3 keeps a weaker check: borders follow barriers more than chance (ratio > 1.0 in every seed; now 1.05–1.28).
  2. A peaceful border mechanism in M3: frontier regions much closer, in travel, to a neighbouring country's capital than to their own drift to it. That is effectively early secession, which the user wants to be hard.
  3. Keep the criterion and tune harder (likely needs every crossing cost dominant over kinship and size, which would fragment the map again).

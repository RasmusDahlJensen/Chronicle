# M6.4 — Raids and confederations

**Feature / status:** the fourth slice of M6, "War and peace" (VISION.md; the plan is in the [M6.1 brief](m6-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review.

**Why:** VISION.md's "Raids": "Before state-level government, conflicts are raids: short actions that steal food or stores, burn buildings and cause casualties, without territorial change." Its M6 "Grounded in history": "threat drives consolidation: a strong or raiding neighbour raises the pull of unions among those it threatens, and herders beside rich farmers gain from raiding and from confederating." M6.1–M6.3 gave civilizations armies, wars and their ravages; the tribes beside them have so far been only neighbours to absorb. This slice lets herders and hungry tribes raid rich farmers, makes walls and armies answer, and lets raiding tribes draw their kin into confederations.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Raids:** once a year a tribe's band next to a civilization's land weighs a raid: hunger and herding push it, a rich store pulls it, and its warriors' odds against the soldiers and walls there decide how likely it is. A raid takes food (exact), may burn a building, kills on both sides (war deaths) and moves no border. Each raid is an event with its causes.
- **Walls answer:** a raided region remembers it (fading over decades); the memory calls for walls there (the Build need for defence), and its realm feels the threat: it keeps more soldiers and looks harder for shelter in a union.
- **Confederations:** a tribe that has raided lately may draw a smaller tribe of its lineage beside it into one confederation (the smaller joins whole); an event.
- **The game:** raids and confederations in the chronicle (the War kind and the Peoples kind); the Region tab says when a region was raided lately; a realm's army says when it is kept for fear of raiders.

## Acceptance

- **Tests:** a band weighs raids by hunger, herding, riches and odds (walls, soldiers and a poor store lower them; a fed band of farmers raids less); a raid moves food exactly, kills on both sides as war deaths, may burn a building, moves no border; the raid memory calls for walls (the need named raids) and raises the threat its realm feels (more soldiers, a stronger pull to a union); a confederation passes a whole tribe; the events and their words; the invariants hold each month with raids, burning and confederations forced; the frame shows a region's raid memory and an army kept for fear of raiders.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):** raids a century and where (tribes beside farmers), walls built after raids, confederations; M6.2–M6.3's health and the M5 gates still hold.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **State:** `state.raided` per region (0–1, its memory of raids); each tribe's raids lately (`raids`, halved each January); each band's herded share of its food (`herdShare`, from the herding method's own output). Metrics: raids, confederations, walls built in regions raided lately. Events: `raid` (now told in full) and `confederation` (new; protocol 44).
- **Raids** (`raids.ts` `raid`, in the war system after the ravages, before the levy; once a year per tribe band, staggered by band):
  - **The target:** of the civilization regions beside the band, the one it is likeliest to raid: chance = 0.6 × (hunger + 0.6 × its herding share) × riches × odds, at most 0.5; the herding share is herded food's share of what the band produces (farming is not herding: a band of farmers that knows no herding is pushed by hunger alone); riches = the target's store in months of food a head ÷ 24 (at most 1); odds = its warriors (people × 10% × its military multiplier) against the soldiers there × their multiplier × (1 + 2 × the walls' defence) + 0.2% of the people × the walls' defence.
  - **A raid:** takes 25% of the region's stored food (at most 6 months of food for the band; carried out and in), kills 5% × (1 − odds) of its warriors and 0.2% × odds × (1 − walls) of the region's people (war deaths), burns one of the region's buildings with a single chance of 15% × (1 − walls) (the townspeople rehoused, a harbor uncounted); no border moves. An event with its causes (hunger, herding, riches × 0.5), its words counted ("1 raider", "2 raiders").
  - **Memory:** the region's raid memory rises by 0.5 (at most 1) and halves every 10 years.
- **Walls answer:** the Build need for defence is the larger of the frontier's exposure and the raid memory (`decisions/build.ts`), named "raids" when the memory is the larger; walls can be built in a village (their smallest tier is 0), where raiders mostly come.
- **Threat drives consolidation:** a realm's raid memory, its regions' memory weighed by their people (a few raided villages of a great realm are little threat to it; a small realm raided all over is threatened), is a threat: its Threat need is at least it, its army wants soldiers for it (`armyTarget`: "for fear of raiders" when it is the larger threat, an explicit flag through the frame), and its union score weighs it like the threat of a neighbour (`unionScore`).
- **Confederations** (each January): a tribe that raided lately may, with one chance a year of 0.1 × min(1, its raids ÷ 5), draw in the largest smaller tribe of its lineage beside it; the smaller joins whole (its bands transferred, as a band joining); cited: raiding, kin × 0.5.
- **The game:** raids in the chronicle ("War and raids") and confederations ("Peoples"); the Region tab says when a region was raided lately; an army kept "for fear of raiders".
- **Rules version 34.**

## Calibration

With the fork tool (five large seeds from year 500 to 3,000):
- **Before the review** (`.chronicle/forks/m6-4-cal1`, the push then the farming-or-herding share): at a raid rate of 0.6 and confederation 0.1, 1,054–1,988 raids by 3,000, 100–158 confederations, 27–47 walls built in regions raided lately; tribes at 3,000 16–38. A fifth to a third of those raids came from farming tribes that knew no herding (the review's first finding).
- **After the review's fixes** (`.chronicle/forks/m6-4-cal2`, true herding): 321–516 raids by 3,000 (13–21 a century), 4–10 confederations, 5–32 walls built in regions raided lately, tribes at 3,000 19–48. Doubling the raid rate (1.2) doubled raids (583–988) and confederations (6–24) but put 31 more regions in unrest on average (Elsewhere +87) and lowered mean stability by 0.03: armies raised and released as raid memories rise and fade. The baseline was kept: raids are the small conflicts of herders and hungry tribes beside farmers (VISION.md's "before state-level government"), not a second war system.

## Review

An independent agent review before the study ran two seeds 3,000 years with every invariant checked monthly, and a save-ready copy at year 1,000 (equal state and chronicle hashes after 600 months). It confirmed the accounting and found nothing degenerate. Its material findings, all fixed:
1. **"Herding" was the farming-or-herding share:** a fifth to a third of raids came from farming tribes that knew no herding, citing herding. Bands now keep their herded share (`herdShare`).
2. **Walls could rarely answer raids** (they needed a town) and never cited them: walls can be built in a village, and the defence need is named raids when the memory is the larger part.
3. **The fear of raiders was out of proportion** (the most any region remembered drove the whole realm's army): the raid threat is now weighed by the people of the raided land, and the Threat need includes it.
4. **The tests did not exercise their titles:** rewritten (see Acceptance).

Its minor findings were fixed too: counted words in the raid's text; the mobilization's reason (raiders or a feared people, explicit flags; an M6.1 template condition that never fired); the cause weights in `RAID_TUNING`; one burn chance a raid and one confederation chance a year; raids "lately" halved each January; an invariant for the raid memory. Riches and hunger matter little in practice (herding leads the causes); that is calibration, and the union pull of raids shows rarely, since raided realms are seldom smaller than a neighbour. A second review of the fixes found tests that could still pass without testing their claims (a burn in the forced run, the people weighting, the confederation's set-up), the raid's wording ("1 of the Vraye die", "food for 0 people"), a region line that promised walls, and docs updated ahead of the evidence; all fixed. The townspeople are now kept within a raided region's people (safe at the tuned death rate, guarded for others), and group streams use an odd salt.

# M5.4 — Trade agreements through hubs

**Feature / status:** the fourth slice of M5 (the plan is in the [M5.1 brief](m5-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** VISION.md's "Trade": civilizations with a shortfall look for partners with a surplus, scored by relations, route distance, hubs and the partner's Openness. A trade agreement moves a fixed amount a year in exchange for another resource or wealth, and lasts until the need passes, relations sour or a party is gone (or, from M6, war). Agreements are contact edges: they raise relations, share knowledge as an exchange does and carry religious influence. They are drawn on the trade-routes lens. M5.3 made shortages real; this slice lets them be traded away.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Proposing trade.** A civilization short of a resource weighs proposing trade at its decision step. It looks to civilizations it has met whose land it knows to hold sites of that resource, within trade reach of their hubs.
  - **What it weighs:** its shortfall, its opinion of them, their Openness, the route's distance and what it would pay. These are its beliefs, through the view.
- **Hubs and routes.** A civilization trades through its hub: its largest settlement with a market or a harbor, or else its capital.
  - **Capacity:** a route carries more the better both hubs are.
  - **Reach:** a route reaches as far as either side's trade range (the Wheel, Sailing, Cartography and Navigation raise it), and further by sea between two harbors.
- **Acceptance** is the partner's own judgement: it must have the surplus in truth, and it weighs what it gains, its opinion of the proposer and its Openness against its Tradition. A refusal is remembered.
- **Terms.** The amount is at most the shortfall, the surplus and the route's capacity. It is paid with a resource the partner lacks when the proposer has one to spare, and otherwise with wealth a year: paid monthly, exactly accounted.
- **Effects.**
  - **Imports and exports:** imports join what the importer has (M5.3's `available`); exports leave the exporter's.
  - **Relations:** trading partners grow intimate and warmer.
  - **Knowledge:** they research what the other knows faster, as an exchange does.
  - **Faith:** each one's faith draws the people of the other's hub region.
- **Ending.** An agreement ends when the importer no longer needs it, the exporter no longer has it to spare, the importer cannot pay, opinion on either side falls below −50, or a party is gone. Each beginning and ending is a chronicle event with its cause.
- **Lab:**
  - **The trade-routes layer** (under Map layers) draws a line between the two hubs of every agreement.
  - **The inspector** lists a civilization's agreements: partner, resource, amount, which way it flows and what is paid.

## Decisions and defaults

- **Hubs and routes** (`hubs.ts`, `trade.ts`).
  - **The hub:** a civilization's largest living settlement with a market or a harbor, else its capital.
  - **Capacity:** a hub carries 0.5 units a year, +1 with a market and +1 with a harbor; a route carries what the poorer hub does.
  - **Reach:** great-circle km between the hubs, up to 3,000 km × the larger side's trade range, or 6,000 km × that between two harbors. With the Wheel and Currency that is about 5,000 km by land, roughly the reach of Bronze Age tin and Classical silk routes.
- **Proposing** (the view, `tradeOptions`; the decision, `tradeScore`).
  - **What it lists:** its two largest shortfalls above 5% where it lacks at least 0.02 units a year (it knows its own lack) and sends none of the resource away itself.
  - **Partners:** civilizations it has met whose land it knows to hold the resource (a site whose deposit it knows of, or forest or rough land for timber and stone). It must believe they could spare at least 0.02.
  - **The belief:** what it believes they could spare is their true spare, off by the same seeded bias as its belief about their people and shrunk by intimacy (M5.1's rule).
    - The decision log records that people belief beside the truth, so the study's belief counts include trade.
    - A proposal that finds nothing to spare corrects the belief (intimacy rises, as a refused union's does).
    - A partner that refused it within 15 years is skipped.
  - **The route** is measured between the partners' true hubs: a people knows where its partners' great markets and harbors are.
  - **The score:** 0.4 × shortfall + 0.1 × opinion ÷ 100 + 0.1 × their culture's Openness − 0.1 × km ÷ reach − 0.5 × wealth a year ÷ its revenue. It is not an option beyond reach.
- **Acceptance** (the partner, in truth).
  - **What it spares:** half its surplus at most (`spare` 0.5), so its own growing demand does not end the agreement at once. Below 0.02 it has none to spare.
  - **Its surplus:** what it works itself beyond its demand and what it already sends. No one sends on what it imports, so exports never exceed what a civilization works (an invariant), and an agreement ending can never leave another party short.
  - **The chance:** 0.6 + 2 × gain + 0.3 × opinion ÷ 100 + 0.3 × Openness − 0.3 × Tradition, within [0, 1]. The gain is the price as a share of its income, or the share of its own barter shortfall relieved.
  - **A refusal:** remembered as `refusedTrade` (−10 opinion, half-life 15 years).
- **Terms.**
  - **The amount:** the least of the importer's lack, what the exporter spares and the route's capacity.
  - **Payment:** barter in the resource the importer spares most of that the exporter lacks; otherwise 400,000 wealth a unit a year.
  - **Wealth payments:** a twelfth of the price each month in whole units, the fraction carried. They are recorded in both treasuries' wealth ledgers (given and received).
  - **In the budget:** the budget view counts a realm's trade, net (what it is paid less what it pays). Taxes cover what imports cost, and an exporter's receipts pay part of its costs.
- **Ending.**
  - **Yearly review** (staggered by agreement id):
    - opinion below −50 on either side (relations soured);
    - the importer's shortfall without it under 5% (need passed);
    - the exporter's surplus without it below the amount (none to spare);
    - the importer's surplus of its barter goods without it below what it sends (unpaid).
  - The agreement then runs through each side's hub of the day, so the trade-routes layer never draws a hub that has fallen or changed hands.
  - **Monthly:**
    - a party gone;
    - a missed payment (unpaid);
    - no one sends more than it works: an exporter whose mines or land were lost ends its newest agreements first (none to spare), and an importer that no longer works the barter goods it sends ends that one (unpaid).
  - Resources are assessed before trade in the diplomacy system, so the check sees the same month's supply.
- **Effects.**
  - Imports join `available` and so access and multipliers; exports leave them.
  - Trading partners' intimacy target rises by 0.3 and opinion by 15.
  - A partner teaches its techs as an exchange does (`teacherOf`).
  - Each partner's state religion draws the people of the other's hub region at 0.2 (once per partner, however many agreements they share).
- **Contract and lab:**
  - protocol 29: the frame's `trades` (hub cells, resource, by sea) and the inspector's `treaties`; rules version 27.
  - The Trade routes layer draws gold land and blue sea lines.
  - The Resources section counts imports and exports.
  - The decision reads "Propose trade with the X for copper".

### Calibration

The quick check was two seeds to year 2,000.
- **First version: almost no trade.**
  - Chronicle made 3 agreements from 227 offers; Atlas made 33 from 5,249 offers, 28 of which ended "none to spare".
  - **Why it failed:**
    - proposers counted sites nobody mined;
    - proposers asked for shortfalls too small to carry;
    - the exporter gave its whole surplus, so any growth in its demand ended the agreement.
  - **The fix:** a believed spare, a minimum lack, and the spare share.
- **Reach was the main limit.**
  - At year 2,000 every listed option was beyond reach: hubs sat 2,100–6,300 km apart against a 1,500 km base. Doubling the base reach gave 12 and 19 agreements.
  - About 70% of offers were refused, so the acceptance base went from 0.3 to 0.6. Selling a surplus is usually welcome; refusal now comes from dislike and Tradition.
  - **Result:** 13 and 16 agreements by 2,000, 35–45% refused, and imports covering 9–12% of shortfalls.
  - Relations, unions and population stayed close to M5.3.
- **What still limits trade:** many short civilizations have not met anyone who holds what they lack. Voyages (M5.6) and contact will widen that.

## Review

The independent review came before the full study. It ran a 3,000-year history with invariants every tick and profiled it. Its findings, all resolved:
1. **Re-exports could drive a stock negative (material, reproduced).**
   - **How:** a civilization's surplus counted its imports, so it could send on what it bought. When the first agreement ended, it was left owing more than it had, and the invariant stopped the history.
   - **Fixed:**
     - surplus comes only from what a civilization works;
     - each month any party sending more than it works ends its newest agreements first;
     - a new invariant keeps exports at or below supply;
     - a regression test.
2. **The lab named a trade decision's target as a region.** It now reads "Propose trade with the X for copper".
3. **The brief said −30 for souring; the tunables say −50.** −50 is kept (chosen in calibration) and the brief corrected. Mean opinion between civilizations runs about −10 to −18 in these histories, so −30 would end agreements with merely cool neighbours.
4. **Smaller fixes:**
   - **Faith:** two agreements with one partner drew twice; now once.
   - **View cost:** the view finds the hub only when it lacks something.
   - **Beliefs:** a proposal that finds nothing to spare corrects the belief, and the people belief behind the spare estimate is logged.
   - **Reviews and the budget:**
     - barter goods are reviewed yearly;
     - payments are in the budget view and taxes;
     - routes run through each side's hub of the day.
   - **Tunables:** the price must be above zero.
   - **Lab text:** the toggle's note, the decision note, and "wealth a year".
   - **Tests:** a partner whose land it does not know is not listed.
5. **Checked and sound:**
   - payments and carry;
   - multiset bookkeeping of partners;
   - polity lifecycle (unions and deaths end agreements the same tick);
   - checkpoints;
   - the hash and versions;
   - determinism;
   - the view rule.
   - **Cost:** `tradeOptions` and `hubOf` together cost about 3% of a run.

## Results

The study is `.chronicle/studies/m5-4` (five seeds, 3,000 years). For iterating: two seeds to 2,000 and per-civilization diagnostics at year 2,000.
- **Trade forms in every seed by 2,000 (M5).**

  | Seed | Agreements by 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 | Active at 3,000 |
  | --- | --- | ---: |
  | Chronicle | 2 / 29 / 118 | 12 |
  | Elsewhere | 12 / 31 / 224 | 18 |
  | Atlas | 1 / 46 / 243 | 6 |
  | Verdant | 11 / 44 / 311 | 8 |
  | Aster | 13 / 43 / 298 | 23 |

  - **Refusals:** 22–32% of proposals by 3,000.
  - **Imports:** they cover 5–31% of shortfalls at 2,000 and 4–15% at 3,000.
  - **Endings by 3,000:** "none to spare" leads (78–229), as exporters' own people and techs come to need what they sold. Then need passed (10–57), unpaid (7–40), relations soured (4–44) and a party gone (4–14).
- **Relations warm a little with trade.** Mean opinion at 3,000 is −4.7 to +12.9 (M5.3: −8.4 to +9.9). 0–23% of pairs are warm.
- **Gates hold:**
  - the longest run above 35%: 78/0/0/0/102 years;
  - civilizations at 1,000: 23/32/16/21/28; at 2,000: 26/26/30/34/28;
  - unions by 3,000: 47/54/56/48/60;
  - hybrids by 2,000: 2/5/3/5/1;
  - splits from a ruling culture by 2,000: 7/17/10/13/17;
  - a religion founded by 2,500 in every seed;
  - decisions citing a belief more than half off: 11–53;
  - the causes rule: 0 short.
- **Speed:** the first full study showed the culture system twice as slow. Each trading realm's hub was looked up once per region for its faith draw. It is now looked up once per realm, with identical histories (the same state hashes).

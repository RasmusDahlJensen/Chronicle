# M6.5 — Alliances at war, coalitions and vassalage

**Feature / status:** the fifth slice of M6, "War and peace" (VISION.md; the plan is in the [M6.1 brief](m6-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review.

**Why:** VISION.md's "Alliances and coalitions": "Defensive allies are called into wars and accept based on their own scores. When one civ's power passes a share of all known power (configurable), polities that know it gain a Threat need against it, so balance-of-power coalitions emerge against would-be hegemons." Its peace terms: "Overwhelming: vassalage, or annexation if the loser is small", and the diplomatic state "tributary or vassal". M5.5's alliances so far only deterred; M6.2's wars were fought by two realms alone.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Allies called in:** when a realm is attacked, each of its allies weighs joining the war on its side (its own score: its opinion of both, the threat it feels from the attacker, the odds, its war-weariness and arrears, a war it fights already); one that joins fights beside it (its strength on the front, its own dead as war deaths, its own weariness) until the peace; one that refuses is remembered for it.
- **Coalitions against a hegemon:** a realm whose power passes a share of all the power another people knows is a threat to that people whatever it thinks of them: alliances against it and wars to cut it down become likelier, so coalitions form against would-be hegemons.
- **Vassalage:** an overwhelming victory over a realm too large to annex makes it a vassal: it pays tribute each year, declares no war of its own and is defended by its overlord, until it believes itself as strong as its overlord and throws it off (an event), or its overlord is gone.
- **The game:** allies in the Wars tab; "vassal" and "overlord" standings (the Diplomacy rows, the diplomacy lens); a realm's overlord and vassals; the chronicle: allies joining or refusing, vassalage, a vassal going free.

## Acceptance

- **Tests:** an ally weighs joining by its own score and joins or refuses (remembered); its strength counts on the front and its dead are exact war deaths; at the peace it stands down weary; the hegemon threat rises with a power share and is felt whatever the opinion; vassalage at overwhelming terms, its tribute exact, no wars of its own, its overlord defends it, its end (strength, overlord gone); the invariants hold.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):** allies joining and refusing, alliances against the largest realm, vassals; the largest realm's share over time (VISION.md: reported in M6, a limit in M7); M6.2–M6.4's health and the M5 gates still hold.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **Allies called in** (`allies.ts`, when war is declared): each ally of the defender that has met the attacker, is not allied to it too, is no vassal and fights no war with it already weighs joining: 0.1 + 0.3 × its opinion of the defender ÷ 100 − 0.2 × its opinion of the attacker ÷ 100 + 0.3 × the threat it feels from the attacker + 0.3 × (2 × the odds − 1) − 0.3 × its war-weariness − 0.3 × its arrears − 0.3 if it fights a war already − 0.2 if a pact binds it to the attacker; the odds are its strength and the defender's as it believes it against the attacker's. Above 0 it joins, citing its strongest reasons (the alliance itself among them); a vassal's overlord always joins (meeting the attacker in the war if it never had). One that joins: its treaties with the attacker end, both remember the war, it sets its army for war. One that refuses: the defender remembers being abandoned (−30, halving every 40 years). Events: `allyJoined`, `allyRefused`.
- **Allies at war** (`war.ts`): each month each ally adds half its strength × its supply to the front to the defender's side (on the defender's ground when the front is there) and loses soldiers at the defender's rate (war deaths, its own count); its weariness grows as a side's exhaustion does but for occupation. Allies are on the defender's side for every purpose: at war with the attacker (no trade, treaties, standing "at war") and in truce with it after the peace. Allies seek no separate peace: they stand down at the peace, their weariness what the war left; the peace counts their dead with the war's.
- **The odds of an attack** (`warTargets`): its own strength alone (allies join only a war on their ally), against the target's, its allies' as before, and its overlord's (who always joins).
- **Hegemons** (`perception.ts` `fearOf`): a civilization whose believed strength is more than 30% of all the strength a people knows of (its own and every living civilization it has met, at least four of them) is a threat to it whatever it thinks of them: (share − 0.3) ÷ 0.7, the larger of that and the fear it feels anyway; none from an ally. A hegemon it does not border is among its threats too, so alliances against it (M5.5's common threat) and pre-emptive wars on it become likelier: coalitions.
- **Vassalage** (`vassals.ts`; VISION.md's peace terms): a winner at 75 or more over a loser too large to annex makes it a vassal (instead of taking land) if it could hold it (`couldHold`): it is no vassal itself, the loser has at most twice its people, and the loser is not already strong enough to throw it off (else cession with tribute). The vassal pays 15% of its revenue a year to its overlord, month by month (wealth flows; its overlord's war income), declares no war (and its overlord none on it), and its overlord joins any war on it. A vassal's own vassals go free (no chains), and a vassal made another's passes to its new overlord, its old one told (`vassalFreed`). Each January a vassal whose strength reaches its overlord's as it believes it throws it off (its overlord remembers it, −30, halving every 40 years), and a vassal whose overlord is gone goes free: the event `vassalFreed`. A vassal unites with its overlord only as any realm does (an extra pull was tried and dropped: see Calibration).
- **The game:** allies in the Wars tab (who joined, their dead; a war it joined as an ally, with its side's lead and both principals' exhaustion) and the ledger's wars (sides and dead); standings "its vassal" and "its overlord" in the Diplomacy rows (which they lead) and the diplomacy lens (lavender and deep purple); the realm's overlord (since when, its tribute, all it paid) and vassals at the head of its Diplomacy; in the chronicle allies joining or refusing, a peace's vassalage and the allies beside the defender, a vassal going free, and coalitions ("whose power towers over all they know"); war stories tell the allies and a vassalage.
- **Development control** (Testing tools, not a product feature): "Make them a vassal" makes the selected realm (at peace, no vassal) the vassal of the strongest realm beside it, as at an overwhelming peace, so the UI can be seen without waiting for one.
- **Stats:** allies joined and refused, coalitions, wars ended in vassalage, vassals gone free and now, the strongest realm's share of all strength and the largest civilization's land share (VISION.md M6: reported).
- **Raid deaths** are war deaths in the ledger (their cause) but counted apart in the stats from M6.5 (`raidDeaths`), so the war table counts wars' dead.
- **Rules version 35, protocol 45.**

## Calibration

With the fork tool (five large seeds from year 500 to 3,000):
1. **First version** (`.chronicle/forks/m6-5-cal1`): 12–59 allies joined by 3,000 (refusals 0–7), 7–24 coalitions, 4–26 wars ended in vassalage, vassals at 3,000 0–3. Fewer allies (an alliance base of −0.1) or a lower hegemon share (25%) changed the counts as expected, but every variant had the same fault: in four seeds the largest realm grew far beyond M6.4's (Chronicle 808 regions at 3,000 against 425; Elsewhere 728 against 402), and unrest with it (Elsewhere 1,224 regions in unrest at 3,000, mean stability 0.52).
2. **Diagnosis:** a history traced region by region (Chronicle, year 1,200: 217 regions in unrest against M6.4's 41) showed one realm of 367 regions, taxed at 38%, holding 194 of them. It had made its 207-region neighbour its vassal in 1059, and that vassal joined it in a union in 1121, pulled by the vassal's extra union weight.
3. **Without that pull** (`.chronicle/forks/m6-5-cal2`, variant): the largest realms stayed near M6.4's (Chronicle 440, Elsewhere 408 at 3,000), and unrest fell back toward M6.4's levels in most seeds and centuries. The pull was dropped.
4. **The independent review** (below) found vassalage imposed on realms the winner could not hold (vassals up to 40 times their overlord's people, some throwing it off within a year, then the same war again): vassalage now needs a loser the winner could hold.

## Review

An independent agent review before the study ran three large seeds 2,500 years with every invariant checked monthly (no failure; no chains, no vassal of a dead overlord beyond a month, no ally of both sides), cloned two histories mid-war with an allied war, a vassal and alliances (identical state and chronicle hashes 360 months on), and found hegemony fears regional powers without making everyone fear everyone (0–43% of civilizations see one at a time). Its material findings, all fixed:
1. **The browser test's diplomacy-lens key** could never match (it listed the old eight standings): it now takes every standing the lens colours, and the standing names from the observer's labels.
2. **Vassalage on realms the winner could not hold** (above).
3. **A vassal made another's silently** (its old overlord, who fought for it, untold): told now.

Its minor findings, fixed: tests that could pass without testing their claims (a truce hid the overlord's restraint; the threshold to throw an overlord off was never tested near it; "never met the attacker" untested); the peace's dead without the allies'; attack odds counting the attacker's own allies (who never join an attack) and ignoring the target's overlord (who always does); an overlord fighting a people it had never met; "base" given as a reason on joining; a vassalage template edited though never used (restored); the overlord's colour as dark as a rival's (now darker, L* 31). Noted, not changed: refusals are rare (allies called mostly honour their alliance) and allies seldom turn a war, since an attacker counts the defender's allies before it attacks; fewer wars in the second half than M6.4 (vassals declare none, allies at war and busy realms neither): see Results.

**The border gate: what was tried.**
- **M6.2** (its brief, Calibration 1–6): goals by land value moved borders without settling them; goals chosen by position, then region by region by the frontier each would draw, raised the ratio from 1.08–1.42 (M6.1) to 1.33–1.62; a stronger frontier motive then added wars without settling more borders.
- **Diagnosis at the close-out** (Elsewhere at 3,000, the M6.5 history): 36% of civilization border edges lie on barriers (1.5 median edges or more) against 21% of all land edges, so borders do lean onto barriers, but the median needs half. Of the 359 cheap border edges, only 76 lie in open country (no barrier within two regions of either side): most could move onto a barrier close by. 279 of them lie between realms bound by a non-aggression pact or an alliance, and 100 between realms that never fought; between those that did, most wars end in tribute or a white peace, so the land seldom changes hands (1.3–2.3% of owned regions a century).
- **Three attempts from that evidence** (`.chronicle/forks/m6-border1`, five seeds from year 500 to 3,000):

  | Seed | M6.5 | Frontier motive 0.2 → 0.6 (more wars for a frontier) | Cession from a lead of 30, not 40 (more land at peace) | Unions weigh the crossing 0.25 → 0.6 (easy borders united away) |
  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | Chronicle | 1.40 | 1.49 | 1.35 | 1.40 |
  | Elsewhere | 1.29 | 1.35 | 1.35 | 1.30 |
  | Atlas | 1.96 | 1.52 | 1.50 | 1.49 |
  | Verdant | 1.33 | 1.38 | 1.38 | 1.33 |
  | Aster | 1.42 | 1.48 | 1.36 | 1.47 |

  The frontier motive comes closest but nearly doubles wars (269–366 by 3,000 against 141–205) and war deaths, and still falls short in four seeds: narrowly in Chronicle (1.49) and Aster (1.48), clearly in Elsewhere and Verdant (1.35–1.38). Cession from a lower lead moves more land without settling it on barriers. Weighing the crossing more cuts unions to a third (7–20 by 3,000) and leaves 33–41 civilizations, without raising the ratio. In every run the ratio rises slowly over the centuries (Elsewhere 1.19 at 1,500, 1.29 at 3,000) and levels off below 1.5 in two to four seeds.

# M6.1 — Armies

**Feature / status:** the first slice of M6, "War and peace" (VISION.md). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**M6's slices** (planned 8 October 2026; each brings the UI for what it adds, VISION.md U):
1. **M6.1** (this slice): armies.
   - Soldiers drawn from the people and taken from the fields.
   - An army's upkeep in the realm's budget.
   - Strength = soldiers × military techs (with their resources).
   - An army sized by threat (and from M6.2, war and its income).
   - Demobilizing unsettles the soldiers.
2. **M6.2:** war and peace.
   - Declaring war from the decision model, framed by its dominant factor.
   - Monthly fronts: war score, casualties as war deaths, occupation.
   - Exhaustion, and peace terms: white peace, tribute, cession, annexation of the small.
   - Claims and grievance.
3. **M6.3:** the ravages of war.
   - Devastation, looting, burning and razing.
   - Ruins and their resettlement.
   - Roads and bridges destroyed.
   - Refugees.
4. **M6.4:** raids and confederations: tribes raid rich farmers, walls answer, and threat drives unions and confederations.
5. **M6.5:** alliances at war and coalitions against hegemons (allies called in, balance of power), and vassalage.
6. **The M6 close-out.**

**Why this slice first:** war needs something to fight with. VISION.md: "Military strength per civ = soldiers × military tech multiplier × resource supply factor × a supply penalty". Its "Grounded in history":
- "army size follows threat and recent war income";
- "the army's upkeep (a cost in the M3c budget) pushes the realm toward arrears";
- "demobilizing unsettles the soldiers".

Building the army first (mechanics before decisions, as the user asked after two rejected slices) means M6.2's wars spend and lose real soldiers, paid from real treasuries.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Every civilization keeps an army:** soldiers drawn from its rural people (who then do not farm), more where the land holds more people.
  - **In peace:** a small garrison, more for a militaristic people and more when a stronger neighbour it dislikes threatens it.
  - **Its size** moves toward its target over months: raising soldiers takes time, and letting them go is quicker.
- **Upkeep:** each soldier costs wealth a year (more for a better-armed army), a new cost in the budget beside administration, services, upkeep and roads. Taxes rise to pay for it, and a realm that cannot pay falls into arrears as before.
- **Demobilizing** many soldiers at once unsettles the realm's regions for a few years (a new stability burden).
- **Strength:**
  - **What it is:** a realm's military strength is its soldiers times its military techs' multiplier (bronze, iron, horses, steel, gunpowder…, each counting only as far as its resources are supplied).
  - **What reads it:** beliefs about strength, threat, pacts and alliances. Strength used to be all its people times the multiplier, so it now follows armies.
- **The game:**
  - **Realm panel:** the army (soldiers, share of the people, strength, its target and why), and its cost in the Economy tab.
  - **Ledger:** the army in the table of realms.
  - **Lab:** the inspector shows the army too.
  - **Region tab:** each region's soldiers.

- **Tests:**
  - **Soldiers:** they are part of their region's people; they never exceed its rural people and work no land. Deaths and moves take them with the people (exact accounting holds).
  - **Upkeep:** it is a cost in the budget and in every wealth flow, and is paid or falls into arrears like the others.
  - **Strength:** soldiers × the military multiplier. Beliefs estimate it.
  - **The target:** it rises with threat and Militarism and falls with what the realm can afford; the army moves toward it over months.
  - **Demobilizing** adds its stability burden, which fades.

## Decisions and defaults

- **Soldiers** (`PopulationGroup.soldiers`, VISION.md "Data model": occupation shares include soldiers) are rural people under arms, counted in their group's people:
  - at most its rural people (people − townspeople); they eat from its food, but work no land: food production, farm taxes and farm labour count only the rest;
  - deaths and moves take them with the people: a group that loses people keeps at most its rural people under arms (`fitSoldiers`, each month in the war system); no separate accounting flows are needed, since soldiers never leave their group's people;
  - a tribe keeps none: its warriors hunt and herd (its strength below).
- **The army's target** (`decisions/army.ts`, pure, from `perception.ts`'s `armyView`), set at the realm's yearly assessment before its taxes, so taxes cover it:
  - **What it wants:** its guard, its people × garrison × (½ + Militarism) (more for a militaristic people), plus its people × threat × the threat it feels: the most from any neighbour it does not ally with, believed stronger than its guard and its allies' help and disliked (VISION.md "Needs": threat; `decisions/threat.ts` `threatOf`, the needs' own rule; less from a pact partner).
    - **Why the guard:** judged against the army it has, the threat flipped targets every year: soldiers raised lowered the threat, so they were let go, and the threat came back (one realm reversed 165 times in 200 years in a first run). Judged against its guard, a raised army cannot talk itself down; rivals that dislike each other arm up to what they can afford, and stand down when they no longer do (28 reversals of more than 20% in 200 years across 36 realms).
  - **What it can afford:** the soldiers its army budget pays for at a soldier's upkeep: afford × (½ + Militarism) × its customary revenue × (1 − arrears), but never more than its surplus at customary taxes plus `headroom` × that revenue. A militaristic people spends more of its revenue on arms; a realm whose administration, services and upkeep already take its revenue keeps little more than its floor rather than let its land decay to pay soldiers.
  - **The target** is the smaller of the two, never below floor × its people, with its reasons: the soldiers its Militarism, the threat (naming the people it fears) and the cost add or take away.
  - **Events:** a target rising by half or more is a mobilization, falling as far the other way (to two thirds or less) a demobilization, each by at least `eventSoldiers`. Each cites what changed since the year before: the threat, its people (its guard) or what it can afford for a mobilization; the threat passing, the cost or fewer people for a demobilization. A mobilization names the people it fears.
- **Raising and releasing** (monthly, the war system): each region's soldiers move toward its share of the target (by rural people, at most maxShare of them, in whole soldiers that sum to the target: the largest remainders take what flooring leaves): raising closes 1/raiseMonths of the gap a month (recruiting and training take time), releasing 1/releaseMonths (quick). A union sets the united realm's army at once, without an event, so it neither sends home the soldiers the union brought nor calls that a mobilization.
- **The budget beside the army:** a realm's strain (M3c: what makes it let far regions go, and weighs on expansion and building) counts its costs other than its army; and the maintenance it defers plans with the soldiers it has, not those it means to raise, so a mobilization is paid from savings while taxes catch up rather than by letting land go at once. Taxes still plan for the army it means to keep.
- **Upkeep:** each soldier costs upkeep × the military multiplier^arms wealth a year (a better-armed army costs more), a new cost (`army`) beside administration, services, upkeep and roads. It is paid monthly with them, in every wealth flow (`WealthFlows.army`), and unpaid it is arrears like the rest.
- **Demobilizing unsettles:** while a realm stands down (it keeps more soldiers than it means to), soldiers released in a region add their share of its people to its `unsettled` measure, which fades with a time constant of demobYears; the region's stability loses demobUnrest × min(1, unsettled ÷ demobScale) (a burden named "returning soldiers"). Moving soldiers between its regions as their people change is not a demobilization. A region whose people die out leaves no unsettled measure to the next.
- **Strength** (`truthOf(…, 'strength')`, read by beliefs, threat, opportunity and alliances): a civilization's soldiers × its military multiplier (its military techs, each counting only as far as its resources are supplied, M5.3); a tribe's people × warriors × its military multiplier.
- **Tunables** (`ARMY_TUNING`, validated): garrison 0.4%, threat 2%, floor 0.1%, maxShare 8% of a region's rural people, raiseMonths 12, releaseMonths 3, upkeep 1.2 a soldier-year, arms 0.5, afford 20%, headroom 10%, demobUnrest 0.2, demobScale 2%, demobYears 3, warriors 10%, eventChange 50%, eventSoldiers 300.
  - **Why these numbers:** revenue is about 0.07 wealth a person-year (M5 close-out: costs of 0.04–0.1 a person, taxes 15–20%). A garrison of 0.4–0.6% of the people then costs 7–10% of revenue; a threatened realm's 2–3% would cost 40% and more, which only a realm with a surplus can pay. In a 1,500-year run armies held 0.5–0.8% of civilizations' people (the most one kept 1–2%). Historical peacetime armies kept well under 1% of their people under arms; ancient states at war raised several per cent.
- **Frames** (protocol 41; U's close-out took 40):
  - a civilization's `army`: soldiers, target, share of its people, strength, upkeep a year, the people it fears and the target's reasons;
  - the region's soldiers (`soldiers` beside `specialists`);
  - `costs.army`, and the stability burden `demobilized`;
  - each realm's soldiers in `civList`;
  - the events `mobilization` and `demobilization` (the chronicle's "War and raids").
- **The game:** the realm panel's Overview shows the army (soldiers, share, strength, target and why); Economy lists its cost; the Region tab its soldiers here; the ledger's table of realms an Army column; the lab's inspector the same.
- **Rules version 31.**

## Study

`.chronicle/studies/m6-1` (five seeds, 3,000 years, about 9 minutes):
- **Armies:** soldiers held 0.38–0.71% of civilizations' people at year 1,000 and 0.20–0.48% at 3,000 (the most one realm kept: 0.7–2.3%); their upkeep was 6–12% of civilizations' costs at 1,000 and 3–7% at 3,000. Mobilizations · demobilizations by 3,000: 162 · 92 / 144 · 40 / 170 · 87 / 138 · 75 / 195 · 49.
- **The M5 gates hold:** trade agreements by 2,000 in every seed (11 / 19 / 15 / 28 / 43; M5's close-out 10 / 21 / 18 / 25 / 39); non-aggression pacts by 2,000 (71–113); cross-border roads by 2,000 in every seed (6 / 3 / 2 / 7 / 12; M5: 5 / 3 / 3 / 7 / 13); overseas first contacts by voyage by 3,000 in every seed with unmet peoples overseas.
- **The economy:**
  - arrears are unchanged (0–3 realms at 1,000, 2,000 and 3,000, as in M5's close-out);
  - land let go unkept at 3,000 is 9.9 / 12.9 / 5.2 / 6.7 / 5.4% (M5's close-out 6.0 / 10.6 / 4.9 / 4.2 / 3.1%): realms that pay for armies let somewhat more far land go, Elsewhere above M5's 11%. That is the cost of armies the user asked for (VISION.md M6: the army's upkeep pushes a realm toward arrears), not a defect: arrears did not rise, and on seed Invariants the review's comparison shows buildings lost and unrest back at their levels without armies;
  - buildings lost to unpaid upkeep by 3,000: 2,286 / 2,121 / 1,538 / 1,885 / 5,790 (M5: 2,682 / 2,229 / 1,808 / 1,450 / 4,035).
- **Story health:** civilizations at 3,000: 23–31; the longest run of one people above 35% of the world's: 0–102 years, as before.
- **Speed:** 128–180 ms a simulated year (M5's close-out 110–169).

## Review

The independent review (an agent) checked accounting, the target, upkeep, strength, the frame and the UI, ran a 1,500-year history with every invariant checked each tick (all held; no NaN), and compared 3,000-year histories against `civ-u` on seed Invariants and two study seeds. Its findings:
1. **The army's upkeep strained the peaceful economy (material).** On Invariants at year 3,000, unrest outbreaks went from 812 to 2,372, buildings lost from 924 to 4,324 and land let go unkept from 4.6% to 11.4%; ablations showed the upkeep, not the demobilization burden, was the cause: armies counted as empire strain (so far regions were let go), taxes above the custom, and deficits after a mobilization. Fixed: the army budget is held to the surplus plus a headroom, strain counts costs other than the army, and deferred maintenance plans with the soldiers kept. On Invariants at year 3,000: unrest outbreaks 813 (812 without armies), buildings lost 902 (924), unkept land 3.6% (4.6%), mean stability 0.861 (0.854); taxes are about 2.5 points higher (19% median against 16.4%): the price of armies.
2. **Unions churned the army (material):** the united realm's target was spread over more people, so every region sent soldiers home within months, then raised them again. Fixed: a union sets the army at once, quietly; and soldiers sent home unsettle only while a realm stands down.
3. **Minor, fixed:** event causes are shares of what moved (they reached 1.03); a region whose people die out leaves no soldiers or unsettled measure; the state hash covers each realm's army and the unsettled measure; the levy hands out whole soldiers that sum to the target (a realm of small regions could keep none); the demobilization text says what the realm stands down; the stability tunables' doc lists the burden; tests now cover dearer arms, the event thresholds and causes, the union path and the levy's sum.
4. **Minor, kept:** the security dilemma between hostile neighbours (each judging the other against its own guard) arms both up to their budgets; with the budget now binding it reads as history's arms races.

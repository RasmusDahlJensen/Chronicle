# M4.4 — Schisms and the secular age

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M4 (culture and religion), slice 4 (the last). Status: verified (7 October 2026), committed on `main` and tagged `civ-m4`, not pushed, so CI has not run.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Schisms** (VISION.md "Schisms": "its adherents lose contact for a long time"). A religion's followers form bodies of neighbouring regions; its main body holds its holy land.
  - Followers cut off from it for long enough become a sect with one tenet changed: a tenet replaced by one the religion lacked, never one its others exclude.
  - The sect's name descends from its parent's, its colour lies near its parent's, and its holy land is its most populous region.
  - The schism is an event naming the change ("holding to Holy war in place of Pacifism"), and the inspector names the faith a sect split from.
  - A realm whose heartland's people form the sect takes it as its state religion.
- **The secular age** (VISION.md "Secular age"): from the Early modern era on, a realm grows secular, era by era but never wholly. As it does:
  - its peoples of another faith are unsettled less;
  - zeal weighs less in its research;
  - it founds religions less readily.

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md "Religion" (schisms, secular age), milestone M4.

**Not in this slice:**
- Schisms from hostility between co-religionist realms, or after their wars, come with M5 and M6.
- In the secular age, VISION.md's ideological friction over government forms comes with M7, and religious difference in relations with M5.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Bodies and the main body** (`schisms` in `faith.ts`, at each religion's yearly refresh):
  - bodies are the religion's followers in neighbouring land regions or in one polity (VISION.md counts the same civilization as contact), so a realm's enclaves and overseas provinces are not cut off from its other followers;
  - the main body holds the holy land, or, once the holy land has fallen away, is the most populous;
  - followers outside it count their years apart (`PopulationGroup.faithApart`), back to 0 whenever they rejoin it or change faith.
- **A sect** (`FAITH_TUNING`): a body of at least `schismRegions` 3 regions becomes a sect with a chance of `schismRate` 0.02 a year × how long its people have been apart. The years are people-weighted, and the chance rises from none at half of `schismYears` 150 to all of it at 150 (graded: one newcomer does not hold back a whole body). At most one sect forms per religion a year, larger bodies first.
  - The schism is recorded before any change of state religion it brings, and its text says whether the followers were cut off from the holy land or, once that is lost, from the main body.
  - **Its tenets** (`changeTenet`): one replaced by a tenet the religion lacks that the rest do not exclude (or, if none, one dropped).
  - **Its founder** is the polity holding most of its people. Its name descends from the parent's (`descendName`, in the language of its most populous region's culture), and its colour turns by up to `sectHueShift` 30°.
- **Secularity** (`secularity`): `secularPerTech` 0.05 for each tech of the Early modern era or later a polity knows, at most `secularMax` 0.8 (gradual, as VISION.md says). It multiplies down:
  - faith friction, × (1 − secularity);
  - zeal's weight on religion techs, which moves toward 1 by secularity;
  - Zeal in building shrines, temples and pious wonders (`build.piety`);
  - the founding chance, × (1 − secularity).
  - Religious difference in relations comes with M5, and VISION.md's government-form friction with M7.
- **Rules:** 23. The contract is unchanged (the `schism` event type already existed; its text is new).

## Review (independent M4 milestone review, 7 October 2026) and fixes

- **No blocking defect.** Every M4 acceptance criterion holds when recomputed from the study's events and stats.
  - Cultures split from a civilization's culture 10–22 times by 2,000, always held by a civilization.
  - Hybrids number 1–5 by 2,000.
  - The first religion is founded by 2,283.
  - Within 500 years, a religion is followed in 6–15 civilizations; counting state religions only, 3–15.
- **Fixed:**
  1. **Medium: sects formed inside their parent's own realm and dissolved within decades,** and the same place split again (a Verdant enclave three times). Followers were in contact only through land neighbours, so enclaves and overseas provinces counted as cut off, and the realm's state religion pulled them straight back. A realm's followers are now one body.
  2. **Medium: years apart carried over when a people changed faith,** and one newcomer held a whole body back for 150 years (a hard cutoff). The count resets on a change of faith, and the chance is graded by the people-weighted years apart.
  3. **Low:**
     - the secular age is gradual (by tech, not by era) and also fades Zeal in building;
     - the schism is recorded before the state religion change it brings, and its text distinguishes a lost holy land;
     - the parent's counts drop at once;
     - the assimilation halves are tunables;
     - README no longer overclaims;
     - tests cover a realm's enclave (no sect), the reset on conversion, and fewer foundings in the secular age.
- **Recorded:**
  - **Religion can converge:** Atlas's first religion stood alone for 1,285 years in 15–22 civilizations, and reformations did not change that. M5 (religious difference in relations) and M6 (holy war, conquest) are where faiths are meant to divide again.
  - **Hybrids almost stop forming after 2,000,** as assimilation shrinks second peoples below a quarter of a realm.
  - **The save-ready test stops at year 920,** before religions and hybrids exist. The new state is plain data, which a structured copy carries.
  - **The inspector's ancestry** shows the top six starting peoples, rounded, and traces descent to the starting peoples only.
  - **Speed:** 110–164 ms per year (studies take 5.5–8.2 minutes per seed at about 3,000 regions), against VISION.md's budget of about 10 minutes at about 4,000 regions, with M5–M9 still to come.

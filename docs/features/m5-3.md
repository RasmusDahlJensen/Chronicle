# M5.3 — Resource supply and demand

**Feature / status:** the third slice of M5 (the plan is in the [M5.1 brief](m5-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** VISION.md's "Supply and demand": demand comes only from the techs a civilization knows, so the strategic map changes with every era. A shortfall weakens what depends on the resource but never blocks it. Shortage is a need that trade (M5.4) answers, and later war (M6).

**Observable outcome:**
- **Demand.** Each civilization demands the resources its known techs use:
  - copper (Copper and Bronze working), tin (Bronze working), iron (Iron working, Steel, Industrialization), coal (Industrialization), oil (Combustion engine) and uranium (the nuclear techs);
  - timber (Boatbuilding, Sailing) and stone (Masonry, Engineering).
  - The amount is its people ÷ 40 million units a year (one site covers about 40 million people; see "The scale" below).
- **Supply** is what it works in its own land:
  - each usable mineral site worked by a mine, and each stone site worked by a quarry, gives a unit;
  - timber sites give a unit once it knows Forestry;
  - forested land supplies timber, and rough or mountainous land stone, in proportion to its area.
  - Imports come with trade (M5.4).
- **Shortage weakens, never blocks.** A tech's multipliers count only as far as the scarcest resource it uses is supplied (supply ÷ demand, down to a floor of 25%). Bronze working's +30% strength becomes +7.5% without tin, and its farming bonus shrinks likewise.
- **Shortage is a need and a motive.**
  - The decision view's **shortage** need is the largest shortfall.
  - Exploring weighs it (new land may hold what it lacks).
  - Expanding weighs land that would make good what it lacks: sites it could work there now, and forest or rough land for timber and stone.
- **Lab:** a civilization's inspector shows each resource it demands: supply, demand and how much of it is met, with shortfalls marked.

## Acceptance

- **Tests:**
  - supply counts only worked, usable sites in owned regions plus land by area;
  - demand follows known techs and people;
  - access is supply ÷ demand within [floor, 1];
  - a tech's multipliers scale with the access of what it uses, and full access changes nothing;
  - the shortage need and the expansion and exploration factors rise with a shortfall;
  - tribes are untouched.
- **Study:**
  - the share of civilizations short of something, and mean access to copper, tin, iron, timber and stone, at 1,000, 2,000 and 3,000;
  - expansions citing a resource;
  - the earlier gates hold.
- **Browser:** the inspector shows a civilization's resources.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **The scale (the user's decision, 8 October 2026).**
  - **The change:** VISION.md's "one site covers about 50,000 people" is scaled to this simulation's peoples, `RESOURCE_TUNING.peoplePerSite` = 40 million. Realms here hold 20–40 million, so 50,000 would leave every realm short of everything forever.
  - **Calibration:** a fork from year 1,000 for two seeds compared 10, 20 and 40 million.
    - At 10 million only 16–28% of copper demand was met.
    - At 40 million a world's ~30 copper sites cover about as many people as live then (unevenly, so trade has work), and its ~9 tin sites only a third. Copper is uneven, tin is globally scarce, and timber and stone are mostly met: VISION.md's intent.
    - The population moved by −0.8%; unions and civilizations stayed the same.
- **Demand:** every resource a known tech needs (`use`, plus a new `demand` effect for timber on Boatbuilding and Sailing and stone on Masonry and Engineering) is demanded once, at people ÷ 40 million units a year. Gold and salt are not demanded (they pay wealth, as before).
- **Supply:**
  - **Sites:** a unit per usable site in its own regions that is worked: by a mine in a living settlement of the region (minerals), a quarry (stone), or Forestry (timber).
  - **Land:** forest, boreal forest and rainforest supply timber at a unit per 50,000 km²; mountains and snow supply stone likewise.
  - **Imports:** these arrive with trade in M5.4.
- **Access weakens, never blocks.**
  - **The scale:** a tech's multipliers count only as far as the scarcest resource it needs is supplied: 1 + (multiplier − 1) × access, with access = supply ÷ demand within [0.25, 1]. At full access the multiplier is exactly the tech's own value, so histories do not change until a civilization is short.
  - **Where access lives:** in each civilization's knowledge, set yearly. Every reader of multipliers sees it, with no call site changed.
  - **Tribes:** never assessed (VISION.md: "per civ"), so their access stays 1.
- **Needs and motives:**
  - **The shortage need:** the largest shortfall (1 − supply ÷ demand).
  - **Expansion:** weighs 0.3 × the largest shortfall among the resources whose sites it knows in the candidate region. It cites `resource`, so the M3 expansion test now also accepts `resource` among an expansion's reasons.
  - **Exploration:** weighs 0.3 × the shortage need, since new land may hold what it lacks.
- **When it runs:** yearly per civilization in the diplomacy and trade system, before relations: trade (M5.4) will read it there.
- **Lab:** the inspector's Resources section lists each resource a civilization demands or works, as supply of demand (and the share met), with shortfalls marked.
- **Contract:** protocol 28 (`resources`). Rules version 26.

## Review

The independent review came before the full study, per Dev 03's routine. It found no blocking defects:
- history is unchanged until a civilization is short (identical hashes through year 800);
- the hash, saves, tribes' access and the query layer hold;
- access matched supply and demand at every check of an 1,100-year run.

Its findings, all resolved:
1. **The inspector rounded small demands to zero.** It hid real shortfalls and called needed resources "not needed". Fixed: rows carry three significant digits and the simulation's own share met, and the lab writes "short" (not colour alone).
2. **A fork that changed the floor failed its invariants.** Fixed: the invariant no longer reads the floor (access 1 where nothing is demanded or for a tribe, otherwise within (0, 1] and never below the share met); a fork's floor takes effect at each civilization's next assessment.
3. **Expansion was drawn to sites it could not yet work** (timber before Forestry, stone before quarries), while forests and mountains that would help now counted for nothing. Fixed: `reliefThere` weighs what the land would make good now (workable sites, forest and rough land), at most the shortfall.
4. **Structure:** the land cache is keyed on the geography. One helper (`available`) feeds every shortfall, so M5.4's imports are a single change.
5. **Text:** the capacity comment, the "0 until M5.3" wording and the browser check are corrected. The exploration factor is `resourceShortage` (famine's `shortage` stays). The study's needs table shows shortage.
6. **Tests:** negative supply cases; access through learning, merging and inheritance; relief; and a real history being assessed.

## Results

The study is `.chronicle/studies/m5-3` (five seeds, 3,000 years, 7.1 minutes). For iterating: a two-seed check and a fork from year 1,000 (the scale).
- **Shortage is common and uneven:**
  - civilizations short of something: 19–32% at 1,000, 52–63% at 2,000, 73–79% at 3,000;
  - at 2,000, demand met: copper 41–83%, tin 0–30% (globally scarce), iron 28–47%, timber 70–77%, stone 77–96%;
  - 15–21 expansions per seed were chosen partly for a resource.
- **Its effect is felt but not crippling.** Atlas's population at 2,000 is 5.5% below M5.2's (its Iron-age realms are short of iron, so their farm bonus shrinks).
- **Gates hold:**
  - the longest run above 35%: 78/0/0/0/102 years;
  - civilizations at 2,000: 29/27/32/30/30;
  - unions by 3,000: 41/58/51/47/58;
  - hybrids by 2,000: 2/5/4/5/1;
  - splits from a ruling culture by 2,000: 12/17/7/12/12;
  - a religion founded by 2,500 in every seed, followed in 3–15 civilizations within 500 years;
  - decisions citing a belief more than half off: 10–38;
  - the causes rule: 0 short.

# M3c.4 — Empire strain, and review fixes for M3c.3

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3c, slice 4 (the last). Status: verified (7 October 2026), committed on `main` and tagged `civ-m3c`, not pushed, so CI has not run; awaiting the user's review (VISION.md rule 2's stop).

**Observable outcome:**
- **A large, old realm holds its far provinces less firmly.** The strain of size and age lowers stability most in the regions farthest from the capital. Together with the heavier taxes and arrears such realms already face (M3c.2), this makes their edges restless and less productive. Wonders, temples and light taxes still steady them. The inspector's stability line names the strain.
- **Strain shapes unions.** A realm whose costs already outrun its customary taxes is loath to take in more.
- **The study shows it:** stability and unrest in the largest, oldest third of realms against the smallest, youngest third, and the largest people's share of the world.
- **Fixes from the M3c.3 review:**
  - one drought is one relief episode;
  - taxes rest on a true measure of how calm a realm is;
  - relief the treasury could not buy is counted.

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md milestone M3c ("stability strain from size, age and taxes, so large old empires are hard to hold but possible and rewarding to keep"; revolts, secession and civil war remain M7's), "Wealth", "Unification", and the user's M3b review answer 8.

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Strain** (tests):
  - it grows with a realm's size and age, most in its far regions;
  - a small young realm feels hardly any;
  - it is part of a region's calm, unlike taxes and arrears.
- **Unions:** a strained realm admits less (its admission chance; the union test exercises admission).
- **Health checks** (five-seed study):
  - the largest, oldest realms are less stable and have more regions in unrest than the smallest, youngest ones, yet they last (no breakups until M7);
  - the largest share stays below 35% in most seeds;
  - M3c's other health checks hold.
- **Gates:** M1–M3, M3b and M3c gates hold, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Strain** (`stability.ts`; `STABILITY_TUNING.strain` 0.02, `strainCore` 0.25): each region loses 0.02 × (the realm's size factor × age factor − 1) × (0.25 + 0.75 × min(1, remoteness)).
  - The factors are the administration factors (`BUDGET_TUNING`): (regions ÷ 20)^½ from 20 regions, and up to double with age.
  - About 0.06 at the edge of a 100-region, 1,000-year realm, and 0.13–0.18 for 300–500-region realms after 2,000 years. The heartland feels a quarter of it.
  - It is part of the region's calm (what taxes rest on), and cited as a cause of unrest.
- **Unions** (`UNITE_TUNING.strainRefusal` 0.6): the larger realm's admission chance is × (1 − 0.6 × its own budget strain).
  - **Tried and removed:** a "purse" pull (0.15 × the smaller realm's budget strain) drawing cash-strapped realms to a larger neighbour.
    - Young realms spread thinly over many regions are strained, so it fed consolidation: Atlas's leading people grew to 165 regions by 700 against 86, and to 42–47% of the world's people against 31–33%. Unions in Atlas and Elsewhere doubled by 1,000.
    - Without it, Atlas holds 32.5% at 1,000 and 27.4% at 1,500.
    - It worked against what this slice is for: making large empires hard to keep.
- **Review fixes for M3c.3:**
  - **Relief episodes:** each region records the tick relief last reached it (`reliefTick`, cleared when its people leave). Relief within 12 months of the last continues the episode; only a new episode is an event. The inspector shows relief arriving this month or last.
  - **The hungriest people first:** relief goes in order of food security, not food wanted.
  - **The order of the yearly assessment:** calm, then taxes on it, then stability under the new rate (milestone review).
  - **Calm:** each region's stability without taxes and arrears, clamped once, recorded when the region is judged (`state.calm`). Taxes are now set after the regions are judged, from this year's calm.
  - **Shortfalls counted:** relief still wanted is counted as held back for lack of treasury (the treasury could not pay for what a donor could send) or of food to spare within reach.
  - **Documented:**
    - relief food sent in a month lands that month (a month's consignment), so it spares that month's hunger too;
    - distance sets its cost and what spoils on the way;
    - relief reads the capacity as last refreshed.
  - **Tests:**
    - an episode across a drought's months;
    - the shortfall counts;
    - Refrigeration's store limit;
    - a negative invariant test for the relief flow.
- **Contract:** protocol 20 (stability's `strain`). **Rules:** 18.

## Independent milestone review (M3c as a whole, 7 October 2026)

- **No accounting, determinism, hash, save-readiness or architecture bug in M3c.**
- **Fixed before the tag:**
  1. **Medium: misreported dominance.** The briefs' "largest share below 35%" sampled centuries and missed the first farmers' 38–44% around year 700 in three seeds. The M3c.2 and M3c.3 briefs are corrected, and the study now measures the longest run above 35% every year (`trackDominance`).
  2. **Medium: the treasury-shortfall metric overstated the treasury's role.** It now counts one month of unmet need, attributed to the treasury only as far as food to spare in reach went unpaid for (that food set aside so it counts once). The treasury's share fell from 11–32% to 7–18%.
  3. **Medium: regression tests were missing.**
     - union admission under forced strain, and the count of strain refusals;
     - calm differing from stability under heavy taxes and arrears;
     - the budget view reading calm;
     - relief to the hungriest people first;
     - an invariant that land nobody holds has calm 1 and no relief record (the resets when people leave).
  4. **Low/medium:** the unmet acceptance item (unrest) and the small union effect are now stated plainly above, and a refusal count was added.
  5. **Low: stability lagged taxes by a year.** Now calm is measured, taxes are set on it, and stability is judged under the new rate.
  6. **Low:** the review package below.
- **Questions it raised** are among the questions for the user below.

**Limitations:**
- No realm breaks up, revolts or secedes before M7, so strain and arrears cost only output, research, taxes and the wear of buildings and roads.
- Relief is overland only; sea carriage waits for trade (M5). Food sent lands within the month.
- The "rich" third of realms by output a year is mostly the largest third.
- Engineering is not reached by 3,000 in Elsewhere, so its bridge gate cannot be checked there.

## Questions for the user (the stop after M3c)

1. **How hard should a large empire be to hold?**
   - Today the strain of size and age, heavy taxes and arrears keep large old realms steady at 0.73–0.88 stability, above the unrest line (0.4). Strain alone reaches about 0.18 at the edge of the largest realms.
   - Until M7 adds breakups, harder means more unrest at the edges, with less output and research there.
2. **Should neglect cost more visibly?**
   - Over 3,000 years no building, road or wonder was lost to unpaid upkeep in any seed, despite 44–64 times realms fell into arrears. Arrears end before anything wears away.
   - Faster wear would let poor realms visibly lose temples, granaries and roads.
3. **Is famine relief too strong or about right?**
   - Relief cut famine deaths to a quarter to a half of M3c.2's.
   - The treasury holds back 7–18% of the unmet need; the rest is lack of food to spare within reach.
   - Rich realms lose far fewer people to famine than poor ones, but "rich" here is mostly "large".
4. **Taxes:**
   - the customary rate is 20%, heavy above 30%, at most 50%; realms tax 13–19% at the median;
   - realms keep half a year to three and a half years of costs in reserve, more for traditional peoples.
   - Does that feel right, or should rulers differ more (greedier, more frugal)?
5. **What comes next?** VISION.md's order is M4 (culture and religion). P (persistence) waits until you ask for it; the state stays save-ready (a test).

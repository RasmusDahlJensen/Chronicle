# M3c.1 — Steady tiers, wonders known by word, save-ready state

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3c (added at the M3b review, 6 October 2026), slice 1. Status: implemented and committed as a checkpoint; one gate open (Chronicle's border ratio, below), carried into M3c.2.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Towns stop flipping.** A small place whose townspeople hover around the village–town line is no longer announced again and again as growing into a town and shrinking back. A settlement changes tier only after its size has stayed past the line for five yearly checks in a row.
- **Wonders are known only by word.** A civilization knows of a wonder only if it holds it, has met the people who hold it, or has met a people who has: word of great works travels one step further than other news. A people far from any contact may set out to build the Pyramids that stand across the world. It then learns that they exist, and remembers.
- **Saving stays easy.** The whole simulation state is plain data. A test copies it in the middle of a history and checks that the copy continues exactly as the original, so save games (slice P) can be added whenever the user wants them.

**Relevant spec sections and the user's decisions (M3b review, 6 October 2026), recorded in VISION.md:**
- rule 2 (M3c before a stop for review; P waits until the user asks, with save-readiness guarded by a test);
- "Settlements" (tiers change only after lasting change);
- "Wonders" (known only through contact, word travelling one step further);
- the M3c milestone and its health checks.

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Tiers:** no settlement changes tier more than a few times in a century (the study's tier table). Tier changes fall well below M3b's 22,000–40,000 per seed.
- **Wonders:** a civilization never sees a wonder that neither it, nor a people it met, nor a people they met holds (unit test). A civilization that tries to build a wonder that stands elsewhere learns of it.
- **Save-ready:** a state copied mid-history continues with the same state hash and event-log hash (test).
- **Gates:** M1–M3 and M3b gates hold in the five-seed study, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Tiers** (`SETTLEMENT_TUNING.tierYears` 5):
  - each yearly settlement check, a settlement whose averaged townspeople call for another tier counts a year (`Settlement.tierYears`);
  - it changes tier when the count reaches five, and the count resets whenever the average is back within its tier;
  - the existing 24-month average and 60% demotion margin stay.
- **Wonders by word** (`perception.ts` `knowsOfWonder`):
  - a wonder elsewhere rules its type out of a civilization's Build options only if the civilization holds it, has met its holder's people, or has met a people who has;
  - `beginWonder` still refuses a wonder whose type stands or is under way anywhere. The refused civilization then remembers that wonder (`Polity.heardWonders`), so it does not try again;
  - a union passes on what the smaller civilization had heard of.
- **Save-ready** (`tests/simulation-save.test.ts`):
  - the state is plain objects, arrays, typed arrays, maps and sets, which `structuredClone` copies completely; only the chronicle is a class and gets its prototype back;
  - the test runs the "Save ready" world (standard size) to year 900, copies it, runs both 20 more years with invariants, and compares hashes.
- **Rules 15;** the contract is unchanged.
- **Why the dwell alone was not enough:** a five-year dwell cut tier changes by about two thirds, but a few settlements still changed tier 6–10 times a century. Two causes:
  - **Townspeople followed food security month by month,** steeply (slope 3.5), so a region's townspeople swung with the seasons. Now they close a twelfth of the gap to what the surplus frees each month, at least one person (`SPECIALIST_TUNING.adjust` 1/12): people move to and from the towns over months, not all at once.
  - **Housing filled in a fixed order,** capital first, so a region's whole swing landed on its last settlement (one swung between 6,437 and 317 townspeople). Now growth still fills settlements in that order, but a decline is shared by every settlement in proportion to its townspeople (`house`).
  - **Variants** (Chronicle to 1,100: tier changes, most changes of one settlement in a century, settlements changing more than 3 times):
    - sharing alone: 4,914 · 4 · 6;
    - with the dwell: 4,727 · 4 · 2;
    - with the smoothing: 4,201 · 3 · 0;
    - all three (kept): 4 · 1 by 2,000 (6,237 changes).

M3c.2's administration cost by travel distance makes land behind barriers costlier to hold, the principled lever. The gate is carried open into M3c.2 and not weakened. If it is still marginal after M3c.2, the evidence goes to the user at the M3c review.

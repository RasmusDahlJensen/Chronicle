## Diagnosis

Baseline (generator 5, captured from a clean export of `03e33f0` with `node scripts/world-digests.ts --out .chronicle/g1/digests-generator5.json`): 20 of 159 worlds could not be published. 19 failed with "Hydrology did not converge within 12 drainage passes": 16 of 100 Standard sweep seeds (`g1-1, 2, 6, 10, 11, 15, 17, 19, 20, 27, 42, 45, 53, 60, 78, 84`), 2 of 50 Large (`g1-28`, `g1-37`) and `Timing` at Standard. One more, `g1-29` at Large, generated but failed transport validation (below). `Timing` at Large, the five study seeds, `Repeat` and `Third` generated.

Instrumenting the pass loop on `Timing` (Standard) showed a two-state cycle from pass 4 onward: cells 11057 and 11570, diagonal neighbours at the same elevation (59 m), trade one catchment. When one is a 1 m pond (60 m) the river drains to the other, which becomes a dry terminal with runoff above 5,000 and is raised to 60 m; the first pond has lost its supply and is lowered back to 59 m. The next pass mirrors it, forever, so a higher pass limit alone cannot help (VISION.md's observation). The independent review traced the failures: 4 are such pairs (`Timing`, `g1-1`, `g1-15`, `g1-19`); in 14 others a single one-cell pit beside a spill path at the same level alternates between dry terminal (raised) and unsupplied pond (lowered) while the river takes the spill path.

**Fix:** the pass loop records each sink state (cell and level, in insertion order, which the flood also uses). Because each pass is a pure function of that state, a repeated state proves the run would never converge. On a repeat, the cells whose level differed within the cycle keep the highest level they reached and become fixed ponds the retention rule no longer lowers (still bounded by the 20 m depth and 100,000 km² area limits). The pass limit rises from 12 to 48, which also only affects worlds that previously failed. Worlds without a repeated state never take this path, so their output is unchanged by construction; the sweep confirms it. All 19 repaired worlds converge one pass after the fix (at most 9 passes).

**Second failure mode (found by the stricter sweep):** after review, the digest script also runs `encodeGeneratedWorld`, which validates the manifest, water graph and every tile exactly as the host publishes them. Generator 5's `g1-29` (Large) generated but could not be published: its drainage converged with a one-cell pond (bed 131 m, level 132 m) beside a dry 131 m cell that drains elsewhere, and the water contract rejects a pond above a lower non-lake neighbour (it would leak). A raised dry terminal had kept enough supply to be accepted as a pond after its catchment moved to the neighbour. **Fix:** when the passes converge, a pond fed by a sink that leaks into a lower non-lake neighbour (other than its outlet) has those sinks released, so the cell drains through the neighbour, and routing runs again. Worlds whose converged ponds never leak are unaffected. No small synthetic terrain reproduced this (450,000 random trials), so `g1-29` itself is the regression; the hydrology unit tests now also assert the no-leak rule of the contract for every scenario.

## Tin and oil placement

`src/world/generation/resource-clusters.ts`, after the existing sparse sites and before fertility, with its own hash streams (seed + 12001/12002 for tin, 13001/13002 for oil):

- **Tin:** up to 3 provinces at Large (2 at Standard), centres on mountain or ≥ 1,000 m land, at least 80 cells apart (40 at Standard); 2–3 sites within 4 cells of the centre (2 at Standard) on ≥ 500 m land, at least 2 cells apart. Total capped at ⌊(copper − 1) / 2⌋, so tin always stays below half of copper; a world with fewer than 5 copper sites has no tin (VISION.md allows worlds without a resource). Changed after review from a 2-site floor, which gave two Standard sweep worlds as much tin as half their copper.
- **Oil:** up to 5 basins at Large (4 at Standard), centres on desert, steppe or low coastal plain (≤ 100 m within 3 cells of the sea; 2 at Standard), all ≤ 300 m, at least 60 cells apart (30 at Standard); 3–5 sites within 6 cells (3–4 within 3 at Standard) on land ≤ 400 m, at least 2 cells apart.
- Snow, ice and water cells never receive either. Clusters that cannot reach their minimum size are skipped.

Decision (ambiguity, rule 6): VISION.md says wetland "barely exists", so oil uses desert, steppe and low coastal plain only.

**Independent review** (a separate agent, read-only, two rounds). Round 1 found no blocking issue; resolved findings: the mechanism description was wrong for most failing seeds (corrected in code, brief and ARCHITECTURE.md); repaired worlds were not validated by any test (the digest script and `tests/geography-digests.test.ts` now encode them, which exposed `g1-29`); no unit-level cycle regression (added); the 2-site tin floor broke "far rarer than copper" on two Standard worlds (removed); WORKFLOW.Md stage (updated).

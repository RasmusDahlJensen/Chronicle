# M3.3 — Joining and stability

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3, slice 3 of 3. Status: implemented and verified (2 October 2026), **but M3's acceptance is not met**. Two criteria fail: settled civilizations at year 1,000 are 101–155 against the required 10–60, and the border ratio is 1.05–1.13 against ≥ 1.5. This is VISION.md stop condition (a): the criteria still fail after diagnosis and further evidence-based attempts (below). The work is committed on `main` without the `civ-m3` tag and not pushed, so CI has not run. **Stopped for the user's decision** on how civilizations consolidate (options below); the user had also asked to approve M3 before M3b.

**Observable outcome:**
- When a farming tribe settles, larger peoples first, it weighs joining a civilization next to it against founding its own. The chronicle says which and why: "The X tribe of N people joins the Y, bringing R regions: they are kin".
- Kin, a large, well-fed and stable neighbour and an easy crossing draw a tribe in; Tradition, Expansionism and pride hold it back. A civilization takes in only tribes it can govern from its capital.
- Smaller farming tribes keep living as tribes until they join or are taken in. The largest civilizations reach 170–210 regions.
- Each civilization region has a stability. Hunger, distance beyond the capital's reach and rule over another people lower it; unrest breaks out below a threshold (an event), lowering output and research.
- The inspector shows a region's stability and what lowers it.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **The joining choice** (`decisions/join.ts`, pure; the tribe's view `joinView` in `perception.ts`). The options are each civilization the tribe has met whose land it sees next to its own, and founding its own (score 0.1).
  - A civilization's pull = 0.6 if kin (the same founding people) + 0.3 × culture similarity + 0.6 × min(1, log10(1 + its people in sight ÷ the tribe's people) ÷ 2) + 0.1 × how well fed those people are + 0.1 × their regions' stability − 0.15 × the cheapest crossing's travel-km ÷ 1,000 − 0.3 × Tradition − 0.3 × Expansionism.
  - The choice is weighted random among the best three above the minimum, as at every decision step.
  - The civilization admits the tribe with chance 1 ÷ (1 + (travel-km from its capital to the tribe's land ÷ its reach)⁴). A tribe turned away founds its own; the event's causes say so.
  - A joining tribe's bands pass to the civilization where they live (`transferGroup`), each founding a village; the civilization learns what the tribe saw. Joins take effect after the month's population pass, so no one is born or dies twice in a month.
- **Settling by scale:** the yearly settling chance is multiplied by min(1, (the tribe's people ÷ 200,000)²). A people forms a state when it is large enough to need one, so large peoples found the first civilizations and smaller farming tribes go on as tribes for a while.
- **Fewer, larger tribes:** breaking away now uses reachKm 2,000 and tribeBands 80 (it was 1,200 and 30). About 17% of splits break away (25% before), and there are about 320–470 tribes at year 500. This is within the user's "sometimes independent, mostly together".
- **Governance reach:** 3,000 travel-km (it was 1,500). Chiefdoms govern about seven region crossings out.
- **Stability** (`stability.ts`, yearly per civilization):
  - stability = 0.9 − 0.6 × hunger − min(0.8, 0.4 × (travel-km ÷ reach − 1, beyond reach)) − 0.5 × (1 − culture similarity) where another people lives.
  - Unrest begins below 0.4 and ends above 0.5.
  - Below 0.4, output falls by up to 20% and specialists' research by up to 50%, in proportion.
  - Fracture (revolt, secession) is M7.
- **M3.2 review fixes:**
  - The settler checks come before a band is displaced: a band the settlers could not replace is taken in instead.
  - Expansion causes are the needs' contributions; multipliers are logged with a × and never cited.
  - Expansion is infeasible (−∞) when the source cannot spare settlers and no tribe lives there.
  - Expeditions start from the edge of sight.
  - Absorption announces its village after the absorption event, with causes.
  - A displaced band no longer counts as a pressure-led move.
  - The study's gap counts the time since the last expansion.
  - An architecture test scans decision code for `import()` type expressions and true-state type names.
  - The civilization list rejects duplicates.
  - The state hash covers each decision's choice and outcome.
  - The M3.2 brief's scenario figures are corrected.

**Why it stops short:**
- M3's ways to consolidate are tribes joining when they settle and civilizations taking in tribes' bands as they expand. Once a tribe has settled into a civilization, nothing in M3 can merge two civilizations; that comes with vassalage (M5) and war (M6).
- Every tribe settles within a few decades of learning Agriculture (years 530–750). Farming spreads within months between neighbours, because most tribes already hold research progress towards it.
- So the count at 1,000 is fixed by about year 750, and many tribes settle with no civilization next to them, or one that is too far from its capital to govern them.
- Even making joining nearly certain and governance continental leaves 75–87.
- Borders are still mostly the tribes' old boundaries, so they follow barriers only slightly (ratio 1.05–1.13).

## Options for the user

1. **Peaceful unification (recommended).** Add one graded choice to M3's decision step: a civilization next to a much larger, kin or better-run neighbour weighs joining it, as tribes do; the larger one admits it within its reach.
   - Consolidation then continues between 600 and 1,000 and beyond, emergent and never certain.
   - Crossing costs weigh in, so remaining borders drift to mountains and great rivers.
   - Needs a VISION.md change: an M3 scope addition.
2. **Keep M3 as built and move the target.** Accept about 100–150 civilizations at 1,000 and let vassalage (M5) and conquest (M6) consolidate further. Needs a VISION.md acceptance change.
3. **Fewer, larger peoples from the start.** Make breaking away rare (about 5%), so a handful of huge tribes settle as large states.
   - Consolidates by construction.
   - But tribes would span continents, and the "sometimes independent" splits the user asked for become rare.

Whichever is chosen, two follow-ups: tech costs need recalibrating (below), and the border ratio needs re-measuring.

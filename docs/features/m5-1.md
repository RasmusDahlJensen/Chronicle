# M5.1 — Beliefs, intimacy and opinion

**Feature / status:** the first slice of M5, "Decision model, diplomacy and trade" (VISION.md). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**M5's slices:**
1. **M5.1** (this slice): beliefs about other peoples, intimacy, and relations and memory.
2. **M5.2:** the decision model: effective values, all needs, utility scoring with recorded causes.
3. **M5.3:** resource supply and demand.
4. **M5.4:** trade agreements through hubs.
5. **M5.5:** non-aggression pacts, alliances and road agreements with cross-border roads.
6. **M5.6:** overseas voyages.
7. **The M5 close-out.**

**Why this slice first:** every later M5 slice decides about other peoples (whom to trade with, ally with or trust), and VISION.md requires such decisions to rest on what a people believes and how it feels, never on the truth.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Beliefs** (VISION.md "Beliefs about other polities"). Every civilization holds estimates of each people it has met: their population, strength, wealth and era, each with a confidence.
  - Each estimate is off by a bias drawn once per pair (seeded, never re-rolled), shrinking as the two grow intimate.
  - So a remote neighbour can be thought twice as large as it is, for generations, until contact corrects it.
- **Intimacy** grows with a shared border, nearness and sharing knowledge (later slices add trade, alliances and roads, M6 war), and fades without contact.
- **Opinion** (−100 to 100, VISION.md "Relations and memory"), with its reasons:
  - standing reasons, recomputed yearly: shared or rival faith, how alike their cultures are, kinship, tension along a shared border, sharing knowledge;
  - memories that fade: being turned away from a union, a refused exchange.
- **Decisions use them.**
  - Uniting weighs the neighbour's believed size and the civilization's opinion of it.
  - Sharing knowledge weighs opinion.
  - The decision log records each belief it used, and the study compares it with the truth.
- **Lab:** the inspector's new Diplomacy section lists, for a civilization, the peoples it knows: opinion with its top three reasons, intimacy, believed population and strength (with how sure it is), sharing, and the year they met.

## Acceptance

- **The query layer covers beliefs.** Decision code still sees only perception's view types (the Biome rule and its negative test). A test checks that:
  - an estimate is the truth times (1 + bias × (1 − intimacy));
  - a pair's bias is the same every year, so its belief error changes only through intimacy;
  - a stranger's bias is at full strength, and a long, close contact shrinks it.
- **Opinion and intimacy:** tests check the standing reasons, that memories fade, and that intimacy rises with a border and fades without contact.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):**
  - At least one recorded decision cites a belief more than 50% off the truth (this is M5's acceptance).
  - The M3–M4 gates still hold.
  - The tables report mean belief error by intimacy, the spread of opinions, and the decisions citing beliefs.
- **Browser:** the Diplomacy section shows a civilization's known peoples, with opinion reasons.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **Who keeps relations:** civilizations, of the living peoples they have met (tribes decide through their bands until later milestones give them actions). They are judged once a year per civilization, staggered by id, in the diplomacy system's slot (`relations.ts`).
- **Bias:** a pure function of the pair (seed, observer, other, kind) through its own six-word random key. It is drawn the same every time, so it is "drawn once at first contact, never re-rolled" without storing it, and no system stream can collide with it.
  - **Size:** exp(N(0, 0.5)) − 1 within [−0.7, 2], so a stranger is typically misjudged by a third either way and sometimes threefold.
  - **Asymmetric:** each side misjudges the other in its own way.
- **Estimates** = truth × (1 + bias × (1 − intimacy)); confidence = intimacy.
  - **Strength:** until M6's soldiers, people × the techs' military multiplier (the simplest neutral form, which M6 extends).
  - **Wealth:** the treasury. **Era:** rounded and kept within the list.
- **Intimacy** moves 0.08 a year toward its target and fades 0.03 a year above it.
  - **The target:** 0.5 for a shared border, 0.2 for contact (within two regions) and 0.3 while sharing knowledge, at most 1.
  - **Over time:** a new neighbour reaches 0.7 in about nine years; a people out of contact fades over decades.
- **Opinion** (−100 to 100) sums:
  - **Faith:** +25 for the same organized faith; −30 × the pair's mean Zeal for rival organized faiths, halved by a tolerant faith (the folk ways are neither). Both weigh less in the secular age: × (1 − the pair's mean secularity), as VISION.md asks.
  - **Culture:** 30 × (similarity − 0.85) ÷ 0.15, within ±30. Cultures are fairly alike (a spread of about 0.13), so likeness is judged against a typical one.
  - **Kinship:** +10 for a shared lineage.
  - **Sharing:** +15 while they share knowledge.
  - **Border tension:** −40 × min(1, its regions on their border ÷ 4) × their mean Expansionism.
  - **Why these values:** the first values (likeness against 0.5, tension as a share of the realm's regions) made opinion almost uniformly warm. By 3,000 the mean was 33–48 and no pair was ever cold, which would leave M5's alliances and M6's wars nothing to work with.
  - **Memories,** fading by half-life: being turned away from a union (−20, 25 years) and a refused exchange (−10, 15 years).
  - **Reasons kept:** the three strongest, rounded to a tenth.
- **Unions use beliefs.**
  - **Size:** a civilization asks to join a neighbour it believes more populous, and the size factor is the believed population ratio (it was the known-region ratio).
  - **Admission:** the larger realm's admission checks the true population, so a union asked for on a belief far off can fail: "the X were not as many as believed". That failure teaches the truth: intimacy rises by 0.3, and it waits the usual 30 years before asking that realm again.
  - **Opinion:** adds ±0.1 to the union score and ±0.05 to the sharing score at ±100.
- **Calibration** (fork tool, from year 500 to 2,000).
  - **The problem:** the switch to believed population raised unions (mean 55 against M4's 41 by 2,000). Atlas held one realm above 35% of the world's people for 1,057 years.
  - **The variants:** dropping opinion, a size weight of 0.15, or a gentler size scale. 0.15 came closest to M4 everywhere: unions 41/49/43/35/59 against 37/49/40/27/53, civilizations 24/25/30/32/27 against 25/26/35/31/27, and the longest dominance at most 119 years.
- **The decision log** records, for each option, the beliefs it weighed with the truth before the action (decide.ts, outside the choice). The study counts chosen decisions that weighed a belief, and those more than half off.
- **Dead peoples:** their relations are dropped when they end.
- **Lab:**
  - **Diplomacy section:** for a civilization, the 24 peoples it knows best (by intimacy), with opinion and its three reasons, believed population and strength, intimacy, whether they share knowledge and the year they met.
  - **Decision options:** each shows its beliefs as "believed people N (truly M)".
- **Contract:** protocol 26 (`diplomacy`, option `beliefs`, `BELIEF_KINDS` in the shared contract). Rules version 24.

## Review

The independent review found no defects in determinism, the query layer, the invariants or the contract. It checked:
- copies by `structuredClone` and by V8 serialize keep the hash;
- the invariants every 10 years on two worlds;
- every diplomacy row within its schema.

Its findings, all resolved:
1. **Faith in the secular age.** Faith in opinion ignored the secular age. Fixed and tested.
2. **Repeat asking.** A union refused as "not as many as believed" could be asked again every six months. Fixed: the failure teaches the truth and the civilization waits. Tested in the M2 union test.
3. **Stale fixture.** The M2 union fixture picked its pair by regions. Fixed: by population.
4. **Test gaps.**
   - The decision log's truth and the belief counts are now checked in the M2 test.
   - Memories of refusals are checked.
   - The architecture negative cases now cover `believe` and `truthOf`.
5. **Dead peoples' relations.** Dead peoples kept their relations, which were hashed and saved. Fixed.
6. **Empty intimacy bands.** An empty band read as 0% error. It now reads "—".
7. **Docs.** Fixed: ARCHITECTURE, the perception header, the lab's believed strength and the "met in year" wording.

## Results

The study is `.chronicle/studies/m5-1`: five seeds, 3,000 years, the final values.
- **M5 acceptance:** decisions weighing a belief by 3,000 were 154, 341, 264, 194 and 302 by seed. Of those, 10, 34, 12, 24 and 21 cited a belief more than half off the truth (M5 asks for at least one).
- **Beliefs:**
  - **Error:** the error of believed population is 26–112% between civilizations that know each other little and 7–15% between those that know each other well.
  - **Intimacy:** mean intimacy is 0.48–0.72.
- **Opinion** spreads both ways:
  - at 1,000, before faiths spread, the mean is −8 to −18 and 8–23% of pairs are cold (≤ −30);
  - by 3,000 the mean is −12 to +8, with 0–17% of pairs warm and 8–21% cold.
- **Earlier gates hold:**
  - unions by 3,000: 40/49/43/47/57 (M4: 43/51/45/32/58);
  - civilizations at 2,000: 27/27/32/32/30 (M4: 25/26/35/31/27);
  - the longest run above 35%: 78/0/0/0/102 years, the same as M4;
  - hybrids by 2,000: 2/6/8/5/1;
  - splits from a ruling culture by 2,000: 12/15/9/13/15;
  - the first religion founded in 1,604–2,064, spreading to 6–21 civilizations within 500 years.
- **Speed:** 151–216 ms a year. The study shared the machine with a game for its whole run: every system was about 30% slower than in M4's study, in the same shares, and the new diplomacy system takes 0.7% of the time.

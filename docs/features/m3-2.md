# M3.2 — Decisions, expansion, migration and the political map

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3, slice 2 of 3 (M3.1 knowledge of the world; M3.2 decisions, expansion and migration; M3.3 joining and stability). Status: verified (2 October 2026); committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run.

**Observable outcome:**
- Every six months each civilization weighs expanding, exploring or doing nothing, using only what it knows.
- A civilization whose regions are crowded expands into the best neighbouring land it can govern. Either its settlers found a village there, or it takes in a tribe's band living there, or that band moves on to land that feeds it.
- People migrate from crowded regions to less crowded ones, within a civilization and across borders, so crowding reaches the frontier.
- Governance reach, in travel-km from the capital, makes land beyond it ever less worth taking.
- Expeditions map land beyond sight and can meet new peoples.
- In the lab:
  - The Political lens (the default) colours civilizations and marks capitals with stars.
  - A civilization list shows the largest civilizations; clicking one centres the map on its capital.
  - The inspector shows a civilization's reach, how far this region is from its capital, and its last decision with every option weighed.

**Scenario, inputs, and expected result:**

Whenever its land pressure is above 0.5 and land within reach remains, the gap since its last expansion stays under 50 years.
- The five study seeds: M1 and M2 acceptance still hold, every seed has first contacts, and the decision mix and border ratio are reported.
- The 10–60 civilizations at year 1,000 and the border ratio are M3.3's acceptance, once tribes can join civilizations.

**Boundary/failure cases and invariants:**
- Decision code imports only the view types (`PolityView`, `Candidate`), tunables, the RNG and its own folder: a Biome rule, with negative architecture tests for true state, a mutating query function and a dynamic import.
- Expansion meets the true world: a target taken first by someone else fails and is logged.
- Exact accounting: settlers and migrants are ledger migration, settlers carry their share of the store (no more than half of what the new land feeds), migrants never leave a region more crowded than the one they left, and a taken-in band keeps its people, food and crops.
- Every civilization region has exactly one living village, and the capital stays in the heartland.
- Polities meet on arrival.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Who decides:** only civilizations in M3; tribes act through their bands' moves and splits (population system).
- **Needs and scores** (all graded, no thresholds; `DECISION_TUNING`, `EXPAND_TUNING`, `EXPLORE_TUNING`):
  - **Expand**, per candidate (known land next to its own that no civilization holds as far as it knows, and that can feed farmers): score = drive × value × culture − distance − reach.
    - drive = the source region's land pressure + 0.5 × hunger + 0.5 × opportunity. It is the source region's pressure, not the civilization's average, because settlers come from that region. With the average, a full heartland was diluted by new, half-empty villages and expansion stalled.
    - value = (the land's static farming value ÷ the civilization's mean)^0.5, × 0.7 where a tribe's band lives.
    - culture = (0.5 + Expansionism) ÷ 1.5.
    - distance = 0.00003 per travel-km of the crossing.
    - reach = 0.05 × (travel-km from the capital ÷ reach)^4: mild within reach and prohibitive beyond it (0.8 at twice the reach). Earlier weights (0.5 and power 3, then 0.15 and power 4) made poor land within reach never worth taking, so a crowded civilization stalled with governable land left.
  - **Explore:** curiosity (unknown regions next to its sight ÷ 8) × (0.5 Openness + 0.5 Expansionism) × (0.3 + land pressure), plus 0.3 within 50 years of a new sea reach.
  - **Do nothing:** 0.08. Earlier, at 0.15, it outweighed most expansion scores.
  - The choice is weighted random among the best three above 0.02.
  - The log keeps the last 8 steps per polity, with every option's factors. The event an action causes carries its strongest three factors as causes.
- **Land value** (`SimulationState.landValue`): static per region, the people its land feeds at full game with Neolithic farming and herding, computed once. A civilization's own techs scale all land alike.
- **Governance reach:** 1,500 travel-km × the techs' reach multiplier (Chiefdom government factor 1 until M7). Travel cost is an edge's travel-km plus 10% / 50% / 100% for crossing a stream / river / great river; sea crossings cost 1.5 × km. It is computed over the civilization's own land from its capital: own regions relay, neighbouring land is reached but not crossed.
- **Expansion:**
  - Settlers are 15% of the source region's people (at least 20, leaving at least 20). They found a named village; the region becomes the civilization's.
  - A tribe's band living there is taken in with chance 0.5 + 0.6 × (the civilization's share of their people − 0.5) − 0.6 × (the band's Tradition − 0.5), clamped to [0.1, 0.95]. Otherwise it moves on to the free land within reach that feeds all of it best.
  - A band with nowhere to go joins. The first version sent it to the land with the best farming value; two foraging bands then starved there and broke M1's every-band-has-births-and-deaths rule.
  - A taken-in band keeps its culture, food and crops, and its tribe ends with its last band.
- **Exploration:** from the civilization's region with the most unknown neighbours (or, failing that, from its sight), an expedition walks 3 steps (5 with coastal sailing, 8 with navigation). Each step picks a neighbour weighted towards unknown land. It maps every region it passes and their neighbours as snapshots, and meets whoever lives on its path.
- **Migration** (population system, yearly per civilization, staggered by id): each region sends 0.1 × (its land pressure − a neighbour's) of its people to each less-crowded populated neighbour held by a civilization (its own or across a border), shared among them. Migrants join the people there; their culture blends from M4. Tribes spread by moving and splitting instead.
  - Moved here from M3.3 because expansion needs it: without it the frontier never fills and expansion stalls.
- **First contact on arrival** (M3.1 review): a group arriving in a region meets everyone next to it, and anyone across the sea where either side's reach covers the crossing. Before, contact waited for the arriving polity's sight refresh. A breakaway inherits its parent's contacts before it arrives.

## Verification and how the user will try it

`npm start`, open http://127.0.0.1:5173/ (it restarts itself when the code changes). Run to year 600:
- Tribes settle into civilizations. As each settles next to remaining tribes, the chronicle says "The X take in the Y band of N people in region R", or a band moves on.
- Click a civilization: the inspector's Governance section shows its reach and this region's distance from the capital, and its last decision with each option and its reasons.
- The Civilizations list under the chart lists the largest; click one to go to its capital (a star on the map).

Run to 1,000: the map is full. Civilizations rarely expand (only into land freed by famine or ruin), and most decision steps are Do nothing; M3.3's joining consolidates tribes into civilizations as they settle.

npm run study:history -- --all --years 3000 --out .chronicle/studies/m3-2

## Results, known gaps, and user review

**M3 acceptance scenario** (`tests/simulation-m3.test.ts`; the probe used the same fixture):
- Chronicle's lone civilization fills its first region in about 280 years. Its founding band of 384 people grows to about 650,000; one fertile region feeds about 760,000 farmers, and pressure only rises from 60% of capacity.
- It then expands steadily to 72 regions and 47 M people by year 900, reaching beyond its 1,500 km reach into land it governs less well.
- It is under land pressure > 0.5 with land within reach in 367 decision steps from year 717. Its longest gap since the last expansion in those steps is 38 years (required < 50). Its longest gap overall is 281 years, the first region filling.

**Five seeds, 3,000 years** (`.chronicle/studies/m3-2/story-health.md`):
- M2 acceptance still holds in every seed:
  - Agriculture first in 527–539, on top-quartile river or lake land in all five.
  - Growth after ≥ 3× before (4.2–4.8×).
  - Eras: Bronze 787–858, Classical 1,760–2,220.
- M1 acceptance still holds:
  - population ×458–655 and bands ×64–85 by 500;
  - 0 silent band-years;
  - at most 10 months above 1.1× capacity;
  - 89–91% of moves citing depletion or pressure;
  - water ratio 1.44–1.56.
- The first run of this slice broke M1's 24-month rule (21–40 months in four seeds). Migration could pour a large region's people into a region that feeds 73 people, and settlers into land too poor for them. Migration now moves at most half of what would even out two regions' crowding, and settlers are at most half of what the new land feeds.
- By year 1,000:
  - 417–601 civilizations (M3.3's joining is what consolidates them; target 10–60).
  - Civilizations expanded 54–159 times and took in 219–408 tribes' bands; 0–8 bands moved on instead.
  - 99–194 expeditions; 143–240 M people migrated.
  - First contacts: 228–299 per seed.
- Border ratio 0.97–1.05 at 1,000 and 3,000 (target ≥ 1.5 in M3.3): borders are still the tribes' old boundaries.
- Decision mix: by 600, 1% Expand, 0.1% Explore, 99% Do nothing; from 700 on, almost all Do nothing, because the land is full and Expand, Explore and Do nothing are M3's only actions. The story-health target (Do nothing below 70%) waits for M3b's Build and M5's diplomacy and trade.
- Longest gap between expansions per civilization: median 0 (most never expand after settling in a full world), max 1,818–2,108 years.
- Performance: 35–48 ms per simulated year (five seeds in parallel, with invariants). The decisions system costs about 2.3 ms per year in a single process; it now skips the capital distance search when there is no land to weigh, with an identical history hash.

**Known gaps:**
- The map is still a patchwork (M3.3).
- Tribes do not decide yet: they still act only through their bands.
- Expansion never reaches into another civilization's land (war is M6).

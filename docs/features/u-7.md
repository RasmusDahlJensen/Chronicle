# U.7 — All of M1–M5 in the game, ready for M6

**Feature / status:** the seventh and last building slice of milestone U, before its close-out (the plan is in the [U.1 brief](u-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** on 8 October 2026 the user asked that U be finished so the game "encapsulates all the data and gameplay we see from M1–M5", and that it be prepared to support M6+. An audit of every field the frames carry against what the game shows (the lab's inspector shows them all) found these still missing from the game:
- **Lens keys:** the political, culture, faith, era and descent lenses colour the map without saying what the colours mean. The lab has a legend for each.
- **Ruins:** VISION.md's map layers include them, and M3b leaves ruins where settlements are abandoned. Neither the game nor the lab draws them; only the region panel lists them.
- **The strongest options a decision weighed:** their scores, their strongest factors and the beliefs they weighed beside the truth. The game shows only the chosen option.
- **A region's food and people:**
  - its capacity, game stock and food by method;
  - births and deaths this year and last;
  - food security, stores, crops in the field, the share farmed or herded;
  - townspeople and rural people;
  - irrigation;
  - governance reach and the distance from the capital.
- **A people's descent and faith:**
  - how its culture began, its descent from the starting peoples, its line and traits, and the whole culture's values beside the region's;
  - its faith's tenets, founder and year, its parent faith, and whether this is its holy land.
- **Research options:** the strongest candidates at the last choice, by weight.
- **What each settlement pays and costs its realm.**
- **The world's wonders, cultures and religions** as lists (the wonders standing and being built, the largest cultures and religions with their regions and people).

**Observable outcome:**
- **Under the lenses,** each lens has a key:
  - **Political:** the largest peoples by colour.
  - **Culture:** the largest cultures.
  - **Faith:** the religions and the folk ways.
  - **Era:** each era with its peoples.
  - **Descent:** the founding peoples.
- **Ruins on the map** (game and lab): marked where they stand, razed ones crossed out, named when zoomed in. A people's perspective shows only the ruins it sees.
- **The realm panel:**
  - **Overview:** the strongest options the last decision weighed.
  - **Knowledge:** the research candidates.
  - **Region:** a people section (descent, line, traits, values, faith), a food and people section, governance reach, each settlement's account, and the region's ruins.
- **The ledger:** the world now, the wonders of the world, the largest cultures and the religions.
- **Development controls:** a history they altered says so in the game too.
- **Ready for M6:** the brief records where each M6 feature plugs into the game:
  - war standings in the diplomacy lens;
  - wars in the realm panel;
  - war stories and the chronicle's war kind;
  - map layers for contested, occupied and devastated land;
  - ruins (now drawn).

## Acceptance

- **Tests:**
  - **Ruins:** the frame's ruins are exactly the history's ruined and razed settlements, validated by the contract.
  - **Keys:** each lens's key lists what its colours mean, from the frame.
- **Browser:**
  - each lens shows its key;
  - ruins are drawn as the frame reports them;
  - the panel shows the weighed options, the research candidates, the people's descent and faith, the region's food and people, and the settlements' accounts;
  - the ledger lists the wonders, cultures and religions.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **Frames** (protocol 39):
  - **`ruins`:** every ruined or razed settlement's cell, name and whether it was razed; the 4,000 that fell last when there are more (`MAX_FRAME_RUINS`, so a long war-torn history keeps frames under the browser's 1 MB limit).
  - **Weighed beliefs:** each belief a decision weighed now names its people.
- **Ruins on the map:** drawn in the shared map (game and lab) once a region is a few pixels across, as a small grey square with a gap, crossed out when razed. Their names show when zoomed in, below living places. A people's perspective draws only the ruins it sees.
  - **None yet in practice:** no ruins arise in the M1–M5 histories of the test world (none by year 1,500), because abandonment almost never empties a region. M6's razing will be the first to exercise the drawing, and its browser test will assert ruins drawn from real razing. The browser checks now that the map draws exactly what the frame reports.
- **Lens keys** list, for each lens, what its colours mean, largest first, from the frame:
  - **Political:** the ten largest peoples.
  - **Culture:** the ten largest cultures.
  - **Faith:** the religions and the folk ways.
  - **Era:** each era reached, with its peoples.
  - **Descent:** the ten largest founding peoples, by people.
- **The realm panel:**
  - **Overview:** "Its strongest options": the four options the last decision scored highest (the frame carries four), each with its score, its four strongest factors in words, and each belief it weighed beside the truth. The chosen one is marked; an option scored below zero (one that could not be taken is logged at −1) says "ruled out". A note says how the choice is made (by chance among the best three, weighted by score).
  - **Knowledge:** the research candidates as bars against the strongest, with how research is chosen (by chance, weighted, among everything it could research; it keeps its target unless another grows much stronger), and the peoples met within two regions that quicken research.
  - **Region:**
    - **Its people:** origin, descent, line, the culture's reach and traits, faith (tenets, founder, year, parent, holy land), and the values here beside the whole culture's.
    - **Food and people:** capacity and game stock, food at capacity by method, births and deaths, food security, stores and crops, the share farmed or herded, townspeople and rural people, governance reach and the distance from the capital, and when its people arrived here (with the year the realm settled).
    - **Each settlement's account:** what it pays and costs its realm a year, and whether it is the region's seat; a resettled place says what it was once called.
    - **Ruins:** after the living places, each ruin ("the ruins of X", or "X, razed"), its founding year and its latest events.
    - **The harvest line:** it adds famine and irrigation.
- **The ledger:**
  - **The world now:** its people and townspeople; civilizations and tribes and the regions they live in; settlements by tier, and ruins; cultivated fields; roads and bridges; trade routes; cultures and religions.
  - **Wonders of the world:** standing, then being built, with where, whose and when.
  - **Tables:** the twelve largest cultures (with their parents) and the twelve largest religions (with their realms, regions and people), each with how many more there are.
- **Lens keys** count in words ("1 region"), key the folk ways only while some people keeps them (with their people and regions), and say how many more there are beyond the largest shown.
- **Development controls and restarts:** the top bar marks a history the lab's development controls altered (how many times, and any year it was loaded from), and a dismissable notice says when the world's history started again at year 0.
- **Ruin names** are placed with the settlement names, after every living place, so they never cover one.
- **Ready for M6:** where each M6 feature plugs into the game, so its UI is part of its slice. Lists that must grow with M6 are typed over the shared lists, so the compiler asks for each new entry:
  - **Standings:** "at war" (and later vassal and overlord) join `DIPLOMATIC_STATUSES`. `STATUS_NAMES`, the lens colours (`STANDING_COLORS`, over `StandingKey`) and the lens key's words are typed records over it; the map's standing counts and the key are derived from the colours. The diplomacy rows and the lens follow.
  - **Costs:** a new cost (the army's upkeep in M6.1) joins the frame's `costs`. `COST_NAMES` is a typed record over them; the game's and the lab's totals sum every cost, and their lines are drawn from it.
  - **Realm tabs:** a tab added to `REALM_TABS` needs its label (`TAB_LABELS`) and its view (the dispatch is a typed record), so a wars tab cannot show another tab's view.
  - **The treaty web** is drawn from `frame.treaties` (alliances, pacts, trade, joint roads), not from standings: wars need their own frame data (who fights whom, since when) and their own lines in it.
  - **Claims** (VISION.md "Diplomacy tab": claims in either direction) and grievances belong in the Diplomacy tab with M6.2.
  - **Wars on the map:** contested, occupied and devastated land are region states. They join the territory fill the way the story highlight and the perspective do (`useWorldMap`'s territory effect), with their keys in the lens row.
  - **Ruins:** now drawn and listed; razing adds to them. The frame keeps the latest 4,000.
  - **Stories and the chronicle:** a war story kind joins `STORY_KINDS` with its template (a test requires one); the chronicle's "War and raids" kind is ready.
  - **The lab** shows the same frames through the shared map and its inspector.
- **Known gap:** VISION.md's perspective "labels and tooltips show believed values and confidence" is not done (U.6 left it out): the perspective lens shows what a people sees and remembers, and the Knowledge tab its beliefs beside the truth, but the map has no tooltips.

## Review

The independent review (an agent) checked the frames, the panels, the renderer, the tests and the docs, and probed the test history (no ruins by year 740). Its findings, all resolved:
1. **The game's region panel hid ruins (material):** it listed only living settlements, and not a resettled place's former name. It now lists the ruins after them, and "once X".
2. **The ruins test was vacuous (material):** the test history has no ruins, so it compared empty lists. It now ruins one region through the real rules (`ruinSettlements`) and razes another, then checks the frame's ruins, the region's panel and the contract.
3. **"Ready for M6" claimed more than the code did (material):**
   - the standings were hand-written lists that a new status would have slipped past (the key and the counts); they are now typed over `DIPLOMATIC_STATUSES` and derived;
   - the realm tabs' dispatch fell back to the Region view; it is now a typed record;
   - the cost totals were summed by hand, so M6's army upkeep would have dropped out of the balance; they now sum every cost;
   - the treaty web does not follow standings, and claims were missing: both are recorded above;
   - ruins were uncapped (the browser refuses frames over 1 MB); the frame now keeps the latest 4,000.
4. **Still missing from the game:** the contacts that quicken research, the world's townspeople, settlements by tier, its peoples and their regions, and fields (the ledger's "world now"), and the altered-history and restart notices. All added; the perspective tooltips are recorded as a known gap.
5. **Labels:** "Its strongest options" (not every option), "ruled out" for options that could not be taken, the region's own arrival year, no dead "cut off" branch, plurals in the keys, folk ways counted, "and N more" religions, and research bars safe when every weight is 0.
6. **Renderer:** ruin names are placed with the settlement names, last.
7. **Smaller fixes:** a misplaced schema comment moved; the browser checks now wait for the realm and check the chosen option, the first research option, the faith, the wonders and the religions, and the altered-history mark; the README, the architecture and the screenshot script (which no longer stops when no civilization exists) are updated.

# M5.6 — Overseas voyages

**Feature / status:** the sixth and last slice of M5 (the plan is in the [M5.1 brief](m5-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** VISION.md's "Overseas voyages" are the Columbus moments. "A civ with Sailing or Navigation and a harbor sends an expedition toward unknown coasts. The chance of loss at sea scales with distance and falls with tech. A successful voyage reveals a strip of coastal regions, can trigger first contact, and is a named chronicle event with a generated explorer name." Until now the sea reached only crossings of up to 900 km, so other continents stayed unknown. VISION.md's "Sharing knowledge" adds that "trade partners and allies exchange snapshots every few months", which M5.4 and M5.5's treaties now make possible.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Voyages.** A civilization that knows Sailing and holds a harbor weighs sending a voyage at its decision step.
  - **What draws it:** Openness and Expansionism, land pressure and resource shortage, and a fresh sea reach.
  - **What holds it back:** the risk of loss, a recent voyage, and earlier voyages that found nothing.
  - **What it can know:** only its own harbors and reach, not what lies beyond.
- **The voyage itself.** It sails from its harbor nearest an unknown coast within reach: 2,000 km with Sailing and 8,000 km with Navigation, great-circle.
  - **Where it heads:** nearer coasts are likelier, and coasts of a landmass where it holds no land three times likelier.
  - **Loss:** it may be lost at sea, likelier the further it sails and less likely with Navigation.
  - **Arrival:** it maps the coast it reaches and the nearest coastal regions of that land, and meets whoever lives there.
  - **Nothing unknown in reach:** it finds only open sea.
  - **The chronicle:** each voyage names its explorer (a name in its people's language). One that meets peoples or reaches a large land new to it stands out.
- **Shared maps.** Once a year a civilization learns the maps of its trading partners and allies, keeping the newer view of each region.
- **Lab:** the decision reads "Send a voyage overseas", and voyages and discoveries appear in the chronicle.

## Acceptance

- **M5:** in every seed at least one overseas voyage reveals another landmass. In every seed where another landmass holds polities unknown to the voyager, a voyage causes first contact.
- **Tests:**
  - only a civilization with Sailing and a harbor weighs a voyage, less soon after one and after empty voyages;
  - a voyage maps a strip of one unknown coast within reach and meets who lives there;
  - a lost voyage learns nothing;
  - with nothing unknown in reach it finds open sea;
  - treaty partners share their maps;
  - the decision code sees only the view.
- **Study:**
  - voyages by 1,000, 2,000 and 3,000;
  - lost and empty voyages;
  - voyages reaching another landmass, and land new to the voyager;
  - first contacts made by voyages;
  - the earlier gates hold.
- **The complete `npm run check` passes.**

## Decisions and defaults

- **Score** (VOYAGE_TUNING): novelty × (0.5 × Openness + 0.3 × Expansionism) × (0.2 + land pressure + 0.3 × shortage) + 0.2 × staleness within 50 years of a new sea reach − 0.3 × the chance of loss at half its reach.
  - **Staleness:** min(1, years since its last voyage ÷ 100).
  - **Novelty:** staleness × 0.5 ^ (empty voyages in a row, less one per 200 years since the last, and none once its sea reach has grown since). So empty voyages hold a civilization back for a while but never for good, and Navigation brings a fresh start (VISION.md: "a civ that just learned Navigation gets a strong boost").
- **Where it heads:**
  - **Unknown coasts:** any within reach.
  - **Known coasts:** also one it has heard holds a people it has not met.
  - **Distance:** great-circle from its nearest harbor. No sea route is traced, a limitation recorded for later.
- **Loss:** min(0.8, km × 0.15 per 1,000 km) with Sailing, × 0.04 per 1,000 km with Navigation. A voyage with nowhere to head sails half its reach and may be lost too.
- **Arrival:** it maps the target and up to 6 coastal regions of its landmass, nearest by steps over land. A landmass of 10 or more regions is large.
- **Calibration:** with novelty renewing over 50 years, Chronicle and Atlas sent 188 and 274 voyages by 3,000, many to islets. Over 100 years, voyages are rarer, and their chronicle importance follows what they found (0.5 for meeting peoples, 0.35 for a large new land, less for islets and known coasts).
- **Shared maps:** a civilization's yearly contact step (research system) absorbs each trading partner's and ally's map (`absorbMap`, the newer snapshot per region), as a joining people's map is absorbed.
  - **Timing:** VISION.md says "every few months"; yearly matches the other yearly knowledge steps.
  - **No meeting:** hearing of a people is not meeting them.
- **Measuring the acceptance:** `voyageContacts` counts only first contacts a voyage makes on another landmass than the voyager's own. The study reports how many civilizations can sail and how many of them have not met some people living on another landmass, the condition in VISION.md's clause.
- **Contract:** protocol 31 (the voyage action); rules version 29.

## Review

The independent review came before the full study. It ran all five seeds to 3,000 (invariants every tick in two) and checked determinism by running three seeds twice. It found no correctness defect, and both M5 clauses held in every seed by its count. Its findings, all resolved:
1. **Empty voyages stopped a civilization's voyages for good (material).**
   - **What happened:** the empty count only reset on arrival, and even the fresh-reach bonus was damped by it. By 3,000 nearly every sailing civilization had stopped; Aster sent none after 2,300, though some had coasts in reach through new harbors.
   - **Fixed:** empties fade over 200 years each, and a grown sea reach clears them; the fresh bonus is not damped.
2. **The acceptance was not measured (material).**
   - **What happened:** voyage contacts included meetings on the voyager's own landmass, and nothing showed whether peoples overseas remained unmet.
   - **Fixed:**
     - only contacts overseas are counted;
     - the study reports sailing civilizations, and those with peoples unmet overseas.
3. **The voyage test passed without meeting anyone or reaching another landmass (material for tests).**
   - **New tests:**
     - target weighting;
     - a true overseas first contact (with its importance and `firstContact` event);
     - a trade partner's map shared;
     - empties fading and clearing.
4. **Smaller fixes:**
   - **Targets:** known coasts holding unmet peoples are now targets.
   - **Empty voyages:** they can be lost.
   - **Text:** "meets a new people" in the singular.
   - **Importance:** meeting on one's own continent no longer ranks with an overseas contact.
   - **Names:** the per-civilization count is `emptyVoyages` (not to be confused with the world total).
   - **Recorded:** yearly map sharing, and that no sea route is traced.
5. **Checked and sound:**
   - the random stream (salt 6) and names;
   - the new fields' initialisation, hashing and checkpoints;
   - no truth in the view;
   - lake harbors cannot sail (harbors are built only by the sea);
   - "new land" judged from the map before revealing;
   - `absorbMap` semantics and cost (2–16 ms for all partners at 3,000);
   - the template data.

## Review (independent M5 milestone review, 8 October 2026) and fixes

The reviewer recomputed every M5 criterion from the study's data (events and stats agreed exactly) and looked across slices.
- **Blocking, fixed: exporters let their land go unkept** (since M5.4).
  - **The cause:** a realm's yearly check of what it can keep up left out its trade income. Its taxes had already been lowered by those receipts (M5.4), so an exporter saw a deficit that was not there and let regions go from the outside in, and importers too little.
  - **The damage:**
    - buildings lost to unpaid upkeep rose from 1–3 thousand a seed (M4, M5.3) to 24–54 thousand by 3,000;
    - land let go unkept rose from 4–10% to 11–40%;
    - road coverage fell to 75–94%;
    - events per half-millennium tripled with rebuild churn.
  - **It inflated M5's own counts:** trade, joint roads and voyages were remade after decay.
  - **Fixed:** the check counts trade income, net. A regression test fails without the fix: an exporter whose receipts balance its budget keeps all its land up.
  - **Lab:** the budget line shows "trade agreements ±X".
  - **Versions:** rules 30, protocol 32.
- **Non-blocking, recorded for the milestones they concern:**
  1. **Do nothing is 80–96% of choices**, against story health's under 70%. Each M5 action is at most 1.5%. M6's war decisions and milestone U's view of the decision mix should revisit this.
  2. **Pacts are common and almost never end** other than by a party being gone. Mean opinion at 3,000 is warm (+5 to +20), and alliances are rare. M6 must weigh pacts against the temptation of war (pact terms or renewals).
  3. **Some needs barely move.**
     - Hunger is 0.00 in every seed's snapshots, people-weighted, though famines kill locally.
     - Infrastructure is near 0.
     - Shortage sits at 0.7–0.8, because tin is globally scarce.
     - M6's "desperate war" needs local hunger.
  4. **The beliefs rule, recorded as assumptions:**
     - **Treaties are public:** alliance candidates are filtered by third parties' true pacts and alliances;
     - **Openness is known:** trade options read a partner's culture's Openness, as sharing already does;
     - **Hubs are known:** recorded in M5.4.
  5. **`state.treaties` only grows** (about 300 entries by 3,000). It is scanned monthly; index the active ones before 5,000-year histories.
  6. **Docs:** the architecture doc's contract description (now protocol 32 with M5's fields), M5.2's status and M5.5's review section are corrected.
- **Checked and sound:**
  - the treaty invariants;
  - the state hash;
  - capped logs and faded memories;
  - determinism;
  - belief error shrinking with intimacy (2–9% between well-acquainted peoples);
  - speed (113–165 ms per simulated year, about M4's);
  - the earlier gates;
  - history stays varied: realms rise, unite and split, and faiths spread.

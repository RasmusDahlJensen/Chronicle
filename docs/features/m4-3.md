# M4.3 — Faiths: founding, tenets, spread and state religion

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M4 (culture and religion), slice 3. Status: verified, awaiting user review.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Folk ways first.** Every people keeps the folk ways of its culture until a religion reaches it (VISION.md "Folk beliefs"); the folk ways do not spread.
- **Religions are founded** (VISION.md "Founding").
  - A civilization that knows Organized religion (or Theology) and whose rulers still keep the folk ways may found a religion.
  - What moves it is a crisis (the memory of hunger in its land) or a zealous people.
  - The religion is named in its founders' language. It draws two to four tenets weighted by their values (zealous founders preach, warlike ones bless war, open ones are tolerant).
  - Its holy land is the founders' capital region, and it is their state religion from the start.
  - Founding is an event naming the tenets.
- **Religions spread from people to people** (VISION.md "Spread"; until M5 only through same-region and neighbouring contact).
  - A people is drawn to the faith of its neighbours (another realm's less), to its realm's state religion (more where a shrine or temple stands) and to holy land beside it.
  - It converts sooner when the faith preaches (Proselytizing), and later when it holds to its ways (Tradition) or already follows another religion.
  - Conversions are events, one per religion and realm a year.
- **State religion is the rulers' faith** (VISION.md "State religion"): the faith of the realm's heartland people. When they take up another, or return to the folk ways, the realm's state religion changes (an event), and peoples of the old faith are now of another.
- **Faith matters:**
  - **Unrest:** under a state religion, peoples of another faith are less settled, more if zealous and less under a tolerant faith. An ascetic faith steadies its followers.
  - **Values:** a faith's tenets pull its followers' values (Pacifism away from Militarism, Ancestor veneration toward Tradition).
  - **Research:** zealous peoples weigh religious techs more.
- **The map and inspector show it:**
  - a "By faith" peoples lens: religions in strong colours, the folk ways pale, and a legend of religions with the civilizations, regions and people following them;
  - the region inspector names the people's faith (its tenets, founders and year, and whether this is its holy land) or the folk ways of their culture, and a civilization's state religion;
  - the stability line shows the burden of another faith.

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md "Religion" (folk beliefs, founding, tenets, spread, state religion), milestone M4.

**Not in this slice:** schisms and the secular age (M4.4); missionaries, trade routes and roads as channels (M5); holy war and conquest (M6); Pious rulers and theocracy (M7).

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Tests:**
  - tenets: two to four, never two that exclude each other, weighted by the founders' values;
  - founding: only with the knowledge and the folk ways; moved by crisis or zeal; the heartland follows the religion as the state religion; event text with the tenets;
  - spread:
    - draws from neighbours (another realm's less), the state religion, a shrine and holy land;
    - conversion happens, with a heartland conversion changing the realm's state religion (event);
  - friction (zeal, tolerance), asceticism, a return to the folk ways, a religion's death and the stats;
  - tenet pulls, the zeal research weight, and Organized religion needing only Writing;
  - invariants: a polity's state religion is its heartland's faith, and every faith is a living religion or the folk ways;
  - the contract's religion list matches the markers.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):**
  - **M4:** every seed founds an organized religion by year 2,500, and within 500 years of the first founding some religion is followed in 3 or more civilizations;
  - **M1–M4.2 gates hold.**
- **Browser:** the faith lens and legend (none founded at year 200), the inspector's folk ways, and the complete `npm run check`.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **One faith per region's people,** as for cultures (M4.1); faith −1 is the folk ways of the people's culture, which carry no religion entity.
- **Organized religion needs only Writing and costs what Mathematics does** (M2's graph had Writing and Philosophy at Philosophy's cost).
  - With Philosophy, Organized religion was first known only at 2,182–2,962 (or never by 3,000) in the M4.1–M4.2 studies, too late for M4's founding by 2,500.
  - With Writing alone at Philosophy's cost, it was still too late in three seeds: Elsewhere never learned it by 3,000; Verdant learned it at 2,680 and founded at 2,800; Aster founded at 2,701.
  - Priesthoods arose with writing. Philosophy still leads to Theology.
  - **Eras:** Organized religion stays a Classical tech (VISION.md's list). A people's era is now the latest era up to which it knows a tech of every era (`eraOf`): it passes through each age. With the old rule (the latest era of any known tech), a people learning Organized religion before any Iron tech was labelled Classical (Atlas's leader at 1,254, its first Iron tech at 1,425). Catch-up reads eras, so its neighbours researched older techs faster. The rule applies to any shortcut in the graph, and VISION.md says only that eras are labels computed from the techs a people knows.
- **Zeal in research** (an addition to VISION.md's research weights): techs tagged religion weigh × (`religionZealBase` 0.8 + the people's Zeal), so a zealous people pursues them sooner, and religion techs are somewhat favoured overall.
- **Founding** (`realmFaith`, yearly per civilization; `FAITH_TUNING`):
  - a civilization that knows Organized religion or Theology;
  - chance `foundingRate` 0.05 × trigger, × `reformation` 0.01 when its rulers already follow a religion (a reformation is rare, but possible: no hard cutoff). With `faithAttachment` 0.3 in its place, 5–36 reformations per seed made a new world religion every few decades;
  - the trigger is the stronger of:
    - **a crisis:** famine deaths over about the last year in its regions as a share of its people × `crisisScale` 200, so a famine killing 0.5% of the realm is a full trigger. At 50, famine led one founding in 100;
    - **its people's zeal:** (heartland Zeal − `zealFloor` 0.4) ÷ 0.6.
  - The Pious ruler arrives with M7.
  - **First tried:**
    1. Rate 0.02 with the zeal floor at 0.5. Most peoples' Zeal lies near 0.45, so founding waited 100–500 years after the knowledge (Aster: 2,222 → 2,701).
    2. The floor at 0.3 with hardship as the crisis. Every founding then cited "zealous people" (even at Zeal 0.36), and the crisis branch never fired, because hardship fades and is spread thin over a realm.
    3. Only realms of the folk ways could found. The first religion then spread into its neighbours before they could found their own, and in Atlas one religion stood alone for 1,400 years.
- **Tenet effects beyond their pulls:** Monument builders as a state religion weigh shrines, temples and wonders × `monumentWeight` 1.5 (cited on wonders). Asceticism's followers produce `asceticWealth` 95% of their wealth, its counter to the stability it gives (VISION.md: "lowers wealth, raises stability").
- **Tenets** (`religions.ts` data, validated):
  - Proselytizing (spread ×1.5);
  - Holy war;
  - Pacifism;
  - Tolerance (friction ×0.5);
  - Ancestor veneration;
  - Asceticism (stability +0.02 for followers);
  - Mercantile blessing;
  - Monument builders.
  - Each pulls its followers' values by 0.05–0.15. Holy war excludes Pacifism and Tolerance.
  - They are drawn weighted e^(4 × Σ favour × (value − ½)).
  - Holy war and Mercantile blessing act through war and trade in M5 and M6; until then only their pulls.
- **State religion changes cite why:** the heartland's people converted, or the heartland moved to another people.
- **Spread** (`faithYear`, yearly for each polity's peoples together, staggered by polity id, so conversions are one event per religion and realm a year, citing the mean of what drew them):
  - **Draws** (kept by kind: neighbours, state support, shrine, holy land):
    - neighbours: the share of neighbouring peoples following the religion, another polity's at `foreignContact` ½;
    - the realm's state religion: `stateSupport` 0.3, plus `templeSupport` 0.3 with a shrine or temple in the region;
    - holy land under or beside the people: `holyDraw` 0.5.
  - **Conversion:** the people may take up a religion with a chance of `conversionRate` 0.1 × all draws together (at most 1) × the chosen religion's spread tenets × (1 − `traditionHold` ½ × Tradition) × attachment (1 for the folk ways, `faithAttachment` 0.3 for another religion).
    - The religion is chosen in proportion to the draws, a graded choice: the review found that always taking the strongest made a draw of 0.49 against 0.5 hopeless.
    - A people whose faith is its realm's state religion converts × `stateHold` 0.3 as readily: the rulers' faith is entrenched. Without it, heartlands changed faith about once a century (39–214 changes of state religion per seed).
  - New peoples (settlers, bands that move on or break away) keep the faith of the people they came from.
- **State religion:** a polity's `stateReligion` is its heartland group's faith (an invariant), updated when that group converts or the heartland moves (`followRulers`). Founding sets it without a change event.
- **Friction** (`faithBurden`, part of a region's calm):
  - under an organized state religion, a people of another faith (including the folk ways) loses `friction` 0.12 × (`zealFriction` ½ + Zeal) × the state religion's friction tenets (doubled after the review found faith cited in almost no unrest);
  - a faith's stability tenets steady its followers;
  - it is cited as a cause of unrest.
- **Contract:**
  - protocol 24;
  - the frame lists religions (id, name, hue, regions, people, civilizations) and each marker's faith (−1 for the folk ways);
  - the inspector's `people.faith` and polity `stateReligion`;
  - stability gains `faith`;
  - the new event type `faithSpread`; the `religionFounded` and `stateReligionChanged` texts.
- **Rules:** 22.

## Review (independent agent, 7 October 2026) and fixes

- **No blocking defect.** Every way a heartland changes keeps the state religion its faith (the invariant held through the study). Hashing, the contract and its checks, and tenet drawing were found sound.
- **Fixed:**
  1. **Medium: conversion events were grouped by month,** so 35–48% of them were extra. They are now one a year per religion and realm.
  2. **Medium: the founding trigger.** The crisis branch never fired, and almost everyone counted as zealous. Famine is now the crisis, and the zeal floor sits at 0.4.
  3. **Medium: Bronze-age peoples were labelled Classical** once Organized religion needed only Writing, which fed their neighbours' catch-up. The era rule now passes through each age.
  4. **Medium: the first religion could not be challenged.** Founding was blocked for a realm that had converted (a hard cutoff); now a reformation is possible at a lower chance. Conversion also chooses in proportion to the draws.
  5. **Medium: faith barely touched unrest.** Friction is doubled.
  6. **Medium: conversions and changes of state religion had no causes.** They now cite what drew the people, or why the state religion changed.
  7. **Medium: Monument builders and Asceticism lacked their VISION.md effects.** Both now have them.
  8. **Low:**
     - the inline halves are tunables;
     - the frame check covers each religion's civilizations;
     - Polity's doc comment is back above it;
     - tests now cover:
       - Tradition and another religion slowing conversion;
       - a reformation and its text;
       - the famine trigger;
       - the era rule;
       - Monument builders and ascetic wealth.
- **Recorded:** religions still only ratchet: no people returns to the folk ways. That is by design (the folk ways do not spread), so schisms (M4.4), reformations and later war and the secular age are what split faiths. The study's "followed in" counts a civilization when any one of its peoples follows the religion.

- **M4 acceptance (religions):** every seed founds an organized religion by 2,500 (between 1,386 and 2,283). Within 500 years of the first founding, one religion is followed (by at least one people) in 6–15 civilizations.
- **What moves founders:** zeal leads most foundings, and famine some (Chronicle 1, Aster 2). There are 0–4 reformations per seed.
- **Religions become world faiths:** two to eight per seed by 3,000, followed by 80–96% of the world's people. Most civilizations take one up as their state religion once (16–28 changes of state religion per seed).
- **Faith matters for unrest:** it is cited in 6–287 of 272–1,116 outbreaks of unrest per seed (0–28 before the review).
- **Conversions:** 1,154–2,881 conversion events per seed, one per religion and realm a year.
- **Gates hold in every seed:**
  - **M3 borders:** the ratio at 1,000 is 1.026–1.181; civilizations at 1,000 number 19–31.
  - **Dominance:** the largest share is at most 29.6% from 1,000 on (Atlas), with runs above 35% of 0–102 years.
  - **M1:** silent band-years 0, and the longest stay above 1.1× capacity is 2–10 months.
  - **M2:** growth ×4.2–5.6, and Agriculture invented independently 27–42 times by 1,000.
  - **M3b:**
    - settlements by water or a site 88–93%;
    - 4–5 wonders by 2,500;
    - road coverage 100% at 1,500;
    - bridges within 2–11 years of Engineering, now reached in every seed;
    - fields regrew in every seed.
  - **M3c:**
    - treasuries 1.2–1.8 years of costs;
    - at most 4 tier changes of one settlement in any 100 years, with 0–3 settlements changing more than 3 times;
    - famine kills less in rich realms at 2,000 and 3,000.
  - **M4.1–M4.2:** 10–22 splits from a culture that rules a civilization by 2,000, and 1–5 hybrids by 2,000 (unchanged).
- **Eras:** the study's "first reached" column counts the first discovery of any tech of an era, so Atlas shows Classical at 1,254 (Organized religion). A people's own era label now passes through each age.

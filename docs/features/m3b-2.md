# M3b.2 — Wealth and buildings

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3b, slice 2. Status: verified (6 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run; M3b continues with M3b.3.

**Observable outcome:**
- Civilizations earn wealth from their townspeople (more in market towns), keep a treasury, and spend it on buildings and their upkeep.
- At its decision step a civilization may build. It picks the building its settlements need most and starts it in its neediest settlements. The chronicle records each completed building with its reasons, for example a granary after hard years, or a temple where unrest grew.
- Buildings change the world:
  - granaries keep more food;
  - shrines and temples steady their regions;
  - libraries, observatories and universities raise research;
  - markets raise wealth and housing;
  - aqueducts let cities grow into metropolises;
  - walls guard exposed frontier towns (their defence counts from M6).
- Unpaid upkeep wears buildings down until they are lost.
- The lab shows the treasury, income and upkeep, each settlement's buildings and their condition, and works under construction.

**Relevant spec sections and agreed decisions:**
- VISION.md:
  - "Settlements and infrastructure": Buildings, and Destruction (neglect);
  - "Resources, scarcity and trade": Wealth;
  - "Personality and the decision model": the Build action;
  - "Specialists and townspeople";
  - rule 8, exact accounting for wealth;
  - M3b scope: wealth from specialists and markets with exact accounting; buildings as data with the Build action; the early buildings; upkeep and decay;
  - M3b acceptance: every building event records its causes.
- Mines, quarries and harbors follow in M3b.3, wonders in M3b.4.

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Exact wealth accounting:** every month each civilization's treasury equals last month's plus production plus wealth received minus construction, upkeep, wealth given and wealth lost (an invariant). Wealth is a whole number and never negative.
- **Buildings are data:** each has the tech that unlocks it, cost, construction months, upkeep, placement and effects, validated at startup. A building becomes available only through a tech that names it in its unlocks.
- **Every building completed or lost is an event with its causes.** A completed building cites the needs that drove the decision; a lost one cites unpaid upkeep.
- **Effects are visible in the simulation:**
  - housing rises with markets and aqueducts (cities and metropolises appear);
  - research rises with libraries;
  - stability rises with shrines and temples;
  - granaries keep more food.
- **Upkeep:** a civilization that cannot pay sees its buildings decay and eventually be lost (unit test).
- **Health:** the decision mix stays healthy (no action but Do nothing above 60%); Build is chosen in every seed.
- **Gates:** M1–M3 and M3b.1 gates hold in the five-seed study, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Wealth** (`economy.ts`, `WEALTH_TUNING`):
  - Each townsperson earns 1 a year, times their settlement's buildings' wealth multipliers (market × 1.5), less in unrest as output is.
  - The production system adds a month's earnings to the treasury in whole units; the fraction waits for next month.
  - Tribes have no treasury.
  - A union passes the smaller civilization's treasury and its works under construction to the larger one. A civilization that dies out loses its treasury and abandons its works.
  - Every change is a flow in `ledger.wealth` (before, produced, construction, upkeep, received, given, lost), checked exactly each tick.
- **Buildings as data** (`buildings.ts`):
  - Each has a purpose (food, faith, learning, trade, housing, defense), cost, months, upkeep, the smallest tier it stands in, and effects (multipliers on housing, research, wealth, food store and spoilage; additions to stability and defence).
  - A building is available once the civilization knows a tech that names it in its unlocks: Masonry (granary, walls, shrine), Writing (temple, library), Organized religion (temple), Currency (market), Engineering (aqueduct), Astronomy (observatory), Printing (university).
  - Startup validates them. A settlement's housing and its `bonus` (what its buildings add up to) are derived and recomputed whenever its buildings change (an invariant).
  - Mines, quarries and harbors come in M3b.3, and wonders in M3b.4. Fortresses, monuments and shipyards wait for war, prestige and voyages (M4–M6) under the scope rule.
- **The Build action** (`decisions/build.ts`, pure; `BUILD_TUNING`):
  - **Need by purpose, per settlement:**
    - food: hardship (the memory of hunger, fading 2% a month) + 0.2 × the share farmed;
    - faith: (1 − stability) × (0.3 + Zeal);
    - learning: 0.3 + Openness;
    - trade: 0.4 + 0.6 × Openness;
    - housing: crowding above 70% of housing;
    - defense: foreign peoples on the region's borders ÷ 3 × (0.1 + Militarism).
  - Each need is scaled by size = min(1, townspeople ÷ 8,000).
  - The type's score is purposeWeight × the mean of its best batch (ceil(regions ÷ 8), at most 12) − 0.5 × the batch's cost ÷ (income + treasury ÷ 5) − 0.6 × the upkeep after building ÷ income.
  - The best type is the Build option. It is begun in that batch of settlements, and the decision's drivers become the completion events' causes.
  - One Build decision begins a building type in several settlements, so a large civilization is not limited to one building per six months. Its label says so ("granary in 4 settlements").
- **Construction system** (`construction.ts`, monthly):
  - Upkeep is paid first. Unpaid, every building loses 1/60 of its condition a month times the unpaid share, regains 1/24 a month when paid, and is lost at 0 (event "building decayed", citing unpaid upkeep).
  - Works are then paid in equal monthly instalments while the treasury allows, and completed (event "building completed", citing the decision's needs).
  - Works in a settlement fallen to ruin, or no longer the civilization's, are abandoned.
- **Effects in the systems:**
  - housing (market × 1.25, aqueduct × 2.5);
  - research of the settlement's townspeople (library × 1.25, observatory × 1.15, university × 1.4);
  - stability of the region (shrine +0.03, temple +0.06);
  - the region's food store limit and spoilage (its best granary: × 1.5 and × 0.8);
  - walls carry a defence of 0.5 that counts from M6.
- **Aqueducts may stand in towns.** At first they needed a city, but a non-capital town tops out at 10,000 with a market, below the city threshold, so only capitals could ever have one: 2–8 per seed by 3,000.
- **Speed:**
  - A first version recomputed building effects in the monthly hot paths: 67–102 ms per simulated year.
  - Settlements now cache their buildings' bonus; the monthly housing and upkeep passes allocate nothing in the usual month.
  - Chronicle runs at 33 ms per year without invariants.
- **Contract** (protocol 12, rules 9):
  - actions include build; a decision option's target is the building type and its label names it;
  - event types append "building decayed";
  - the inspector shows each settlement's buildings with their condition and works with the share paid, and a civilization's treasury, income, upkeep and works.
- **M3b.1 review fixes** (recorded in that brief): the fill order, steadier tiers, one "by water" rule, the GROWING salt, the history search, and doc and label fixes.

## Independent review (after the commit)

One general-purpose agent reviewed `b282d45`. It found no blockers: every treasury change is a recorded flow, and the worn-building invariant cannot fire falsely. Its findings, fixed with M3b.4:
- **Should-fix: 54% of granaries were redundant.** Their store effect is the region's best, but each settlement was judged alone. Granaries, shrines and temples now serve their whole region (one per region), so stability no longer stacks per settlement either.
- **Should-fix: granaries cited "hardship" when there was none** (95% started below 0.01 hardship). The food need is now cited in its two parts: hard years and farmers' storage.
- **Should-fix: metropolises were unreachable.** A fully built capital houses exactly 50,000, and the average never reached the threshold of 50,000. It is now 40,000.
- **Minor:**
  - wording ("granarys", "a walls", "a aqueduct"): buildings now carry their names with articles and plurals;
  - works now die with a settlement that falls to ruin, and are abandoned if the settlement has shrunk below the building's tier when it completes;
  - hardship moves with a band that moves away;
  - hardship and the repairing flag are in the state hash;
  - the ledger entry is taken before a treasury changes;
  - granary effects without allocation;
  - the union test checks that the treasury, works and repairs pass;
  - the contract comment for Build options.

# M3c.2 — The wealth system: taxes, administration, services and deficits

**Feature / milestone / status:** VISION.md milestone M3c, slice 2. Status: verified (6 October 2026) and committed on `main`, not pushed, so CI has not run; the review findings below are fixed in M3c.3.

**Observable outcome:**
- **A realm lives off what its people do.** Townspeople's trades and the farmers' surplus are taxed; mines and quarries pay the crown. The settlement inspector shows what each settlement earns and what it costs, and what its region adds (farm taxes, sites) and costs (administration). Many villages and far provinces run at a loss, carried by rich cities and farmland.
- **Running a realm costs more the larger, farther-flung and older it is.** Each region costs administration, more the farther it lies from the capital, the more regions the realm holds and the longer the realm has stood. Townspeople need services, more per head in large cities. Buildings, roads and wonders need upkeep.
- **Each year a realm sets its taxes** to cover its costs and keep about a year of them in its treasury. Taxes above the customary rate make people unhappy (stability), taxes below it content. Treasuries stop growing without end.
- **A realm that cannot pay falls into arrears.** Its buildings and roads wear, and unrest grows, first in the regions farthest from the capital. The chronicle tells when a realm raises heavy taxes, eases them, and falls into or out of arrears, with the costs behind it.
- **Decisions weigh what the realm can afford.** Building weighs the surplus the realm raises at its customary rate after costs. Expansion weighs the administration new land would cost.

**Relevant spec sections:** VISION.md "Wealth" (the design agreed at the M3b review, 6 October 2026), "Stability", milestone M3c and its health checks.

## Acceptance (this slice)

- **Exact accounting** (invariant, every month): treasury before + produced (taxes and sites) + received − construction − administration − services − upkeep (buildings, wonders and roads) − given − lost = treasury after. Every cost paid or unpaid is accounted for.
- **Each settlement's earnings and costs sum to its realm's** (test): the realm's revenue and costs are the sums over its settlements and regions plus its roads.
- **Health checks** in the five-seed study:
  - treasuries level off at a few years of costs in most civilizations instead of growing without end;
  - some settlements and some regions run at a loss;
  - administration per person of the largest, oldest realms is clearly higher than that of small young ones;
  - realms in arrears exist but are not the rule.
- **Gates:** M1–M3, M3b and M3c.1 gates hold, and the complete `npm run check` passes.

## Decisions and defaults (rule 6)

- **Output and taxes** (`economy.ts`; `BUDGET_TUNING`):
  - **Output a year, before tax:**
    - each townsperson's trades produce 5, times the settlement's markets and the like;
    - each farmer's surplus is 0.2 × the share of food farmed or herded × surplus, where surplus is clamp((food security − 0.8) ÷ 0.2, 0, 1): hungry farmers have nothing to sell;
    - both are lower in unrest, as all output is.
  - **The crown's share:** a realm takes its tax rate of that output. The customary rate is 20%, which keeps a townsperson's yield at M3b's 1 a year. Mines and quarries pay their yield to the crown directly, untaxed.
  - **A wonder's wealth** (the Colossus) multiplies trades only, not farm taxes.
- **Costs a year** (`budget.ts`):
  - **Administration of each region:** (100 + 0.01 × its people) × (1 + ½ × its remoteness, at most 2) × max(1, regions ÷ 20)^½ × (1 + (1 − e^(−years since settling ÷ 600))).
    - Remoteness is travel-km from the capital ÷ governance reach.
    - The size factor makes the burden grow faster than the realm.
    - The age factor (offices, privileges and courts) approaches double over a few centuries; reforms (M7) will cut it back.
  - **Services of each settlement:** townspeople × 0.3 × (1 + townspeople ÷ 10,000)^½. They cost more per head in large cities.
  - **Upkeep** of buildings, wonders and roads, as in M3b.
  - **Remoteness is measured for the owner** (`SimulationState.remoteness`, `remoteOwner`):
    - at the owner's yearly assessment;
    - in the month a region joins a realm (the construction system re-measures a realm with land not measured for it).
    - An invariant checks that every held region is measured for its owner.
- **Paying** (`construction.ts` `payCosts`):
  - all costs are due monthly, a twelfth at a time with the fraction carried, and paid while the treasury allows;
  - what is paid is split into the administration, services and upkeep flows in whole units;
  - the share left unpaid wears buildings and roads (as M3b's unpaid upkeep did);
  - it feeds the arrears: the share unpaid, averaged over 12 months.
- **Taxes, set once a year** (the stability system's assessment; `decisions/budget.ts`, from the perception budget view):
  - **Target:** the rate that covers the realm's costs, less its sites, and refills its treasury toward a reserve of 0.5 + 3 × Tradition years of costs over five years.
  - **Limits:** between 5% and 50%, moving at most 2 points a year.
  - **Events:** a realm raising its rate above 30% records heavy taxes, citing its costs; easing back to the customary rate is an event too.
- **Stability:**
  - **Taxes:** above the customary rate they lower every region's stability, by up to 0.3 at the maximum. Below it they raise it by up to 0.05.
  - **Arrears** lower each region's stability by 0.5 × arrears × (0.2 + 0.8 × min(1, remoteness)): the far regions first.
  - Both are cited as causes of unrest.
  - **Arrears events:** a realm whose arrears pass 10% falls into arrears, an event citing its costs, until they fall below 5%.
- **Decisions:**
  - **Build** (buildings, wonders, roads) weighs cost against (surplus + treasury ÷ 5). It weighs added upkeep against max(surplus, 5% of revenue). Revenue is what customary taxes and the realm's sites raise; surplus is revenue minus costs. A realm whose costs outrun customary taxes builds little.
  - **Expansion** weighs administration: 0.5 × strain × (½ + ½ × min(1, the land's remoteness)), where strain is clamp(costs ÷ revenue − 1, 0, 1). A strained realm is loath to take on far land; one with a surplus is not held back.
- **The inspector** shows:
  - the realm's tax rate, revenue (trades, farms, mines and quarries), costs (administration, services, upkeep, roads), surplus or deficit, and arrears;
  - what each settlement pays and costs, with a profit or a loss; a region's seat (its main settlement) also takes in the region's farm taxes and mines and quarries and pays its administration;
  - the stability line names taxes and arrears.
- **Contract:** protocol 18 (events `taxes` and `arrears`; the budget fields). **Rules:** 16.
- **Calibration** (a Chronicle run to 2,000 before the five-seed study):
  - **Wonders:** none were built. Their whole cost (1.5–5 million) was weighed against one year's surplus, which only M3b's hoards had covered. A wonder now weighs its yearly instalment (cost × 12 ÷ months) instead.
  - **No settlement ran at a loss:** the region's farm taxes and administration stood outside every settlement's account. VISION.md's design gives a settlement "a tax on the surplus food of the farmers around it", so each region's main settlement is now its seat and keeps the region's account.
  - **Every treasury sat at the same 0.9 years:** the reserve now follows Tradition, from half a year to three and a half years of costs.

## Review (independent agent, 6 October 2026)

- **No accounting, NaN, determinism, hash or architecture bug.** The focused tests pass.
- **Findings, fixed in M3c.3:**
  1. **Medium:** `unite` re-assesses the larger realm through `assess`, which now also sets taxes. A union therefore takes an extra tax step (and may announce heavy taxes) outside the yearly cadence. The fix splits the yearly assessment from the stability pass a union needs.
  2. **Low:** rates stepping by 0.02 drift in floating point (0.30000000000000004), so the heavy-tax event fires at exactly 30% and easing waits until 18%. The fix stores rates rounded.
  3. **Low:** the study measures total costs per person (VISION.md's words). An administration-per-person statistic will show whether size and age, not services, drive the difference.
  4. **Low:** the remoteness invariant holds only because no system after construction changes owners yet. War and fracture (M6–M7) must re-measure the regions they move. Remoteness can also lag by up to a year after a capital moves.
  5. **Low:** there are no negative invariant tests for the tax bounds, the remoteness owner or the new flows. Regions without a living settlement cannot occur (an invariant), so their branch in the accounts is dead code.
  6. **Design, for the M3c review:** taxes always rise to cover costs up to 50%, so arrears come only at the cap. VISION.md says a realm "must choose between the unrest of heavy taxes and the decay of arrears". M3c.3 makes how far a realm will raise taxes depend on how calm its people are, so restless realms accept arrears instead.

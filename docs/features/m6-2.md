# M6.2 — War and peace

**Feature / status:** the second slice of M6, "War and peace" (VISION.md; the plan is in the [M6.1 brief](m6-1.md)). Status: verified, awaiting user review (VISION.md rule 2).

**Why:** VISION.md's "Diplomacy and war": "Wars are abstract contests of strength and endurance, resolved monthly at per-year rates and summarized yearly. Most end in negotiated peace when one or both sides run out of will, not in annihilation." M6.1 gave every civilization an army paid from its treasury; this slice lets realms go to war with it, for reasons the decision model can name, and make peace on terms the war earned. It is also M6's main mechanism for VISION.md's border gate ("Borders settle onto barriers … by year 3,000"): conquest crosses easy land and stops at rivers and mountains.

**Observable outcome:**
- **Declaring war** is a decision a civilization weighs at its decision step against a neighbour (VISION.md "Decision step": Declare war), named by its dominant factor (VISION.md's war framing):
  - a war of conquest (Expansionism × a weaker or richer neighbour);
  - a war for farmland (hunger or crowded land, and good land over the border);
  - a war for a resource it lacks that their land holds ("the Tin War");
  - a holy war (Zeal × a rival faith);
  - a war of reclamation (claims on land it lost);
  - a pre-emptive war (a stronger neighbour it fears);
  - a war for a natural frontier (land that would carry its border onto a river or mountains, or shorten it across open country).
  It fights for land next to its own (and a region beyond), weighs whether it can win (the strength it believes on each side, with allies, and the ground it must take) and what war would cost it (arrears, liking, war-weariness, breaking a pact, reach).
- **Each month of war** (the war system):
  - each side's strength meets the other's on the front: its soldiers × military multiplier × supply (falling with distance from its capital; roads shorten it); the ground of the side being pushed back helps it (defensibility, walls, a river or mountains on the border);
  - the war score moves toward the stronger side, with chance;
  - both sides lose soldiers, more the weaker: war deaths in the population ledger;
  - a side ahead occupies the regions it fights for, one by one (and gives them back when the score turns); occupied land pays its owner nothing and is restless;
  - each side tires (exhaustion): from its dead, the war's length, its occupied land and its arrears; less for a militaristic people.
- **Peace** comes when a side is exhausted, when the attacker has held its goal for a year, or when a side seeks peace at its decision step and the other accepts. Terms follow the war score (VISION.md's table): white peace, tribute for some years, cession of the occupied land, or annexation of a small loser.
- **After a war:** a truce between them; both remember the war; the loser claims what it lost (grievance, fading over generations), which can bring wars of reclamation; war-weariness holds both back from the next war for a generation; armies stand down (M6.1's demobilization, which unsettles).
- **The game:**
  - **Diplomacy:** "at war" is a standing (the lens and the rows), and each row shows claims in either direction.
  - **The map:** a wars layer: the land fought over striped red, land an enemy holds striped in the occupier's colour.
  - **The realm panel:** a Wars tab: its wars now and of the last century, what each is fought for and why, how it stands (the lead, both sides' exhaustion), the dead and the land held, and how it ended; the Region tab says who holds the region in war.
  - **Stories:** wars are stories ("the Tin War: the Vraye against the Kesh…").
  - **The ledger:** a table of the wars of the last century (sides, years, how each ended, its dead, land that changed hands).
  - **The chronicle:** war declared (and a pact broken), the year's fighting (when notable), regions conquered, peace signed.

## Acceptance

- **Tests** (`tests/simulation-war.test.ts`):
  - **Declaring:** on a neighbour, for land beginning at the border; never on an ally, during a war or a truce; a pact broken (an event, remembered by the victim and the breaker's treaty partners); treaties end; both remember.
  - **The option:** its motives are its factors, the dominant one frames it; the odds, Militarism, liking, a pact, reach and a natural frontier move it as they should.
  - **The ground and the goal:** the goal is chosen region by region by `goalWorth` (its first region worth most, each next to its land or the goal so far), `frontierGain` as defined; harder ground lowers the believed odds; the counter is the defender's choice; the side pushed back is helped by its ground.
  - **A month of war:** the score follows the stronger side; deaths are exact war deaths (ledger and invariants); occupation follows the score; exhaustion rises; a year of war in the whole simulation keeps every invariant.
  - **Several wars:** two wars on one realm never hold the same region; a realm annexed in one war ends its other wars that month (both were invariant failures in a first calibration run).
  - **Peace:** white, tribute, cession (land, people and claims move) and annexation of a small loser; tribute paid month by month; seeking and accepting peace.
  - **The frame:** wars, occupation, claims and the standing at war; a state copied mid-war continues exactly (save-ready).
  - **The game** (browser): the Wars tab, the realm's standing at war, the wars layer and its switch, the ledger's table of wars, on a war fought in the lab's history.
- **Study (five seeds, 3,000 years):**
  - wars per century, and how they ended: VISION.md's story health asks 2–20 a century, at least 60% white or negotiated; M6 asks more than half not annexation;

  - the border ratio against all land at 3,000 (M6's close-out gate: ≥ 1.5 in every seed), reported;

## Decisions and defaults

- **State:** `state.wars` holds every war (VISION.md "Data model": belligerents, goal, war score, exhaustion per side, start year, contested regions), never deleted: its name, sides, framing and resource, goal (the defender's regions the attacker fights for) and counter (the attacker's regions the defender may take), the regions occupied (by whom, since when), the score (−100 to 100, the attacker's favour), each side's exhaustion and dead, its start, end, outcome, the regions ceded and the tribute. Each civilization lists its wars, its claims (a region and when it was lost) and its war-weariness; `state.occupiedBy` holds each region's occupier (−1 none).
- **Declaring war** (`perception.ts` `warTargets`, `decisions/choose.ts` `warScore`): on each civilization it has met whose land touches its own, not its ally and outside a truce, while it fights no war itself. A pact partner can be attacked: the pact is broken (an event), and the victim and every people bound to the breaker by a treaty remember it.
  - **The goal** (`claims.ts` `chooseGoal`): up to three of their regions, chosen one at a time, each next to its land or to one already chosen (their capital only when they hold no more), the one worth most at each step (`goalWorth`): exp(frontierWorth × the frontier it would gain) × land value ÷ (land value + the mean of its own) × (1 + its claim).
    - **The frontier gained** (`frontierGain`): each border edge taking the region would draw (to their other land) counts its travel cost over the world's median edge, less 1.25 (a crossing a quarter harder than the median is a barrier); each border edge it would close (to its own land or the goal so far) counts the same, negated; borders with third peoples stay borders and do not count. Above 0 the border would lie on barriers or run shorter across open land. The goal's worth is × exp(1 × that gain).
    - **Why so** (calibration, below): goals ranked by land value moved borders without ever settling them, since land value varies a hundredfold between regions; a goal by position first, a region beyond the border allowed, carries borders to the river or the mountains.
  - **The odds** it believes: its strength with its allies' (½ each) × its supply to the goal against theirs with the allies it knows × the ground of the goal (`defenseOf`: defensibility, the walls it sees, and how hard the way in is) × their supply (from their capital as the crow flies × 1.4); clamped from 0.35 to 0.7. The goal is chosen from the owners it knows (its map).
  - **Motives**, each 0–1 and named as its factors: conquest (a weaker or richer neighbour, the needs' opportunity rule, × Expansionism; 0.5), farmland (hunger or land pressure × land better than its own; 0.4), resource (the largest shortfall the goal's known sites would relieve: "the Tin War"; 0.5), holy (a rival faith × Zeal, cooled by the secular age; 0.3), reclamation (its claims on the goal; 0.5), pre-emptive (the threat it feels from them; 0.3), frontier (the goal's frontier gain ÷ 2; 0.2). Their sum × 0.4 × (¼ + Militarism) × the odds.
  - **Risks:** its arrears; its liking for them; its war-weariness (its exhaustion when its last war ended, halving every 25 years); breaking a pact (`treachery`); a goal beyond its governance reach; its wars on them that came to nothing (`futilityOf`: each white peace or lost war it began on them, halving every 60 years).
  - **Framing:** the dominant motive frames and names the war ("the War of …", "the War for …", "the Tin War", "the Holy War on the …", "the War to Retake …", "the … War on the …", "the … Frontier War"); a war fought again under the same name is the Second, the Third…
  - **No treaties with an enemy at war:** no pact, alliance, trade or joint road is offered to a people it fights.
- **A month of war** (`war.ts` `wageWars`, in the war system before the levy):
  - **Strength on the front:** soldiers × military multiplier × supply, 1 ÷ (1 + travel-km from its capital to the front ÷ 1,500): roads shorten the way. The front lies in the land of the side being pushed back (the defender's goal while the score favours the attacker, the attacker's counter when it turns), and that side's strength × (1 + ½ × defensibility + its walls' defence) × (1 + the crossing into it: its easiest border edge's travel cost over the world's median, less 1, within 0–3).
  - **The score** moves by 4 × (2 × odds − 1) points a month, plus chance (±2).
  - **The dead:** each side loses 12% a year of its engaged soldiers at even odds, × 2 × the enemy's share of the strength: exact war deaths, from its regions by their soldiers (`ledger.warDeaths`, in the population invariant).
  - **Occupation:** a side ahead holds one region it fights for per 25 points, in order, and gives them back as the score turns; one war holds a region at a time. Occupied land pays its owner nothing and is restless (a stability burden, 0.3).
  - **Exhaustion** a year: 0.08 for the war's length, 40 × its dead ÷ its people, 0.6 × the share of its regions occupied, 0.3 × its arrears; × (1 − ½ × Militarism).
  - **The year's fighting** is summarized on the war's anniversary when 200 or more died or the land held changed.
  - **Several wars at once:** a realm attacked by two neighbours fights both; a side annexed or gone ends its other wars the same month (and peace sought with it ends the war as such), and land that changed hands is no longer held.
- **Peace** (`makePeace`): a side at exhaustion 1, or the attacker holding its whole goal for 12 months, ends the war; a side that seeks peace at its decision step (`peaceScore`: its exhaustion, losing, terms to be had, less Militarism) is accepted when the enemy is tired (0.4) or the terms favour it or are white. Terms by the winner's lead (VISION.md's table):
  - below 15: a white peace;
  - below 40: tribute, 10% of the loser's revenue a year for 10 years;
  - below 75: cession of the land it holds of theirs (never their capital) with tribute (tribute alone if it holds none);
  - from 75: annexation when the loser holds at most four regions or a quarter of the winner's people (its people, land, treasury and knowledge pass, as a union does); else cession with tribute;
  - a small realm whose capital (its whole goal) has been held a year is annexed.
- **After a war:** a truce of 25 years between them; both remember the war (opinion −40, halving every 30 years); a loser of land remembers it (−30, halving every 75 years) and claims it (its weight halving every 75 years, forgotten below 0.05: grievance, and wars of reclamation); its exhaustion lingers as war-weariness. Trade and joint roads between them end when war begins.
- **Armies at war** (M6.1's target): each war adds 2% of its people to the soldiers it wants and its army budget × 1.5; an enemy at war threatens fully. Both sides set their armies at once when war begins and when it ends (standing down unsettles).
- **Tunables:** `WAR_TUNING` (validated).

## Calibration

With the fork tool (`npm run fork`, five large seeds from year 500 to 3,000, `.chronicle/forks/m6-2-cal1` … `cal5`):
1. **First design** (goals by land value, the odds without the ground): wars came in waves and then stopped. By 2,400 nearly every pair of neighbours had a non-aggression pact (M5.5), and with breaking one weighing 0.08 against motives of 0.01–0.05, almost no realm wanted war (0–1 of 22–32 at any time).
2. **More war:** the war weight raised and a broken pact weighing less brought 17–35 wars a century, 94–98% not ending in annexation and 2–3.6% of owned regions changing hands a century; but borders did not settle: the border ratio against all land stayed 0.94–1.27 at 3,000 (M6.1, without war: 1.08–1.42). A seed's history tracked edge by edge showed why: borders were as easy as land in general (median 0.94 against 0.93), 329 of 759 border edges were over 1,000 years old, and the edges wars drew were as easy as those they closed — goals were simply the richest border regions, land value varying a hundredfold.
3. **Goals by position:** ranking by how a region would move the border (then by land) and judging the odds on the ground raised the ratio to 1.13–1.36; a frontier motive added wars without settling more borders.
4. **The frontier gained, edge by edge, a region beyond allowed:** counting each border edge drawn or closed (so shortening a border across open land counts too) and choosing the goal region by region reached 1.36–1.52 at 3,000 (the share of border edges on barriers rose from 22% at 1,000 to 41%); later wars still draw easier edges than earlier ones, and unions and annexations take away hard borders (between small mountain realms and their neighbours).
5. **A barrier from 1.25 median edges** (rather than 1): 1.38–1.54 at 3,000; a larger goal (four regions) and a higher war weight changed little either way, within the spread between runs (±0.05–0.1).
6. **After the review's fixes** (the odds with supply, the memory of fruitless wars, a lighter frontier motive): 6–17 wars a century, 86–96% not ending in annexation, 1.4–2.2% of owned regions changing hands a century with unions; the border ratio at 3,000: Chronicle 1.37, Elsewhere 1.33, Atlas 1.62, Verdant 1.45, Aster 1.44 (`.chronicle/forks/m6-2-cal7`). Most of the borders' rise comes by year 2,000; by then 40% of border edges lie on barriers (1.5 median edges or more), against 22% at 1,000.
- **The border gate is M6's** (its close-out): M6.2 brings the ratio from 1.08–1.42 (M6.1) to 1.33–1.62, short of 1.5 in four seeds. The remaining easy borders run across open country where no barrier lies within a region or two of them; later slices (the ravages, coalitions) and the close-out return to it.

## Review

The independent review (an agent) ran five histories with every invariant checked each month from year 300–500 (all held), copied a state mid-war (it continued exactly) and read the code against VISION.md and this brief. Its findings and what was done:
1. **The same war fought over and over (material).** 80–91% of wars repeated a pair, the same attacker each time, 26 years apart (the truce plus two); one pair fought 29 times, fifteen white peaces in a row. Causes: the odds an attacker believed left out supply (its soldiers far from the front), so wars it could not win looked winnable; war-weariness was too light to hold anyone back (peace came at an exhaustion of about 0.25); nothing remembered that earlier wars on them had failed. The frontier motive framed 74–86% of wars, and the names repeated. Fixed: the believed odds count each side's supply to the front (its own way there; theirs as the crow flies from their capital, × winding); a realm remembers its wars on a people that came to nothing (white peace or lost), weighing against the next (`futilityOf`, halving every 60 years); the frontier motive is lighter (0.2), the goal still chosen for its frontier; a war fought again under the same name is the Second, the Third…
2. **Treaties signed with an enemy at war (material):** 41–74 a seed, mostly pacts signed within two years of the declaration (the next war then "broke" them again). Fixed: no pact, alliance, trade or joint road is offered to an enemy while they fight.
3. **Peace with a side already gone this month corrupted the state (material, rare):** a realm annexed at an earlier decision step could be annexed again, or land ceded to it. Fixed: peace with a side gone ends the war as such (and a decision step no longer sees such a war); a test reproduces it.
4. **Stories (material):** a rise credited land won in war to "its own growth", and a decline cited no war. Fixed: a rise counts what it won in war (regions and people, a cause of its own), a decline names the land it lost in war.
5. **Minor, fixed:** a small realm whose capital (its whole goal) was held a year is annexed, not let off with tribute; a war ending with a side gone has its event and the survivor stands down at once; supply is measured to the front (the land being fought over), not to the enemy's land; the frame carries the counter (the attacker's land the defender may take) and the land still fought over, so the wars layer stripes the front; the state hash covers each war's goal, counter, start, this year's dead, occupation and tribute parties; the goal is chosen from the owners the attacker knows and the walls it sees (VISION.md: a people acts on what it knows); claims weighing almost nothing are forgotten; the exhaustion cap and the crossing cap are tunables; "ends after 0 years" reads in months; the war story says what is held only when something is; a Biome rule (with its negative test) keeps perception from importing the war module; stale comments.
6. **Minor, kept:** the yearly summary of the fighting notices land changing hands only in its anniversary month; a realm fighting two wars uses its full strength in each (about 1% of war-months).
